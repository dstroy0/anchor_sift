\chapter*{Abstract}

The corpus everything in this work is measured against is Salishan speech, written down. This book
says whose words it holds, how wrong it could be, and what was done to find out.

Every table opens with the person who spoke, before the linguist who published and before anyone who
read the paper into a file. Twenty-five tables carry 13,510 rows. Sixteen name their speakers. Nine
cite a published source and name no speaker, and those say so instead of guessing. A linguist wrote
the paper and a person read the paper into a table, and neither of those is whose language it is.

How wrong the corpus could be is stated as a bound, because the checks that matter report
zero and zero failures in a sample is not a rate of zero. Three channels fail for different reasons:
a form written that the paper does not hold, a token the paper prints that no row holds, and a token
the corpus lost on the way out of a reader. Taken as independent the joint bound is
$4.47 \times 10^{-10}$ per line. Section 4 of the derivation says where that independence is thin,
and the largest unmodelled term is that one person read every table.

The instrument found a border it was never shown. Mellesmoen and Kye label every form they cite as
northern or southern Lushootseed, so the boundary sits on disk as a fact a linguist published. Given
the forms and never the labels, holding the concept fixed through the word web and flattening the
pooled run counts to maximum entropy, the term that carries the border comes back as the stressed
schwa, southern, at 45 against 6 over the same concepts, beaten by 1 of 200 random borders.

Two papers here are set in a font with no Unicode map, so their extracted text is the font's encoding
and not the page. Their drafts are rebuilt rule by rule and remain drafts: a rule that guesses at
labialization is named as guessing, and a fifth repair rule was specified, measured to change nothing,
and withdrawn rather than added. Five more papers hold none of their own orthography and are counted
as zero rather than as disagreement.

These tools read a language and can put one back, and that is stated plainly here rather than
softened. Near the center of a subject a regeneration is a copy, and far from it the output is
obvious nonsense. Between them is a narrow band where the output is coherent and may already not be
the language, and nothing here marks which side of it a result fell on. That question belongs to a
native speaker, and no amount of measurement turns it into one an algorithm can answer.
