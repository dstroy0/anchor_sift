\chapter{The recordings under the instrument}

\section{What the prosody field holds}

\textbf{Purpose:} Ask the question the ledger records as unasked, whether the prosody residual
identifies the speaker, and report what the sound representation turned out to hold instead.
\textbf{Scope:}
\texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}python/\allowbreak{}representation/\allowbreak{}sound/\allowbreak{}perceived\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py},
\texttt{maint/\allowbreak{}analysis/\allowbreak{}sound/\allowbreak{}binary\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py},
\texttt{build/\allowbreak{}experiments/\allowbreak{}speaker\_\allowbreak{}residual.\allowbreak{}py},
\texttt{build/\allowbreak{}experiments/\allowbreak{}rotation\_\allowbreak{}cost.\allowbreak{}py}.

\textbf{Bev Phillips}, of Lytton First Nation, spoke all three of these recordings, in
nɬeʔkepmxcín, in the Lytton (ƛ̓q̓əmcín) dialect. \emph{When Old One Created the Earth} is an
original spíləx̣m of hers, written and told by her, and she is an author of that paper and of
\emph{You Will Be Sorry} with Brent Hall. She chose the third story and helped translate it, and
Katherine Givens and Brent Hall transcribed and glossed it. The language is hers and so are two of
the three papers.

\subsection{The question}

The ledger states it and leaves it open. Its wording is that pitch against the speaker's own median
is a per-speaker constant subtracted, which is the center of mass again, so what remains in that
field is the residual, and whether that residual identifies the speaker has not been asked.

The field is already built.
\texttt{perceived\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py} keeps prosody apart from the segment code
deliberately, because a field made shift invariant would put a stressed form and an unstressed one
at one address. It holds four things per 10 ms frame: loudness, pitch against the speaker's own
median, how that pitch is moving, and voicing. Each is thresholded at its own median over the
recording, so each bit splits the frames in half by construction and what a distance between two
recordings reads is the joint arrangement of the four and never their levels.

\subsection{What was made}

\texttt{binary\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py} wrote the representation of all three recordings.

needs tens of minutes. Two of these are 13.9 and 22.5 minutes.

\subsection{The answer, through the necessary condition}

Only one speaker is held, so there is no discrimination question to ask. What is askable is the
condition underneath it: a residual that identifies a speaker has to hold still within one. Three
recordings of Bev Phillips should then sit near the floor of the estimator.

That is Proposition 1's own shape, and it is why the thin sample is enough. A necessary condition
cannot confirm identification, and its failure settles the matter.

Every recording is trimmed to 23489 frames, the shortest, because a null taken at one length bounds
nothing about another. The floor is the recording split by alternating frames and measured against
itself, which samples it instead of cutting it in place. A midpoint cut would put the first half of
a narrative against the second and report the distance between two passages, which is the defect the
corpus chapter records inflating every anchor floor by 1.35 to 3.64 times.

\textbf{The residual does not hold still within one speaker, so it does not identify her.} One
person, one language, one dialect, and her three recordings sit nine to nineteen times the
resolution apart on the field built to hold what is left after her own pitch range is divided out.

\subsection{What that does not settle}

Three narratives in three ICSNL volumes vary together. The story, the session, the microphone and
the encode all move with the recording and nothing here separates them. What is measured is that the
field does not hold still across them, and not which of those moved it.

The failure is also one directional in the useful way. Had the three sat at the floor, that would
have been consistent with the residual identifying her and consistent with the field holding nothing
at all, and a second speaker would have been needed to tell those apart. They do not sit at the
floor, and no second speaker is needed to read that.

\section{The segment field is not comparable between recordings}

\subsection{What the code does}

\texttt{represented} rotates the segment measurement before thresholding it.
\texttt{decorrelated} takes the right singular vectors of the centered measurement, and the
measurement it is handed is one recording. So segment bit 3 of one recording and segment bit 3 of
another are different axes, and a distance between two recordings' segment profiles is partly a
distance between two rotations.

The module explains why it rotates, which is that neighboring bands rise and fall together and
thresholding them as they come gives bits that restate each other. It does not say the basis belongs
to the recording, and nothing downstream of it compares two recordings, so the question had not come
up.

\subsection{A check that was not a check}

The first attempt at measuring this compared two halves of one recording, which share a rotation,
against two whole recordings, which do not. It gives ratios of 8.8 to 1.6 across the widths and
looks like a confirmation.

It is not one. The prosody field is never rotated and gives 18.6 by the same measure. Two recordings
differ for every other reason as well, and a check both arms pass is measuring something both arms
have. It is recorded here because it read as a result while it was being written.

\subsection{What isolates it}

One recording, cut in half once, and the two halves compared twice. In the first arm
\texttt{represented} runs on the whole recording and the frames are cut afterwards, so one rotation
computed over both halves is used for both. In the second, each half of the samples goes through
\texttt{represented} alone and carries a rotation computed over itself. The audio is identical and
every other step is identical, so the gap is the rotation and nothing else.

The measured distances between recordings' segment fields at six bits are 0.2309, 0.2346 and 0.2819,
which is the same size as the rotation cost on its own. \textbf{A segment distance taken between two
recordings is reading their bases.} The field is not comparable across recordings without a shared
rotation, and every segment row in the previous section is withdrawn as a reading of speech.

Nothing else in this tree compares two recordings' segment fields, so no published number moves. What
changes is that the next thing to do so has to fit one rotation over both first.

\subsection{Why the prosody field escapes it}

It is not rotated, and the module says why: its four columns are each a named thing, and rotating
them would mix pitch back into the field the segment code was made shift invariant to get away from.
Loudness, pitch against the speaker's own median, the movement of that pitch and voicing mean the
same thing in every recording.

So the one field that could carry the ledger's question is the one that is comparable, and the
answer it gives is negative.

\subsection{A correction, recorded because the tree exists to prevent it}

This was first written and run as two speakers against one, on the reading that ICSNL58's filename
names Givens and Hall where the other two name Bev Phillips. That was a guess off a filename, and
the paper says who spoke: the story was recounted in Nɬeʔkepmxcín by Bev Phillips, who also provided
much of the translation, and Givens and Hall are the linguists who transcribed and glossed it.

Guessing a speaker off a filename is the exact thing the rest of this tree refuses to do.
\texttt{sift\_\allowbreak{}extract.\allowbreak{}py} states the reason in its own header: a paper names
its speaker in prose nobody has read, and putting a name on somebody's words on no evidence is what
that whole directory exists to avoid. The rule was written down, and it was not followed here until
the paper was opened.

The correction improved the result. Two speakers with one pair each would have given a comparison
with a one in three ordering and no strength in it. One speaker across three recordings gives a
necessary condition, and a necessary condition that fails settles the question.

\section{The waveform itself, with nothing thrown away}

\textbf{Scope:} \texttt{build/\allowbreak{}experiments/\allowbreak{}lossless\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py},
\texttt{build/\allowbreak{}sound/\allowbreak{}lossless/\allowbreak{}}.

\subsection{Why the field that already exists could not answer this}

\texttt{perceived\_\allowbreak{}sound} reduces a recording to 28 bits every 10 ms and discards the
rest. It takes an envelope, smooths the harmonic comb off it, keeps 24 coefficients, rotates them
onto that recording's own singular vectors, and cuts every column at its median. Each of those is a
bound, and the ledger's own entry on bounds is that every one put on a quantity in this work has had
to be taken back off, ten times in one sitting, because the number then describes the choice.

The decoded waveform has no such choice in it. All three recordings are written out here at full
integer width, in order, 215 MB of samples, and every reading below is taken off that. The bytes were
checked against the corpus's signed audit first, on the standing warning that a measurement moving
for no reason should be blamed on the bytes before the method.

\subsection{The first reading was wrong, and the samples say why}

Reading the top \(w\) bits of each sample returned \(H_2(2)\) equal to \(H_2(1)\) and \(H_2(16)\) at
5.04 bits, far under what a speech waveform should carry.

The recordings say why. \emph{When Old One Created the Earth} peaks at 16954 of 32768 and its median
absolute sample is \textbf{6}. Half of a twenty-two minute narrative sits inside thirteen levels of a
sixty-five thousand level converter, because a told story is mostly the quiet between words. The top
bits are sign extension: at two bits they take two values with the commonest holding 0.549 of the
samples.

A linear slice of a waveform spends its levels where the signal is not. That is the same defect the
ledger records four separate ways, and the correction is the one \texttt{envelope.\allowbreak{}py}
already states.

Two seatings are therefore reported. Linear over the range each recording uses, whose \(H_2\) curve
is a fact about the amplitude distribution. And quantile, which puts an equal share of samples in
every symbol, so \(H_2\) comes out at \(w\) by construction. The second is the point: the marginal
has been flattened to maximum entropy and every state is reachable, so what is left to read
afterward is arrangement and not loudness. It is the same subtraction radix makes on two dialects.

\subsection{Where the arrangement sits}

The block shuffle keeps everything shorter than its span and destroys everything longer, so sweeping
the span locates the structure instead of only detecting it. The ledger builds this null on text and
records it as never run on a waveform, and as unreachable with the earlier clips because measuring
the arrangement of units needs recordings of tens of minutes. These are 3.9, 13.9 and 22.5.

Read on the quantile seating at eight bits, live against its own shuffle:

\subsection{There is no unit scale in it, and that is the result}

The obvious reading of that table is a knee somewhere near the word, and the text of one of these
recordings is on disk to check it against: 1736 words over 1350 seconds is 0.778 seconds a word,
arrived at with no reference to the audio.

Swept finely between a phone and six seconds, there is no knee. The excess over the shuffle decays
smoothly and fits a power law in the span:

\[
\text{excess} \;\propto\; \text{span}^{-0.752}, \qquad r^2 = 0.988
\]

over a 64-fold range of spans, with every one of eleven points landing between 0.88 and 1.19 of the
fit. Nothing happens at 0.778 seconds. Nothing happens anywhere.

\textbf{So the waveform's arrangement has no characteristic scale.} That is a finding and not a
failure to find one, and it is the case the ledger already names: data carrying structure at every
scale has no characteristic scale for a window, a band or a ceiling to sit at, which is why every
such bound in this work had to be removed. What holds still for such data is how the quantity changes
with scale, and here that is an exponent measured to \(r^2 = 0.988\).

\subsection{What that settles about reading speech back}

\textbf{Detecting that a recording is speech is available and is not the interesting half.} The
departure from the null runs 363 to 684 at sample scale and is still 1.07 at six seconds. Nothing
this large needs a careful instrument. Most of it is the amplitude envelope, which is to say the
silence between words, and a measure reading that is reading that a person paused.

\textbf{Transcription is not available from this reading, and the reason is structural.} Writing down
what was said requires knowing where one unit ends and the next begins. A boundary is what
Section 4.12 of the method finds in text, by a symbol whose gaps are far tighter than geometric, and
the whole of that construction rests on there being a scale where the regularity sits. This waveform
has no such scale. A power law with no knee supplies no unit boundary at any span, so there is
nothing to segment on and nothing to write down.

That is the same blocker \texttt{examples/\allowbreak{}sound/\allowbreak{}README.\allowbreak{}md}
already records from the other side, that what is needed is a null keeping each call intact while
permuting the order of calls, and nobody has written it. This measurement says why nobody has: fixing
the unit is what that null requires, and the unit is the thing being asked for. The block shuffle is
the closest available substitute and it returns no unit.

\textbf{Dialect cannot be asked at all.} The audio held here is one speaker, one language, one
dialect: Bev Phillips, nɬeʔkepmxcín, Lytton. There is no second dialect to discern from the first, and
a summed reference over one dialect is that dialect. The dialect question has an answer in this work
and it is on the text side, where Mellesmoen and Kye's published labels supplied a border the
algorithm never saw. Asking it of sound needs recordings carrying a border somebody else labeled, and
the corpus holds none.

\section{The join between the web and the sound}

\textbf{Scope:} \texttt{build/\allowbreak{}experiments/\allowbreak{}align\_\allowbreak{}oracle.\allowbreak{}py},
\texttt{build/\allowbreak{}experiments/\allowbreak{}pooled\_\allowbreak{}sound.\allowbreak{}py}.

\subsection{The rung that was missing}

The refs chapter states the three stages and says which one is absent: the word web is built on written
forms, and nothing takes a character out of \texttt{TEXT\_\allowbreak{}SPACE} and hands back what it
stands for, so two dialects writing one word two ways are still two nodes.

The section above went looking for the unit in the waveform alone and found a power law with no knee.
That is the middle of the three ways a partition is fixed, an estimate, and the ledger says an
estimate reaches nothing past what the sample already carries. The third way adds information the
sample does not hold, and it is the only one that does.

\subsection{The supervision was printed on the page and copied into the table}

Hall and Phillips write that there is no standardized punctuation convention for nɬeʔkepmxcín, so
instead of periods they separate each sentence of the transcription with a timestamp into the
recording. The hand extraction copied those into the gloss column.

That is \textbf{126 sentences of Bev Phillips's own spíləx̣m, each carrying the interval of audio it
was spoken in and the exact letters she said}, and nothing in this tree produced any of it. It has
been sitting in the gloss column of a table that was checked against its paper in both directions.

Checked before it was used: the timestamps run forward, the last is 1336 s against a 1350 s
recording, and the 126 sentences cover the whole of it. A sentence runs 2 to 40 seconds, median 10.

\subsection{A segment is a grapheme cluster and not a character}

\texttt{ʷ} is not a segment, it is labialization on the consonant before it, and \texttt{̓} is
glottalization on the base it sits over. Pooling per character would measure two halves of one
segment as though they were independent.

Joining every mark and modifier to the base in front of it, which is the rule the oracles already
use, turns 5552 characters over 35 distinct into \textbf{4839 segments over 50 distinct}. Twenty of
the fifty carry a mark or a modifier: \texttt{c̓ kʷ k̓ k̓ʷ m̓ n̓ p̓ qʷ q̓ q̓ʷ w̓ xʷ x̣ x̣ʷ y̓ z̓ ƛ̓ ə́ ʕʷ ʕ̓ʷ}.
The inventory grows under clustering rather than shrinking, because \texttt{kʷ} and \texttt{k} stop
being one letter counted twice.

\texttt{ə́} is one of the fifty, at 45 instances. It is the same segment that carried the Lushootseed
border, and at the character level it was two codepoints, which is why the radix found it at width
two.

\subsection{Placing a segment inside its sentence, and why the crude answer is allowed}

A timestamp bounds a sentence and not a segment. The segments inside one are placed evenly, which is
wrong in detail, since a vowel is longer than a stop and nothing here knows that.

It is allowed because of what it is compared against. The null permutes which letter is attached to
which interval and changes nothing else, so every interval keeps its position, its length and its
audio, and the even spacing sits in both arms exactly. An error in the placement costs both arms the
same and cannot manufacture a result. It also bounds the finding: what reads as holding together is
holding together at the resolution even spacing allows.

\subsection{What the pooled sound gives}

Each interval becomes the loudness heard in 64 log spaced bands, out of the engine's own
\texttt{perceived\_\allowbreak{}sound}, normalized so a loud instance and a quiet one of one segment
are one shape. A segment type's sound is the mean over its instances, which is the sum. Thirty-four
of the fifty types carry at least 24 instances.

Two things have to be read off that together, and reading either alone gets it wrong.

\textbf{The alignment is real.} Nine of two hundred shuffles reach what the letters a person wrote
down give. The letters and the audio are not independent, and the only thing joining them is a
timestamp printed in a paper.

\textbf{The letters do not separate.} Between over within is 0.214, which is to say a segment's own
instances scatter about five times further than one segment's mean sits from another's. A
representation that recovered a letter from its sound would have that the other way up. This one does
not, at this resolution.

So the join exists and carries something, and it is nowhere near reading a letter off the sound. The
honest form of the result is that a rung which did not exist now exists, has a number on it, and the
number is small.

\subsection{What it needs next, which the measurement names}

Even placement is the binding limit and it is fixable without giving up the supervision. The letters
would still come from the oracle and only the boundaries would come from the audio, and the null
would still permute labels over whatever boundaries were found, so the control does not weaken. A
sentence of ten seconds holding thirty-eight segments currently gives each one 0.26 s where a stop is
nearer 0.05, so most intervals straddle a boundary or land in silence. That is where the scatter is.

What must not be done is to let the audio choose the letters. The timestamps and the transcription are
the whole of what makes this supervision rather than an estimate, and an alignment tuned until the
letters separate would be the algorithm scoring itself against a split it proposed, which the ledger
records as the failure that passes forever.
