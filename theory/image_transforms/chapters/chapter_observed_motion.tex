\chapter{Observed motion is two motions}

\section{The sum a sequence records}

Let an object sit at position $p(t)$ in some frame of reference fixed to the thing being watched,
and let the view map that frame to image coordinates by a transform $T_t$. The image records
$x(t) = T_t(p(t))$, and the displacement between two frames is
\[
x(t+1) - x(t) \;=\; \underbrace{T_{t+1}(p(t)) - T_t(p(t))}_{\text{the view moved}}
\;+\; \underbrace{T_{t+1}(p(t+1)) - T_{t+1}(p(t))}_{\text{the object moved}} .
\]
Nothing about an image separates the two terms. A tracker that links an object to whatever lies
nearest its last image position is linking along the sum, and where the first term is as large as
the second it is following the view.

Optical flow on a moving surface writes the same decomposition. Where the observed points lie on a
surface that itself moves, the total velocity of a point moving along a level line of constant
intensity is
\[
\hat{\mathbf{M}} \;=\; \hat{\mathbf{V}} + \hat{\mathbf{v}},
\]
where $\hat{\mathbf{V}}$ is the velocity of the surface and $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ is the tangential
velocity of the points relative to it. The flow is posed for $\hat{\mathbf{v}}$ alone, once the
surface velocity is fixed by a choice of parametrization.

\section{Plane plus parallax}

For a camera moving through a three dimensional scene the stratification is published and precise.
Register the images by the homography of one dominant plane. That single projective transform
removes every effect of camera rotation, zoom and calibration without computing any of them
explicitly. What remains has two sources only: parallax, which the camera's translation produces
at points off the plane, and objects that moved on their own. The residual parallax field is
epipolar, radial about the epipole. A residual that is not radial about that point is independent
motion.

Irani and Anandan used this to stratify moving object detection from two dimensional scenes up to
full three dimensional ones, and extended the recovery of planar parallax to many frames at once.
Triggs related the plane plus parallax representation to the tensors of multiple view geometry.

\section{The order transforms are removed in}

The lesson carried through this book is an order. Remove the transform of the whole view first,
because every object shares it. Then remove what a group of objects shares, a medium flowing or a
surface turning under them. What is left is each object's own motion, and only that is what a
tracker should link on. Each stage explains part of the residual of the stage before it, and each
stage that is skipped leaves its motion inside every object's apparent step.

Two consequences follow before any method is chosen. First, the view's motion is estimated from
everything in the image, not from the objects being tracked, because everything in the image shares
it. Second, a view that orbits its subject while the subject moves changes perspective as well as
position. A translation alone cannot be the whole first stage.
