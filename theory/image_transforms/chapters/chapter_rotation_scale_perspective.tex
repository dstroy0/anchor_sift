\chapter{Rotation, scale and perspective}

\section{Rotation and scale as translations}

The magnitude of a Fourier transform does not change when the image translates,  it is
called the Fourier-Mellin invariant. Resample that magnitude on a log-polar grid and the two
remaining similarity parameters become translations: a rotation of the image is a translation
along the angle axis, and a uniform scaling by a constant factor is a translation along the log
radius axis. Phase correlation in log-polar space recovers rotation and scale, the image is corrected
for both, and phase correlation in the original space then recovers the translation. Reddy and
Chatterji give the FFT based technique; later work adds pseudo-polar transforms and robustness
to noise. Log-polar resampling in the spatial domain also recovers large scale changes, at least
four-fold, and arbitrary rotations as a preprocessing step.

\section{Rotation in three dimensions}

A turning specimen in a volume is a rotation in the group \(SO(3)\), and the log-polar trick does not
carry over because \(SO(3)\) is not a product of circles. Two published routes do.

\textbf{Through projections.} The projection-slice theorem states that the Fourier transform of a
projection of a volume is a central slice of the volume's Fourier transform. Combined with phase
correlation it estimates rotation parameters from projections, including perspective projections
and in the presence of translational offsets, and it widens the capture range of iterative
registration substantially: in one clinical evaluation initialized registration succeeded in 68.6
percent of cases against 28.8 percent uninitialized.

\textbf{Through spherical harmonics.} Makadia and Daniilidis estimate rotation directly from
functions on the sphere, without correspondences. Spherical harmonic coefficients undergo a unitary
map when the function is rotated. The correlation of two spherical functions is a function on
\(SO(3)\), and its \(SO(3)\) Fourier transform is the pointwise product of the two functions' spherical
harmonic coefficients. The rotation is the peak of that correlation, found by a direct search over a
grid on \(SO(3)\) whose resolution is set by the harmonic bandwidth. The same construction registers
three dimensional point clouds.

A space of spherical harmonics of degree \(n\) has dimension \(2n+1\), and its members are
eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator with eigenvalue \(n(n+1)\). Degree 1 is where a rigid
turn of the whole sphere lives,  rotation is read off the lowest degrees and finer
motion off higher ones.

\section{Perspective}

A homography is a projective transform. It maps points between two views exactly in two cases: a
planar scene seen under arbitrary camera motion, and an arbitrary scene seen under pure camera
rotation. It preserves collinearity and not parallelism, length or angle. With four point
correspondences the direct linear transform solves for it as a linear system, and random sample
consensus makes the estimate robust to wrong correspondences.

Without correspondences the warp is estimated directly from intensities. Lucas and Kanade's
alignment, reviewed in a unifying framework by Baker and Matthews, treats translation, affine and
homography warps alike: it linearizes the image around the current warp and updates the parameters
by gradient descent. The inverse compositional form precomputes the expensive terms once, on the
template, and is the efficient variant most extensions can use without losing that efficiency.

\section{Rigid registration from point constellations}

Where views differ by a large rotation, intensity based alignment loses its capture range and point
based registration keeps it. One published method, built for multi-view volumetric microscopy,
embeds fiducial points and matches their constellations with a local geometric descriptor invariant
to translation and rotation, reaching an average displacement of about one pixel. Because it depends
only on repeatable point detections it is more robust than intensity based registration and orders of
magnitude faster, and the same software is used to stabilise three dimensional time series, not only
to align views.
