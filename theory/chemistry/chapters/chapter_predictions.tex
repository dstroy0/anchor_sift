\chapter{What a chemistry reader would find}

\textbf{Nothing in this chapter has been measured here.} It is the design chemistry adds to the
engine and the predictions that design makes, stated before the reader exists. The engine reads a
protein and a crystal but has no reader for a molecule as such, and the oracle for bond lengths is a
directory reserved and not yet filled. The demonstrations that run on the primitives already in
the tree are in \texttt{examples/chemistry} and are described as what they are; everything below is a
prediction the reader would test.

\section{Where the new parts go, and where nothing is added}

Two modules are new and the rest is reuse, and one thing chemistry needs is authored by another
subject and only consumed here. The element itself, its proton count \(Z\), its electron set, the
Pauli exclusion behind the shell counts, and the periodic recurrence, is the atomic structure, and
that ledger is transcribed once in a shared \texttt{representation/atom} home. A duplicate element table under chemistry would be
a second source of truth for a fact chemistry did not establish, and the engine holds a commit that
would create one.

What chemistry adds is the bonding layer. A molecule needs a representation, atoms as points carrying
element vectors and bonds as the vectors between them, which is a subject under
\texttt{representation/structure} beside the protein and the crystal readers and is theirs to
coordinate, since the three share what an atom is. The bond lengths need an oracle,
\texttt{oracle/chemistry}, holding the tabulated lengths as facts apart from the language family
trees. Nothing else is added. The octet runs on the sift's existing intersection, the disordered
packing on the existing shuffles, and the departure on the existing measures. If the reuse is honest,
no primitive learns that chemistry exists, and the whole subject lives in one representation directory
and one oracle directory, over an element ledger it shares and does not own.

\section{The predictions}

\textbf{First.} The octet as a necessary condition will refuse no real molecule and will prune
candidate arrangements, with the error one directional. A structure the octet admits may still be the
wrong isomer. A verification pass stays mandatory, and the count of survivors will move with the
composition exactly as the lattice cascade's does: a formula holding one element will over-admit
because every atom is compositionally alike, and a formula holding several will prune hard.

\textbf{Second.} A bond-length oracle will be a positive control on par with the crystal cell edge
and stronger than any family tree, because valence fixes the value and a substance cannot argue with
it. A reader of molecular geometry checked against it will not be able to hide an error inside a
tolerance, for the same reason the backbone period could be called wrong the moment it left three.

\textbf{Third.} A crystal of a single element will return a large departure from the disordered
packing with nothing having authored it, placing chemistry beside the prime gaps and outside the band
every memoryless process occupies. A reading that took departure as evidence of production will be
wrong on every mineral, and this is the cleanest place in the tree to show that arrangement is not
authorship.

\textbf{Fourth.} Reading a periodic property along \(Z\) will recover the shell structure only if the
partition it is read against comes from outside the sequence. The engine reads a recurrence in two
composed steps, and both fix a single period. \texttt{measure/periodicity} scores a candidate period
against its own family of multiples, and a repeat at three is read as three and not as its harmonic
six. \texttt{reference/periodic} then builds the maximum-entropy background at that one period, binding
each position to the positions congruent to it modulo \(P\). A periodic property along \(Z\) is not one
period: the shell lengths are \(2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32\), a sequence that grows. No single \(P\) makes the
phase classes fall on the shell closures, and a reader restricted to one period reports a harmonic, a
single wrong period, or nothing. The partition module names the fix. A partition is stipulated,
estimated by a sweep, or supervised from outside. It is not stipulated, since a judgment-picked
boundary set is the bounding this work forbids. It cannot be estimated, since a sweep for one period
cannot recover a growing one, and that attempt is the trap. It is supervised: the shell boundaries are
ground truth the atomic-structure ledger carries, the same kind of fact as the bond-length oracle and
reached from a second direction. The prediction is that the real assignment departs far from its
shuffle once the boundaries are supplied, and sits inside the shuffle band to a single-period reader
without them. The method is shown to run in
\texttt{examples/chemistry/4\_measure/a\_single\_period\_cannot\_see\_a\_growing\_one.py}, where a
single-period reader departs on a constant recurrence and goes flat on a growing one while a reader
handed the boundaries departs; that example writes no element data, and the real sequence and its
boundaries are the ledger's. This is the harmonic trap, already met for one period in
\texttt{measure/periodicity} and arriving again where the recurrence grows, named here so it cannot be
met as a surprise.

\textbf{Fifth.} No reading here will need a bounded element set or a chosen cutoff radius, and any
that appears to will be measuring the bound. The prediction is the engine's own history restated:
the bound will have to come off, and the reading will improve when it does.
