\chapter*{Abstract}

The workbook states what this work needs and cannot afford. Every claim about how far the
construction reaches is empirical, quantified over a set nobody has enumerated, and getting from a
list of instances to a statement about a domain takes a controlled series inside that domain with an
outside answer attached to every row. It then prices that at years, because obtaining a row costs a
database, a fetch, or a laboratory.

Games remove the price. A subtraction game's Grundy sequence is eventually periodic, proved, and its
period is a quantity a dull exact routine computes by comparison. So a row costs a second of
arithmetic, the number of rows is a choice, and no row's answer came from the instrument being
tested. Three hundred and eighty five move sets were generated. Of the three hundred and eighty
three whose period is inside the window the detector can score, the detector returned the exact
period on three hundred and eighty three, at a margin sixteen to twenty six times the best any
shuffle of the same values reached, with no row failing to clear its own floor.

Two older answers sit beside that series. Bouton settled Nim's losing positions in 1901 and every
axis line of a Nim grid holds exactly one, on sixty four lines, four thousand and ninety six, and
thirty two thousand seven hundred and sixty eight. Wythoff settled his own in 1907, and a grid
played out by the minimum excludant recursion, holding no formula, reproduces his closed form
exactly and returns the golden ratio to one part in a thousand.

The correction is on the other side of that pair and it reaches past this book. Wythoff's positions
make a word with no period in it, and handed that word the period detector returns a number anyway.
The number clears its own shuffle floor by three to twenty nine times, so no margin test refuses it.
It is the denominator of a continued fraction convergent of the word's slope, on sixty one of sixty
three rows across nine slopes, and it grows as the window widens: eight, then thirteen, then twenty
one, then thirty four, then fifty five. A rotation by an irrational does agree with itself at the
denominator of a good rational approximation to it, and the detector reads that agreement correctly.
Only the name on the output is false. The diagnostic that separates the two costs nothing and uses
no oracle: a real period is the same number at every window, and one hundred and fifteen of one
hundred and fifteen periodic games give one answer across five windows where none of nine aperiodic
words gives one across seven.

The played half of the book reports a negative and keeps it. Reversi leaves a departure from the
null of twenty one percent, twenty five times the spread between disjoint batches, and a control
game on the same board with the flip rule removed sits on the null. So the departure is the flip
rule. It is not the play: three arms of measurably different strength, in nine pairings, order
themselves clearly by win share and not at all by departure, at a correlation of 0.07.
