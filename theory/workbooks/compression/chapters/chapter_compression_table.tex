\chapter{\texorpdfstring{The compression table}{The compression table}}
\label{chap:compression-table}

\textbf{Purpose:} Track, set by set, the size the crystal reaches against the floor the data allows. The gap between the two is always a number, and every change to the coder is judged against that gap.

\textbf{Scope:} The \texttt{.\allowbreak{}kcr} (the Kolmogorov information crystal, 23 September) and what it holds: the tower's coefficients, the stream, the deflated side bytes, and the seal. The coder is M9 of engine\_\allowbreak{}table.md, and the seal is M13. Measurements come from \hyperref[chap:ledger]{ledger.md}, plus the RSNA project's own runs where they are named. Statuses follow \hyperref[chap:readme]{README.md}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{What “the floor” means here}{What "the floor" means here}}

The true floor is the Kolmogorov complexity K(x) of a set: the length of the shortest program that prints it. K is not computable, and it cannot be measured directly. What can be measured is a ladder of bounds, each one exact for a named class of coder, and each lower than the one before as the coder is allowed to see more. The crystal's size is judged against the rung for the coder it actually is, and the next rung down shows what a better coder could still take.

Each bound is an integer count of bits, and each is a bit length of an exact integer (a multinomial coefficient). Stating it needs no logarithm or float. The cost is size: at 419,430,400 voxels a sample, the multinomial runs to billions of bits. Computing F1 to F3 exactly at that size is open: the tool is not built, and neither is its method.

\textbf{What F4 says (corrected 23 September).} On all 25 44b6 samples, bits 0 to 3 flip 499 to 500 times per thousand transitions in every window of 19 samples, and 350 to 499 in the other 6. The earlier reading here counted those planes as a floor near 25\% to 31\% of raw. That undercounted: bits 4 and 5 are coin flips too (below), and counting uniform planes is not the floor anyway. The floor is the entropy of each voxel's noise at its own level, measured below on 44b6\_\allowbreak{}0113de3b.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The table's algebra}{The table's algebra}}

Every rung and every accepted reading holds to one of these identities, as each engine member holds to its A-number in engine\_\allowbreak{}table.md. A reading that cites none is not accepted. Write a voxel's value in frame t as I\_\allowbreak{}t = P + S\_\allowbreak{}t + n\_\allowbreak{}t: P its fixed pattern, S\_\allowbreak{}t the structure (the bodies and whatever the tracks carry), and n\_\allowbreak{}t the noise, independent between frames and between voxels, mean 0, variance σ²(L) at level L. In C14 to C20, a row is a row of the noise vector integration table.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C0. Exact counts (F1, F2, F3, F5)}{C0. Exact counts (F1, F2, F3, F5)}}

n symbols with counts c\_\allowbreak{}v have exactly n! / Π\_\allowbreak{}v c\_\allowbreak{}v! arrangements. The enumerative code (the stream's index among them) spends ⌈log₂(n! / Π\_\allowbreak{}v c\_\allowbreak{}v!)⌉ bits, and no code that sees only the counts spends less. It is the bit length of an exact integer, with no logarithm formed. F1 takes it over the values, F2 per floor, F3 per context, F5 per level under the functionals.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C1. What one frame costs, given everything else (F4)}{C1. What one frame costs, given everything else (F4)}}

Integer values whose noise has spread σ ≫ 1 carry H ≈ ½ log₂(2πe σ²) bits (a Gaussian's entropy at unit step). For one frame of V voxels:

F4 = Σ\_\allowbreak{}v ½ log₂(2πe σ²(L\_\allowbreak{}v)) bits,

and per voxel the mean of that sum. The Gaussian form is the working estimate; C0 taken per level is its exact form (F5).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C2. The two-part code (F3′, F4)}{C2. The two-part code (F3′, F4)}}

For N frames, a code spends M + N·V·F4 bits, with M the model: P, the gain, the read noise, the tracks. Per frame that is M/N + V·F4, which goes to V·F4 as N grows. The model is the few words; the frame's own draw is the rest.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C3. The frame difference removes the fixed pattern (the photon transfer curve)}{C3. The frame difference removes the fixed pattern (the photon transfer curve)}}

d = I\_\allowbreak{}\{t+1\} − I\_\allowbreak{}t = ΔS + (n\_\allowbreak{}\{t+1\} − n\_\allowbreak{}t). P cancels exactly, and var(d) = var(ΔS) + 2σ².

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C4. The second difference removes linear drift (the photon transfer curve)}{C4. The second difference removes linear drift (the photon transfer curve)}}

e = 2I\_\allowbreak{}t − I\_\allowbreak{}\{t−1\} − I\_\allowbreak{}\{t+1\} = (2S\_\allowbreak{}t − S\_\allowbreak{}\{t−1\} − S\_\allowbreak{}\{t+1\}) + (2n\_\allowbreak{}t − n\_\allowbreak{}\{t−1\} − n\_\allowbreak{}\{t+1\}), with noise variance 4σ² + σ² + σ² = 6σ². Where S moves linearly in t, e holds noise only, and var(e)/6 = σ².

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C5. The neighbor removes smooth structure (F4 by local coherence)}{C5. The neighbor removes smooth structure (F4 by local coherence)}}

For x the next voxel along a row, d − d\_\allowbreak{}x = (ΔS − ΔS\_\allowbreak{}x) + four independent noise terms, and var(d − d\_\allowbreak{}x) = var(ΔS − ΔS\_\allowbreak{}x) + 4σ² ≥ 4σ². So σ² ≤ var(d − d\_\allowbreak{}x) / 4, with equality where the structure changes the same at both voxels: an upper bound that tightens as the structure smooths.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C6. Correlation separates noise from structure (F4 by local coherence)}{C6. Correlation separates noise from structure (F4 by local coherence)}}

White noise has no covariance with its neighbor, which gives corr(d, d\_\allowbreak{}x) = cov(ΔS, ΔS\_\allowbreak{}x) / (var(ΔS) + 2σ²). It is 0 where there is no structure and goes to 1 where structure dominates.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C7. The photon transfer curve (F4)}{C7. The photon transfer curve (F4)}}

Shot noise counts photons, and its variance is proportional to the signal: σ²(L) = g·(L − O) + R², with g the gain, O the dark offset and R the read noise. A line fitted as σ² = g·L + c has c = R² − g·O and meets zero at L₀ = −c/g. If O = 0, then c = R².

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C8. The robust spread (the photon transfer curve)}{C8. The robust spread (the photon transfer curve)}}

For Gaussian noise, the median absolute deviation × 1.4826 = σ. A body that crosses a voxel in a few of its frames moves the mean and the plain variance, but not the median.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C9. The gap in units (every row's gap)}{C9. The gap in units (every row's gap)}}

A stream of B bits a voxel sits (B − F4) / 16 of raw above the floor, and V·(B − F4) / 8 bytes above it per frame.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C10. No combination of frames goes below their draws (F4)}{C10. No combination of frames goes below their draws (F4)}}

N frames of independent k-bit draws have 2\textasciicircum{}(N·k) possible values. A stamp of k bits (an AND, an OR, an XOR, a sum) names only 2\textasciicircum{}k of them. A lossless code keeps the other (N − 1)·k bits somewhere. What every frame shares is read once: ∧\_\allowbreak{}t I\_\allowbreak{}t gives the bits set in every frame, and ∧\_\allowbreak{}t ¬I\_\allowbreak{}t the bits clear in every frame. Those are the anchor bits.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C11. A reversible walk conserves bits (F4, the crystal)}{C11. A reversible walk conserves bits (F4, the crystal)}}

Every lifting step is an integer bijection (A8). Walking the scheduler's record back or forward maps each state to exactly one state and keeps its bit count. The record holds the ops (few bits) and the draw (V·F4 a frame). It shrinks to the ops alone only when the draw came from a program, as in the mock, where the draws recovered to a 64-bit xorshift seed.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C12. Linear complexity (the low planes)}{C12. Linear complexity (the low planes)}}

A random sequence of n bits has linear complexity L near n/2, and L ≤ n/2 − k with probability about 2\textasciicircum{}(−2k). The “low” mark at k = 8 is about 2⁻¹⁶. A linear generator with s bits of state has L ≤ s for every n.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C13. The coder against the count (F5)}{C13. The coder against the count (F5)}}

Exact arithmetic coding spends at most ⌈Σ −log₂ p⌉ + 1 bits over a whole stream, within 2 bits of the model's length. Enumerative coding (C0) spends exactly the count. A fixed-width range coder rounds each interval and loses a sliver per symbol.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C14. Charge before the gain sits on the shot line (F4; rows 2 to 6)}{C14. Charge before the gain sits on the shot line (F4; rows 2 to 6)}}

Let a voxel collect, each frame, μ photoelectrons, D·Δt dark electrons and ε spurious ones, each a Poisson count and all before the gain. Let the offset O be added after the gain, with the level-free terms after the draw (read R², thermal σ²\_\allowbreak{}J, reset σ²\_\allowbreak{}kTC) independent and of mean 0. With one gain g for every electron,

L̄ = O + g·(μ + D·Δt + ε),  σ² = g²·(μ + D·Δt + ε) + R² + σ²\_\allowbreak{}J + σ²\_\allowbreak{}kTC,

giving σ² = g·(L̄ − O) + R² + σ²\_\allowbreak{}J + σ²\_\allowbreak{}kTC exactly. Charge that enters as whole electrons before the gain moves a voxel along the transfer line and never off it. At one exposure, the dark level, the dark shot and spurious charge are read as light, and the line's intercept (C7), c = R² + σ²\_\allowbreak{}J + σ²\_\allowbreak{}kTC − g·O, holds none of them. Dark current is read only where the light is known to be zero. A bias frame (no light, no exposure) gives O as its mean and the level-free sum as its variance. A dark frame at Δt adds g·D·Δt to the mean and g²·D·Δt to the variance, and their ratio is g. No frame at one temperature splits R², σ²\_\allowbreak{}J and σ²\_\allowbreak{}kTC from each other.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C15. The cumulant ladder of a gained count (F4, F5; rows 1, 12 and 15)}{C15. The cumulant ladder of a gained count (F4, F5; rows 1, 12 and 15)}}

Let each electron give an independent output X in lane units, of one law for every electron, and let the count of electrons be Poisson with mean n̄. The output is a compound Poisson sum, whose cumulants are κ\_\allowbreak{}r = n̄·E[X\textasciicircum{}r] for every r ≥ 1. Level-free terms added after the draw (C14) change no slope, and symmetric ones add 0 to every odd cumulant. Against the level, each cumulant is a line of slope

s\_\allowbreak{}r = E[X\textasciicircum{}r] / E[X],

giving s₁ = 1 and s₂ = g·F², with g = E[X] the gain and F² = E[X²] / E[X]² the excess factor (row 12). The transfer curve's slope (C7) is g·F², larger than g by the excess factor. One output for every electron (X = g) gives s\_\allowbreak{}r = g\textasciicircum{}(r−1): F² = 1, and the rungs g, g², g³ the moment ladder is graded against (row 1). An electron-multiplying register's output for one electron is near exponential, with E[X\textasciicircum{}r] = r!·g\textasciicircum{}r. Then F² = 2, s₂ = 2g, s₃ = 6g² and s₃ = 3g·s₂.

The ladder is log-convex. For X ≥ 0, Cauchy–Schwarz gives E[X²]² ≤ E[X]·E[X³] and E[X³]² ≤ E[X²]·E[X⁴], and therefore

s₃ ≥ s₂²,  s₂·s₄ ≥ s₃²,

each an equality only where every electron gives the same X. Both are cross-multiplied comparisons of fitted slopes, with no root or quotient formed. A ladder that breaks either is not one gained Poisson count plus symmetric level-free noise. The slope s₂ alone never splits g from F²; s₂ and s₃ together split them only once the single electron's law is named.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C16. Thinning keeps a Poisson count Poisson (rows 13, 14 and 16)}{C16. Thinning keeps a Poisson count Poisson (rows 13, 14 and 16)}}

If N is Poisson with mean λ and each of its events is kept independently with probability q, the kept count is Poisson with mean q·λ, and the kept and lost counts are independent: the generating function exp(λ·(z − 1)), taken at 1 − q + q·z, is exp(q·λ·(z − 1)). Quantum efficiency (row 14) and partition (row 13) thin the light. On Poisson light they only rescale its mean, and no reading of the frames names Q\_\allowbreak{}e or a partition apart from the light's own rate. For any count with Fano factor f = var(N) / E[N], the thinned count's is

f′ = 1 + q·(f − 1),

A thinning moves a count toward Poisson and never away, and partition is read only on a light whose f ≠ 1. The same identity splits a Poisson count among places. Photons scattered into the neighboring pixels before the draw (optical crosstalk, row 16) leave each pixel's count an independent Poisson count at its own blurred rate.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C17. The structure function over lags (rows 7 and 10)}{C17. The structure function over lags (rows 7 and 10)}}

For noise stationary in t with autocovariance γ(k) at lag k, the structure function D(k) = E[(n\_\allowbreak{}\{t+k\} − n\_\allowbreak{}t)²] is 2·(γ(0) − γ(k)). White noise holds D(k) = D(1) = 2σ² at every lag k ≥ 1, and a random walk of step variance q holds D(k) = k·q. An octave held for 2\textasciicircum{}o frames, redrawn at every multiple of 2\textasciicircum{}o with variance v, adds 2v·min(k, 2\textasciicircum{}o) / 2\textasciicircum{}o, averaged over the frame's phase: it grows linearly to lag 2\textasciicircum{}o and holds there.

Taken less the x neighbor's difference, D\_\allowbreak{}x(k) = E[(d\_\allowbreak{}k − d\_\allowbreak{}\{k,x\})²] with d\_\allowbreak{}k = I\_\allowbreak{}\{t+k\} − I\_\allowbreak{}t. A term both voxels share additively cancels exactly, and each voxel's own terms add: for independent noise of one law, D\_\allowbreak{}x(k) = 2·D(k). A scale a\_\allowbreak{}t shared by the light (row 10) does not cancel. It adds (L − L\_\allowbreak{}x)²·D\_\allowbreak{}a(k), with D\_\allowbreak{}a the scale's own structure function, which is zero only where the two levels are equal.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C18. The row and column sums (rows 8, 9 and 10)}{C18. The row and column sums (rows 8, 9 and 10)}}

Take one frame pair's differences d over a set of voxels (the static ones). Let each be d = r + c + p + e, with r shared by the voxel's row, c by its column, p by its whole plane and e its own, all independent and of mean 0, with variances σ²\_\allowbreak{}r, σ²\_\allowbreak{}c, σ²\_\allowbreak{}p and s. For two distinct voxels, E[d·d′] is σ²\_\allowbreak{}r + σ²\_\allowbreak{}p on the same row, σ²\_\allowbreak{}c + σ²\_\allowbreak{}p on the same column, and σ²\_\allowbreak{}p otherwise. Write T = Σ d² over the M voxels. A squared sum less its own squares is the sum of its ordered pairs' products. Take A\_\allowbreak{}row = Σ over rows of (Σ d)², less T; A\_\allowbreak{}column likewise; A\_\allowbreak{}plane = Σ over planes of (Σ d)², less T; and K\_\allowbreak{}row, K\_\allowbreak{}column and K\_\allowbreak{}plane the ordered pairs each holds. Then

E[A\_\allowbreak{}row] = K\_\allowbreak{}row·(σ²\_\allowbreak{}r + σ²\_\allowbreak{}p),  E[A\_\allowbreak{}column] = K\_\allowbreak{}column·(σ²\_\allowbreak{}c + σ²\_\allowbreak{}p), E[A\_\allowbreak{}plane − A\_\allowbreak{}row − A\_\allowbreak{}column] = (K\_\allowbreak{}plane − K\_\allowbreak{}row − K\_\allowbreak{}column)·σ²\_\allowbreak{}p,  E[T] = M·(σ²\_\allowbreak{}r + σ²\_\allowbreak{}c + σ²\_\allowbreak{}p + s),

for any count of voxels on any row or column, balanced or not. Each is a sum of integer products, and each variance follows by cross-multiplying over the common denominator M·K\_\allowbreak{}row·K\_\allowbreak{}column·(K\_\allowbreak{}plane − K\_\allowbreak{}row − K\_\allowbreak{}column), as \texttt{noise\_\allowbreak{}static\_\allowbreak{}readings} does. The same three lines are E16's patterns ROWS, COLUMNS and PLANES in engine\_\allowbreak{}table.md.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C19. The single-electron tail (row 11)}{C19. The single-electron tail (row 11)}}

Charge that enters as whole electrons before the gain joins the light's count (C14) and sits on every cumulant's line through the offset. With the level-free noise symmetric, every odd cumulant is then zero at the offset:

κ₃ = s₃·(L̄ − O),  κ₂ = s₂·(L̄ − O) + R²,

with R² the whole level-free sum of C14. The two lines' intercepts c₂ and c₃ give O·s₃ = −c₃ and R²·s₃ = c₂·s₃ − c₃·s₂, cross-multiplied: the offset and the level-free sum apart, from frames at one exposure. A charge that does not enter as a whole electron before the gain (one made partway through the gain, which sees less of it) has its own output law X′ and rate ε′, both level-free. It adds ε′·E[X′] to the level and ε′·E[X′\textasciicircum{}r] to every cumulant. The offset κ₃ reads is O + ε′·(E[X′] − E[X′³] / s₃).

Spikes against dips read the same odd part. Where the joint law of the three frames around one is unchanged by the reflection x ↦ 2c − x, the reflection maps each spike at threshold h to a dip at h, and the expected counts are equal. The shot's own skew (s₃ > 0), a sparse tail and a body passing in one frame can each break that, and the two counts alone don't say which: row 11 is not separable here.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C20. Crosstalk in the neighbor correlation (rows 16 and 17)}{C20. Crosstalk in the neighbor correlation (rows 16 and 17)}}

Electrical crosstalk shares a fraction α of each voxel's draw with its two neighbors along one axis after the draw: d′(v) = d(v) + α·(d(v − δ) + d(v + δ)). For d independent between voxels, with variance V,

var(d′) = (1 + 2α²)·V,  cov(d′(v), d′(v + δ)) = 2α·V,  cov(d′(v), d′(v + 2δ)) = α²·V,

and 0 past two steps. The correlation is 2α / (1 + 2α²) at one step and α² / (1 + 2α²) at two, the same at every level, since it is a fixed fraction of whatever was drawn. With the crosstalk along x and V = 2σ², the neighbor route (C5) reads

E[(d′ − d′\_\allowbreak{}x)²] / 4 = σ²·(1 − 2α + 2α²),

where the raw route E[d′²] / 2 reads σ²·(1 + 2α²). A positive α lowers the neighbor route's slope and intercept by the same factor. Optical crosstalk scatters light before the draw, and by C16 the pixels' counts stay independent. It adds no correlation at any reach and shows only in the signal's edge spread (row 16). A correlation of the noise between neighbors (row 17) is sharing after the draw, and one that holds past the second step is not crosstalk of this form.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{C21. A pointwise step is its table (F3′; the table step)}{C21. A pointwise step is its table (F3′; the table step)}}

Let f map the lane's alphabet A = \{0, …, 2\textasciicircum{}b − 1\} to w-bit values. Its table T\_\allowbreak{}f holds f(a) at row a for every a in A. One pass of f over A fills it: 2\textasciicircum{}b evaluations, however many lanes it is read on afterward. For every lane x, T\_\allowbreak{}f[x] = f(x) exactly, because the table is f's graph on A and x lies in A. One table read through another is their composition, T\_\allowbreak{}g[T\_\allowbreak{}f[x]] = g(f(x)), wherever f's values lie in g's alphabet. A chain of steps therefore folds into one table in program order. The fold regroups and does not reorder, since g∘f and f∘g differ in general. The table costs 2\textasciicircum{}b·w bits to state, once for the set. In the two-part code (C2) it sits in the model M, whose share per frame goes to 0 as the frames grow. Inside the reversible stream (C11), a table must be a bijection on its index bits. Any other f rides widened, (x, y) ↦ (x, y ⊕ f(x)), which is its own inverse for every f.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The plan: noise departure by functionals (23 September)}{The plan: noise departure by functionals (23 September)}}

There is much more room to claim, by refining the spatial noise terms and the search. The algorithm can reach the theoretical floor universally if noise departure is treated carefully, with functionals. Once the real categories of noise are known, each gets its functional, and all of them are integrated.

\textbf{The claim, stated exactly.} K(x) is not computable: no algorithm reaches it on every input. What a coder can reach is the entropy of the source's true noise: Shannon's bound, the most any coder takes on average. A coder whose per-voxel counts come from functionals that describe the real noise reaches that bound, to within a constant, on every set those categories cover. “Universal” means exactly that: universal over the sets whose noise the category functionals describe. When a set needs a category the functionals lack, the gap to F5 names it.

\textbf{The categories to write functionals for.} The four noise vectors (noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower.md §16), each a deterministic functional of the sample, read and never assumed. Each is a sum of exact integers, with no float:

\textbf{Departure.} Where the history leaves the floor the functionals predict, something is there: a body, a split, a drive (noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower.md §17). The coder spends bits on departure only, and the search looks only there. The same functionals that set the floor also set what the search counts as a departure. So the null draws, the climb and the sift all read against the integrated floor instead of against one sample-wide reading.

\textbf{The coder that reaches F5.} The counts come from the functionals, and the coder spends Σ −log₂ p over the stream against them. How close it gets depends on its arithmetic:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact arithmetic coding} (the interval held as an exact integer of whatever width it needs) ends within 2 bits of that sum over the whole stream, however long.
\item \textbf{Enumerative coding} in exact integers (the stream's index among every stream with the same counts) spends exactly ⌈log₂(n! / Π c!)⌉ bits: the F1 and F2 counts themselves, with no loss at all. It costs a big-integer multiply per symbol; the engine's exact integer is that machinery.
\item \textbf{A fixed-width range coder} (32 or 64 bits) rounds each interval down to its width and loses a sliver per symbol, small but not zero. That is a rounding. It is the fast approximation to measure against the exact two, not the design.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Two things the 2 bits does not cover.}

\begin{itemize}
\item It is 2 bits from the \emph{model's} length. If the functionals miss a real category, the loss is the mismatch between the model and the data, and F5 sits above the true floor by that much.
\item Stating the model costs bits of its own: the functionals' parameters and the count tables. That is the two-part code (F3′), and a model is worth its bits only where it saves more.
\end{itemize}

It replaces the Rice coder's fixed block, k and escape (the audit's debts) with the data's own counts.

\textbf{Order of work.}

\item Measure F2 on one sample to learn the Rice coder's own gap (the list at the end).
\item Take the photon transfer curve per sample from the frames: shot and read separated, as exact sums. \section{\texorpdfstring{How to close the gap, in order}{How to close the gap, in order}}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Measure F2 on one sample} (44b6\_\allowbreak{}0113de3b). The gap between the crystal and F2 is the Rice coder's own share: its block size, its k and its escape against the counts. That share of the gap is recoverable without changing the tower.
\item \textbf{Measure F3 with the contexts the ledger already points at.} Grouping by floor (−1.16 MB) and pairs along t (−1.99 MB on this sample) are contexts. F3 says what the best such context can take, and so whether to build one.
\item \textbf{Settle F4.} Measured on 44b6\_\allowbreak{}0113de3b at 5.902 bits a voxel (36.9\%): bits 0 to 5 carry no linear generator, and the photon transfer curve with structure removed gives gain 1.162, read noise about 1.6, and no dark offset. Measured on the 25 at 6.229 bits a voxel (38.9\%), gain 0.986 on average. Still to do: the shot line above level 300, where it is assumed and not read; the constant regions and the spatially correlated category (44b6\_\allowbreak{}267148e4, 668e0cc7, 5740d24b) as functionals; the bent fits (12dfb391, 53f95252); the z correlation near 0.12 at the dimmest levels; nonlinear generator classes on the low planes. Read since on 24 of the kcr set, each measured and none yet a functional: the constant region (row 24 of the noise vector integration table), the dim z correlation (row 25) and the bending transfer curve (row 26).
\item \textbf{The noise functionals} (the plan above): the photon transfer curve, each category's functional, F5 integrated, and the range coder that reaches it.
\item \textbf{Build the coder the measurements pick}, and enter every set's new size in this table with its floor beside it.
\end{enumerate}
