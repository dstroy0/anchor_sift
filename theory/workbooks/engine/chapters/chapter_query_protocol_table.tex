\chapter{\texorpdfstring{The query protocol table}{The query protocol table}}
\label{chap:query-protocol-table}

\textbf{Purpose:} The query protocol step by step: what each step asks, the algebra it holds to, what it does, what it wants, what was tried and what it gave, its status, and the next move.

\textbf{Scope:} every step between a question put to a target and a branch consuming the answer: the ask, the unbound and bound passes, the gate, the order of asks, the contention read, cross-branch comparison and the record. The protocol's form and its mnemonics are in gnascor.md, and the open work around it is in engine\_\allowbreak{}plan.md. Statuses follow \hyperref[chap:readme]{README.md}, and every status names the run behind it.

\section{\texorpdfstring{The rule the protocol is held to}{The rule the protocol is held to}}

\textbf{Gate, then rank. Never one score.} A relation holds or it does not, and that answer carries no noise. A cost is measured and every cost carries noise. The gate decides which candidates are admissible and the rank orders whatever survives, and the two are never added together. Kept apart, a noisy cost can cost speed and can never cost correctness. The engine already holds this rule in its own descent: survival is a conjunction, order cannot change a conjunction, and a planner that steers badly costs speed and never a wrong survivor (\texttt{src/\allowbreak{}c/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}nbody/\allowbreak{}orior/\allowbreak{}orior\_\allowbreak{}descent.\allowbreak{}h}).

\textbf{Steer on what is known to be true.} Every relation the ladder holds is arithmetic every system that computes agrees about, and the host computes it. The gate is planned against that ground truth on the host, and a target is asked only the cases the plan placed.

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q2. The unbound pass and the baseline}}
\begin{description}
\item[{the algebra it holds to}] An ask with no bound returns its cost in place of a bit. The spread of those costs is \texttt{.\allowbreak{}knf}, and every bound after is expressed against it.
\item[{does}] \texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}cost} (\texttt{src/\allowbreak{}cu/\allowbreak{}transpiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}monolith.\allowbreak{}cu}) reads the part's clock (\texttt{CS2R SR\_\allowbreak{}CLOCKLO}) before and after a chain of each base precept, 100 steps a turn and \texttt{turns} turns, every step one PTX instruction under a predicate read from a parameter the assembler cannot see, and each precept again interleaved with every other. \texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}run} puts it \texttt{cost runs} times and reads each section's least m, its spread s and its mean, and holds every chain's last word to \texttt{precept\_\allowbreak{}applied} on the host.
\item[{wants}] A watchdog stop marked as a censored sample and not a cost. A reference ask put alongside each real one, in the same conditions.

A chain written plainly comes out of the assembler as one instruction a turn, an empty fence after each step notwithstanding; predicated, every step is kept.
\item[{status}] \textbf{measured}

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q5. The order of asks for slicing}}
\begin{description}
\item[{the algebra it holds to}] A chain of \texttt{n} links measured by asks that each cover half the links, any two overlapping on a quarter: ask \texttt{r} covers link \texttt{c} where \texttt{(r + 1) \& (c + 1)} has an odd count of ones, the rows of a Hadamard matrix of order \texttt{n + 1} with its first row and column dropped. One ask informs every link at once. The per-link solve is \texttt{(n + 1)·x = 4·Sᵀb -\allowbreak{} 2·(Σb)·1}, integer adds and a division by a power of two.
\item[{does}] \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}compiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}ask\_\allowbreak{}order.\allowbreak{}\{h,c\}}: \texttt{ask\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}covers} gives the order from the link count alone and stores nothing, and \texttt{ask\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}solve} gives every link's cost scaled by \texttt{n + 1}. A count one short of a power of two, 3 to 63, has a known order, and every other count is refused and writes nothing.
\item[{wants}] A chain composed to a length a known order holds, and its asks emitted to a target (Q1).
\item[{tried, and what it gave}] \textbf{Proved} (\texttt{test/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}compiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}ask\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}c}, run by \texttt{maint/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}chain\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}sh}, 1,231 checks, 0 failed, at 3, 7, 15, 31 and 63 links): \texttt{4·Sᵀ -\allowbreak{} 2·J} times \texttt{S} is \texttt{(n + 1)} times the identity entry by entry; every ask covers \texttt{(n + 1)/\allowbreak{}2} links and any two share \texttt{(n + 1)/\allowbreak{}4}; integer link costs solve back exactly at 1 to 8 passes; a fixed cost on every ask, and a cost growing with the square of the count an ask covers, each move every link by one amount and leave their order as it was; every count 0 to 127 without a known order is refused. \textbf{Measured}, against one ask per link at one budget (\texttt{python maint/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}measure\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}py}, check 4): the gain is √(n + 1)/2, 1.09x at 3 links, 2.07x at 15, 8.16x at 255, against 1.00, 2.00 and 8.00 predicted. \textbf{Measured}, against a drawn order at the budget with nothing spare (check 5): the known order wins by 5.5x at the median worst-link error, 33.7x at the 95th and 125.1x at the worst of 400, and 17 of 400 drawn orders do not come apart at all. \textbf{Measured} on the host through the protocol (\texttt{test/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}compiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}query\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}c}, run by \texttt{maint/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}chain\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}sh}): \texttt{query\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}put} (\texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}compiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}query\_\allowbreak{}order.\allowbreak{}\{h,c\}}) puts seven links, each a QUERY\_\allowbreak{}ADVANCES ask at a word nothing writes and link k reading it 1 + 64·k times, on the clock the protocol finds by asking, read finely by counting reads of it between turns: each run's cost an exact rational, each of 64 passes solved on its own in exact integers, nothing rounded, summed or cut to a least. The run size is read off the part, the size whose weakest neighbor pair leans hardest; the floor against size has a least between the counting's spread and the interference long runs gather, and the size named moves from run to run (2\textasciicircum{}10 to 2\textasciicircum{}14 puts). Every neighbor pair reads dearer in 44 to 54 of 64 passes, past twice the spread. One read against five, QUERY\_\allowbreak{}EQUALS against QUERY\_\allowbreak{}HOLDS, does not order: an ask's own overhead is larger than four reads.
\item[{status}] \textbf{proved} (the order, the solve and what the count's square does to it), \textbf{measured} (gain, known against drawn; the order put to the host)
\item[{next}] Emit the order to the device once the run channel reaches its addresses.
\end{description}

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q7. The contention read}}
\begin{description}
\item[{the algebra it holds to}] Where links contend the cost of a set is not the sum of its parts. Contention grows as the square of how many links an ask covers. What is left of a sweep ask's cost once the known order's links are taken out is read against a constant, the count and the square of the count, and contention is read where the square's slope clears twice its own spread: \texttt{Puy²·(N + 1) > 4·Pyy·Puu} with the count taken out, every term an exact integer.
\item[{does}] \texttt{ask\_\allowbreak{}links\_\allowbreak{}read} in \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}c/\allowbreak{}transpiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}ask\_\allowbreak{}order.\allowbreak{}c}. The sweep asks cover one link, half plus one and every link, in turn, each a prefix of an order of the links drawn from a seed.
\item[{wants}] Sweep asks emitted beside the known order's (Q1).
\item[{tried, and what it gave}] \textbf{Measured} (\texttt{ask\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}c}, 15 links, 4 passes of the known order, 45 sweep asks, 300 trials, costs in integers around an overhead with a spread of 1000 a pass): contention found on 3.0\% with none, 83.6\% with up to 80 a pair, 100\% with up to 200. \textbf{Measured}, the two terms beside the square: every ask pays an overhead, and the known order spreads it evenly over every link, which reaches a sweep ask in proportion to the count it covers. Read against the square alone the read found contention on 0\% at every amount; with the constant and the count taken out it reads as above. \textbf{Measured}, where the sweep asks sit: covering every count from 1 to 15 found 54.0\% at up to 80 a pair, and the two ends and the middle found 83.6\% from the same 45 asks, the false alarms 2.0\% and 3.0\%. \textbf{Measured} in floating point (\texttt{measure\_\allowbreak{}check.\allowbreak{}py}, check 6): an additive solve's per-link answers are 1.27x as far off at 0.08 a pair, and the square's term carried in the solve brings them back to 1.01x. On the host the sweep asks are put and timed as exact rationals (\texttt{query\_\allowbreak{}order\_\allowbreak{}sweep}), and the read itself takes integer costs: it is not yet put over exact rationals, and no host reading of it stands. \textbf{Measured} on the part (\texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}run}, Q2): two chains with no link between them, interleaved a step at a time, cost the dearer of the two alone, in all 91 pairs of precepts that each cost a cycle a step or more. Chains that share no link do not contend.
\item[{status}] \textbf{measured}
\item[{next}] Emit the sweep asks to the device beside Q5's.
\end{description}

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q8. Cross-branch comparison}}

\item[{tried, and what it gave}] Nothing has run. The rule that a symmetric magnitude cannot see an odd question is shown on the first move in chess (\texttt{examples/\allowbreak{}game\_\allowbreak{}theory/\allowbreak{}6\_\allowbreak{}oracle/\allowbreak{}first\_\allowbreak{}move\_\allowbreak{}advantage.\allowbreak{}py}).

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q9. The record}}
\begin{description}
\item[{the algebra it holds to}] A known order is a carrier and a shuffle is not: the same asks reach the part either way, and only the record of the order makes the answers decodable. The record holds every ask, its order and its seed, every answer with its cost, refusals and censored samples each marked, and then the winning path per problem over those same asks.
\item[{does}] \texttt{chain\_\allowbreak{}shuffle} takes a seed. Nothing writes the record.
\item[{wants}] The record's face, under the member's stem beside \texttt{.\allowbreak{}kdm} and \texttt{.\allowbreak{}knf}. The face is settled and Doug names its suffix (Open 8 in the plan). The record stores what the host cannot reproduce and nothing it can. The known order follows from \texttt{n}, the sweep from its seed, the candidates from the ladder's cases and the descent's placement from those. The record holds those few numbers and the target's answers, and the file is small for that reason.
\item[{tried, and what it gave}] Nothing has run.
\item[{status}] \textbf{theory}
\item[{next}] Follows Q1.
\end{description}

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q15. One ask, many answers}}
\begin{description}

\tablerecord{\textbf{Q17. Held against NVIDIA's compiler}}
\begin{description}

\section{\texorpdfstring{The algebra of the protocol}{The algebra of the protocol}}

Every line is exact. A cost is a reading and carries a spread; a relation is arithmetic and carries none.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P1. The ask and its bound (Q1, Q2, Q3)}{P1. The ask and its bound (Q1, Q2, Q3)}}

An ask a = (address, qualifier) returns, unbound, its cost t(a). Bound by β it returns one bit:

bit(a) = [qualifier holds] · [t(a) ≤ β]

A missing address, a false qualifier, a timeout, an error and too many cycles all read 0. What tells them apart is the baseline, the spread of unbound costs, and the bound is read off it (Q2) and never written by hand. A run that never returns is apart from all of these, a reset and not a reading, and P9 holds it.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P2. The gate (Q4)}{P2. The gate (Q4)}}

A relation r is held on cases K = \{k\_\allowbreak{}1, …, k\_\allowbreak{}m\}. A candidate c survives when

c(k) = r(k) for every k ∈ K

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The survivors are a conjunction.} S = \{c : c(k) = r(k) for every k ∈ K\} does not depend on the order of K, and no order of the cases changes which candidates survive.
\item \textbf{The order is the price.} Putting K in order π costs Σ\_\allowbreak{}c f\_\allowbreak{}π(c) evaluations, f\_\allowbreak{}π(c) the place of the first case c fails (m for a survivor). The descent puts first the case the most remaining candidates fail.
\item \textbf{No cost enters S.} A candidate that fails a case is wrong on every part, and no part is asked about it.
\item \textbf{Proved} (Q4): the descent's survivors equal \texttt{chain\_\allowbreak{}build}'s on every relation the ladder holds.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P3. The order of asks (Q5)}{P3. The order of asks (Q5)}}

For a chain of n links, n + 1 a power of two, ask r covers link c where (r + 1) \& (c + 1) has an odd count of ones: the rows of a Hadamard matrix of order n + 1 with its first row and column dropped, read as 0 and 1. With b\_\allowbreak{}r the cost ask r returns and x the per-link costs:

(n + 1)·x = 4·Sᵀb − 2·(Σb)·1

\begin{itemize}
\item Every link is covered by (n + 1)/2 asks and any two links share (n + 1)/4, and that is the identity 4·SᵀS − (n + 1)·J = (n + 1)·I, with Σb = ((n + 1)/2)·Σx (\textbf{proved} here for n = 3, 7, 15, 31 and 63). The solve is integer adds and one division by a power of two (\textbf{proved}, Q5).
\item Against one ask a link, the gain is √(n + 1)/2 (derived; \textbf{measured} 1.09, 2.07 and 8.16 at 3, 15 and 255 links against 1.00, 2.00 and 8.00, Q5).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P4. Contention (Q7)}{P4. Contention (Q7)}}

Each of N sweep asks covers a count u of links, and y is what is left of its cost once the known order's links are taken out. The model is y = α + γ·u + δ·u² + noise, and contention is δ > 0. With every sum centered and scaled by N,

C\_\allowbreak{}pq = N·Σpq − Σp·Σq

and the count taken out of the square s = u² and of the leftover y,

P\_\allowbreak{}ss = C\_\allowbreak{}uu·C\_\allowbreak{}ss − C\_\allowbreak{}us²,  P\_\allowbreak{}sy = C\_\allowbreak{}uu·C\_\allowbreak{}sy − C\_\allowbreak{}us·C\_\allowbreak{}uy,  P\_\allowbreak{}yy = C\_\allowbreak{}uu·C\_\allowbreak{}yy − C\_\allowbreak{}uy²

the square's slope P\_\allowbreak{}sy/P\_\allowbreak{}ss has squared spread (P\_\allowbreak{}yy − P\_\allowbreak{}sy²/P\_\allowbreak{}ss)/((N − 3)·P\_\allowbreak{}ss), with N − 3 left over past the three unknowns. The slope stands above twice its spread exactly when

P\_\allowbreak{}sy²·(N + 1) > 4·P\_\allowbreak{}yy·P\_\allowbreak{}ss

which is every term an exact integer and no division (\texttt{ask\_\allowbreak{}links\_\allowbreak{}read}, \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}c/\allowbreak{}transpiler/\allowbreak{}bootstrap/\allowbreak{}ask\_\allowbreak{}order.\allowbreak{}c}). Fewer than three counts covered leaves P\_\allowbreak{}ss = 0 and nothing to read; P\_\allowbreak{}sy ≤ 0 reads as adding.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P5. One ask, many answers (Q15)}{P5. One ask, many answers (Q15)}}

A set of checks each failing with share p is asked as one bit, 1 where every check holds and the set halved on a 0. Past p = (3 − √5)/2, about 0.382, asking one check at a time costs no more than any scheme that asks a set as one bit (cited from knowledge: P. Ungar, “The cutoff point for group testing”, Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 13 (1960) 49–54). The channel's answer of 128 bits carries one bit a check, which pays on every relation (\textbf{measured}, Q15).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P6. The rank, read against its own spread (Q16, Q17)}{P6. The rank, read against its own spread (Q16, Q17)}}

Each survivor c is run T times and its runs t\_\allowbreak{}c,1, …, t\_\allowbreak{}c,T taken. A run is lengthened by whatever else the host does and never shortened by it:

m\_\allowbreak{}c = min\_\allowbreak{}j t\_\allowbreak{}c,j,  s\_\allowbreak{}c = max\_\allowbreak{}j t\_\allowbreak{}c,j − m\_\allowbreak{}c,  s = max\_\allowbreak{}c s\_\allowbreak{}c

With the survivors ordered by m, c\_\allowbreak{}1 the least and c\_\allowbreak{}2 the next, c\_\allowbreak{}1 is the part's choice where

m\_\allowbreak{}c2 − m\_\allowbreak{}c1 > s

and otherwise the survivors are one cost and the ruleset keeps its form. The gate (P2) decides which candidates hold and this rule orders the survivors, and the two are never added together.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Measured} (Q16): 8 survivors in \texttt{loop\_\allowbreak{}back}'s place on sm\_\allowbreak{}86, m\_\allowbreak{}c2 − m\_\allowbreak{}c1 = 0.0525 ns a turn against s = 0.9260. The least of one run, \texttt{BRA.\allowbreak{}CONV}, is not the least of the next, \texttt{BRA} with a number, as a difference inside the spread gives.
\item \textbf{Measured} (Q2, the monolith): 15 precepts alone, a million steps a chain, 16 runs. The eleven single-instruction precepts sit within 0.006 cycles of each other in m against s = 2.22, one cost. The two deltas that stand are 0 to 4.05, no instruction against one, and 4.06 to 8.05, one against two. \texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}run} reads a delta inside s as DUAL or GRAY and one past it as LEAD (gnascor).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P7. A form asked of the part (Q16)}{P7. A form asked of the part (Q16)}}

The slot is \texttt{loop\_\allowbreak{}back loop where}. The question is a body the slot closes:

R7 ← 0, R0 ← N;  turn: R7 ← R7 + 1, R0 ← R0 − 1, P0 ← [R0 ≠ 0];  then the candidate, guarded by P0

\begin{itemize}
\item A candidate that takes the way back on P0 runs N turns and answers R7 = N.
\item A candidate that falls through runs one turn and answers 1.
\item N = 2 separates the two, 2 against 1. The counts after it, 1, 3, 5 and 64, hold the answer to N: a form that came back a fixed number of times answers that number and not N.
\item The candidates are every form in the part's machine file, with every register made R8 and every uniform one UR8, which the body does not keep, a predicate made P0 (PT, RZ and URZ kept), a label the body's and a number the distance back to it, 0x40 bytes over four instructions. Nothing decides beforehand which forms jump.
\item A survivor is walked: \texttt{sass\_\allowbreak{}loop\_\allowbreak{}walk} reads its encoding back through the forms of the machine file and checks it as a loop's way back, the flag, the distance, and every register the body keeps left as it was.
\item \textbf{Proved} (Q16): 8 of the 2927 forms answer every count, every one a \texttt{BRA}, and the walk agrees with all 8. No form answers N = 2 without answering every count.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P8. Held against NVIDIA's compiler (Q17)}{P8. Held against NVIDIA's compiler (Q17)}}

For a program p, C(p) is its text through \texttt{c.\allowbreak{}krs} and E(p) its lane through \texttt{sass.\allowbreak{}krs}. V(p) is what NVIDIA's compiler writes for C(p) and h(p) what the host oracle answers.

E(p)(k) = V(p)(k) = h(p)(k) on every lane k

is the gate, and the rank is P6 between E(p) and V(p), with |E(p)| and |V(p)| their instruction counts as a second reading. E(p) holds NVIDIA's line where m\_\allowbreak{}E(p) − m\_\allowbreak{}V(p) ≤ s.

\begin{itemize}
\item Where E(p) costs more, V(p)'s instructions over that part name forms, each in a slot. Each slot is a question for Q16: the forms of the machine file in that slot, the cases of the slot, and the survivors ranked by P6. NVIDIA's listing names the question and the part answers it.
\item The loop closes when E(p) holds NVIDIA's line on every program p, and every form in every ruleset was answered by the part.
\item \textbf{Built:} E(p) for the 8 record programs the host oracle runs, 16 lane programs, every instruction assembled against sm\_\allowbreak{}86's machine file (Q17). \textbf{Measured:} V(p) for the monolith's 18 blocks, one base precept each, held against our reader, our assembler and E(p) instruction by instruction (\texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}differences.\allowbreak{}md}, Q17). \textbf{Theory:} the rest.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{P9. The part's own bound (Q1, Q16, Q17)}{P9. The part's own bound (Q1, Q16, Q17)}}

P1 counts a timeout among the readings of 0, which holds only where the run returns. A run that never returns is a reading apart: the part's display watchdog ends it with a reset, and across a run of asks that reset can hold a kernel's own watchdog past its bound and stop the host with a DPC\_\allowbreak{}WATCHDOG\_\allowbreak{}VIOLATION.

A body runs without end only where control reaches a line it has passed and the way back never closes. Three contexts reach it:

\begin{itemize}
\item A turned bit makes the instruction a branch to itself or a line before it. An isolated instruction turned this way is most often an illegal encoding the part refuses as a clean 0; a turned branch is not refused.
\item The body holds a read it waits on, a register or a word read again until it carries a mark, and the turned bit closes the wait: the register the read names, the value it is held against, or the step that moves it. loop\_\allowbreak{}back (P7) is such a body, its way back taken on P0 = [R0 ≠ 0]; a bit that stalls the step or frees the branch leaves it turning without end.
\item A barrier every lane is waited at, where a turned bit sends a lane down a path that never reaches it, and the lanes that waited do not return.
\item The scheduler word waits on a scoreboard barrier a late result sets and that result never clears it. Above the operation the encoding holds a stall, a write and a read barrier and a wait over six barriers, none of it printed. A result ready after a fixed count carries that count as its stall; a result that comes back late, a load or a memory read, sets a barrier instead, and whatever reads the register it writes waits on that barrier. A barrier no instruction sets is at rest and a wait on it resolves at once; a wait on a barrier whose setter a turned bit sends a lane past never resolves, and the lane does not return.
\end{itemize}

The local absurdity, an isolated instruction turned illegal, the part refuses and the ask reads. The waited read is where a turned bit writes a lawful instruction that breaks the wait, and a lawful instruction runs where an illegal one refuses. An ask that holds a waited read carries its own bound in the body, a turn count past which no turned bit runs, and keeps that count in registers the turned bits do not reach: loop\_\allowbreak{}back holds its candidate off the counter R0, every register in the candidate made R8. The host's patience and the display watchdog are a reset, never the bound.

An ask that turns an instruction's bits is checked before it is put. The opcode, the low word's bits 0 to 11, names the operation, and a turned instruction is put to the part only where its key holds forms in the machine file and no control transfer or wait among them. Such an instruction falls through whatever its other bits hold. A turn past the key leaves the operation as it was. Every other turn is marked skipped and run on nothing, which closes the first context above for every key the machine file holds (\texttt{interface\_\allowbreak{}sass\_\allowbreak{}probe\_\allowbreak{}fields.\allowbreak{}c}, Q17).

The scheduler word is set once for a run and no turned bit reaches it. The assembler writes every instruction to wait on all six barriers and to stall the soonest its operation's result is read, the longest where the krs measures no count, and to set a write barrier where the operation's result comes back late and a read barrier where it is a store (\texttt{sass\_\allowbreak{}control\_\allowbreak{}safe}). \texttt{cubin\_\allowbreak{}safe} holds every instruction it reads to that stall. Which those are is the operation's schedule, a property of the operation the krs holds beside the scheduler's fields (\texttt{sass\_\allowbreak{}operation\_\allowbreak{}schedule}, \texttt{sass\_\allowbreak{}machine}), and not of the one encoding the machine file first saw it with: that encoding sets no write barrier on \texttt{LDS} and no read barrier on any store. A turned bit lies at 0 to 104 and leaves 105 and up as they were: the safe word stands on every flip, and no flip opens a path around a barrier's setter. This closes the scheduler context for a flip probe, where the body is straight-line and every barrier set is cleared by the instruction that set it.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Measured} over the tree's CUDA sources built by NVIDIA's compiler for sm\_\allowbreak{}86 (\texttt{monolith\_\allowbreak{}scheduler.\allowbreak{}md}, 72 cubins, 96976 instructions, each named by our reader and its bits 105 to 127 read through the krs's fields, no listing read): NVIDIA gives a write barrier at every place to \texttt{LDG}, \texttt{LDS.\allowbreak{}64}, \texttt{LDS.\allowbreak{}128}, \texttt{S2R}, \texttt{S2UR}, \texttt{F2I}, \texttt{I2F} and \texttt{ATOMG}, and at most places to \texttt{LDS}, \texttt{MUFU.\allowbreak{}RCP} and \texttt{F2I.\allowbreak{}FTZ}; to 94 other operations at none. A barrier counts its producers, and a wait on it holds until every one of them is back: of 1716 first reads of a late result with its own barrier, a wait on that barrier stands before all 1716, at the reader or at an instruction before it. A late result NVIDIA gives no barrier is held behind another of the same operation out of the same unit: 20 first reads, 16 of \texttt{LDS} and 4 of \texttt{MUFU.\allowbreak{}RCP}, stand behind a wait on a later one's barrier, the results coming back in the order they were put; 6 of \texttt{MUFU.\allowbreak{}RCP} stand behind a wait on an earlier one's barrier and at least 16 cycles past it. No first read of a late result stands behind no wait. Every \texttt{STG}, \texttt{STL}, \texttt{ST}, \texttt{STS} and \texttt{RED} takes a read barrier at most or every place. A fixed result is first read no sooner than 4 cycles on all 21 integer operations timed over 67443 reads, 5 on \texttt{IMAD.\allowbreak{}U32}, \texttt{IMAD.\allowbreak{}WIDE} and \texttt{IMAD.\allowbreak{}WIDE.\allowbreak{}U32.\allowbreak{}X}, and 6 on \texttt{CS2R}. The safe word waits on all six barriers at every instruction, which holds every one of these. Six barriers on every part from SM75 on, each a count its producers raise and a wait drains, and loads that keep their order sharing one barrier, are as the published reverse engineering of the scheduling word gives them (cited from knowledge: the ptxas execution model at gh.evko.io/crucible-notes, and the control codes of the maxas assembler, github.com/NervanaSystems/maxas/wiki/Control-Codes).
\end{itemize}
