\chapter{\texorpdfstring{Compression and tower recursion}{Compression and tower recursion}}
\label{chap:compression-tower}

\textbf{Purpose:} Explain, from first principles and with this engine's numbers, how a whole sample becomes one small exact file and comes back voxel for voxel. The tower recurses one exact step until the sample is one coefficient. The compression step writes what the tower leaves in as few bits as it needs. The CRC-64 is folded into the tower's own passes, and the proof that nothing was lost costs no pass of its own. Each of these had to be argued for before it was accepted, and each is explained thoroughly here so the next reader does not have to be argued into it.

\textbf{Scope:} anchor\_\allowbreak{}sift's \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}analysis/\allowbreak{}tower/\allowbreak{}}, \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}analysis/\allowbreak{}compression/\allowbreak{}}, \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}codecs/\allowbreak{}crc/\allowbreak{}}, the limit \texttt{ENGINE\_\allowbreak{}COEFFICIENT\_\allowbreak{}LIMIT} and the hand-off \texttt{Engine\allowbreak{}Stream} in \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}engine\_\allowbreak{}config\_\allowbreak{}requests.\allowbreak{}h}, and their composition in the entry, \texttt{src/\allowbreak{}engine/\allowbreak{}engine\_\allowbreak{}*.\allowbreak{}cu}. The proof was \texttt{cell\_\allowbreak{}tracking/\allowbreak{}maint/\allowbreak{}prove\_\allowbreak{}codec.\allowbreak{}sh} at d5f6a06. The ledger's statuses (\hyperref[chap:readme]{README.md}) apply to every claim. The tower is integral to the engine, and it is categorized \textbf{measure / recursion}.

Every number here is an exact integer. Where a share of raw is given, it is in per mille, rounded down: the driver prints it the same way, and its “40.6\%“ is 406 per mille rounded down, not a rounding to the nearest.

\section{\texorpdfstring{0. Why these ideas get resisted}{0. Why these ideas get resisted}}

Three defaults push against this codec.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Compression is supposed to lose something}, or to need a model of the data. This codec loses nothing and assumes nothing: every coefficient comes back exactly, and the only choice it makes (a block's Rice parameter) is read off that block's own values.
\item \textbf{A recursion is supposed to cost its depth in passes.} The tower's floors are a recursion, but each floor works only on the lows the floor below it left. For a 44b6 sample, the blocks of all eight floors together hold 447,397,412 lanes, 1,066 per mille of the sample's own voxels, and a check folded into the tower costs no pass at all.
\item \textbf{A test is supposed to cost its size.} Comparing a sample pixel for pixel is 419,430,400 comparisons. The same test, compressed in time, is one 64 bit equality.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{1. The tower: one exact step, recursed}{1. The tower: one exact step, recursed}}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{1.1 The step: the reversible integer 5/3 lift}{1.1 The step: the reversible integer 5/3 lift}}

Take one line of integers x₀, x₁, x₂, … along one axis. The step splits it into highs at the odd places and lows at the even places, in two moves, each of which adds to one half a function of the other half:

high   h\_\allowbreak{}j = x\_\allowbreak{}(2j+1) − ⌊(x\_\allowbreak{}(2j) + x\_\allowbreak{}(2j+2)) / 2⌋ low    l\_\allowbreak{}i = x\_\allowbreak{}(2i)   + ⌊(h\_\allowbreak{}(i−1) + h\_\allowbreak{}i + 2) / 4⌋

The edges are mirrored. Undoing it runs the same two moves backwards, with the signs turned:

x\_\allowbreak{}(2i)   = l\_\allowbreak{}i − ⌊(h\_\allowbreak{}(i−1) + h\_\allowbreak{}i + 2) / 4⌋ x\_\allowbreak{}(2j+1) = h\_\allowbreak{}j + ⌊(x\_\allowbreak{}(2j) + x\_\allowbreak{}(2j+2)) / 2⌋

\textbf{Why the floors do not break exactness.} Each move adds to one half a function of the \emph{other} half only. The inverse has that other half in hand, computes the very same function, floor and all, and subtracts it. It does not matter that ⌊·/2⌋ throws a bit away. The bit it throws away is thrown away identically going up and coming down, and it is never part of what is stored. This is a lifting step, and any function at all could stand in the floors' place: the pair of moves is still an exact integer map with an exact integer inverse. The 5/3 functions are chosen because a smooth line leaves highs near zero.

The high predicts each odd lane from its two even neighbors and keeps only the miss. The low then carries the line's local mean up to the next floor. What the prediction gets right is gone from the highs: the program generates it. What it misses stays: that is the residue.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{1.2 The recursion: floors until the whole sample is one coefficient}{1.2 The recursion: floors until the whole sample is one coefficient}}

The sample is one object over t z y x: time is an axis like the others, not a loop around them (noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}3's “temporal stacking”: samples enter at the bottom and time is written into the lattice). Each floor lifts every axis still longer than one, in turn. The lows, ⌈n / 2⌉ along each lifted axis, are the sub-block the next floor works on, and the highs stay where they landed. Floors rise until every axis is one long:

floor f + 1  =  lift( lows of floor f )

This is the recursion of noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower §9, Kₘ₊₁ = R(Kₘ, ξ), with R one step and the same step at every floor. An axis of length n reaches one after ⌈log₂ n⌉ halvings, the bit length of n − 1, and the tower has as many floors as its longest axis needs. For a 100 × 64 × 256 × 256 sample:

\textbf{What the recursion costs.} While all four axes are still longer than one, each floor's block is about a sixteenth of the one before it: every axis halves, rounded up. The blocks in the table sum to 419,430,400 + 26,214,400 + 1,638,400 + 106,496 + 7,168 + 512 + 32 + 4 = 447,397,412 lanes, 1,066 per mille of the sample. \textbf{What the tower holds.} Every floor's highs, and the one coefficient at the top. There are exactly as many coefficients as voxels (419,430,400 for a 44b6 sample), and the tower is a one-to-one map of the sample onto them. Nothing is merged, averaged or dropped. The coefficients can be wider than sixteen bits, and they are held below ENGINE\_\allowbreak{}COEFFICIENT\_\allowbreak{}LIMIT, 2\textasciicircum{}30 in magnitude (§2.4).

\textbf{Read back down.} \texttt{tower\_\allowbreak{}lower} undoes the floors from the top, each floor's axes in the reverse of the order they were lifted. The sample comes back voxel for voxel.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{1.3 Where the tower sits in the drafts, and where it does not}{1.3 Where the tower sits in the drafts, and where it does not}}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The drafts' floor −4 is not a floor of this tower.} The tower's floors count up from the sample (floor 1) to one coefficient (floor 8). The drafts' floor −4 is where the noise bits become irreducible, and in this engine that is the residue's measured floor (noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower §5). Bits 0 to 4 are set in about 46 of every 100 frames at nearly every voxel, and bits 0 to 3 flip in 499 to 500 of every 1,000 transitions in every window of 19 of the 25 samples. The tower holds that floor whole in its highs. It does not reach below it.
\item \textbf{The tower is not a key.} §3 of \hyperref[chap:keys-explained]{keys\_\allowbreak{}explained.md} collapses a chain of linear steps into one key by pushing the impulse through it once. The 5/3 lift is exact, but it is not linear. On the three lane line (0, 0, 1), the high is 0 − ⌊(0 + 1)/2⌋ = 0; on (1, 0, 0) it is 0 − ⌊(1 + 0)/2⌋ = 0; on their sum, (1, 0, 1), it is 0 − ⌊2/2⌋ = −1, not 0 + 0. The impulse's response therefore does not carry what the tower does to every input. The floors compose transitively as exact maps, but they do not imprint into one key.
\item \textbf{The melting phase.} noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}3 has the intermediate floors dissolve under the rule flash into one operation. The tower runs floor by floor today, four axes a floor. By the line above, the floors cannot be dissolved by imprinting them. Whether another exact route collapses them is open.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{2. Compression: writing the residue down, reading it back}{2. Compression: writing the residue down, reading it back}}

The tower hands its coefficients on the device to whatever writes them, and it writes no stream itself. The entry hands them to the compression step. Most coefficients are small, because the prediction took what the program generates and left only the misses. The compression step writes each one in about as many bits as it needs, and every one comes back exactly.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.1 Zigzag: signed to natural, exactly}{2.1 Zigzag: signed to natural, exactly}}

A coefficient v can be negative. Zigzag maps the integers one to one onto the naturals, interleaving the signs so that small magnitudes stay small:

v ≥ 0  →  2v          v < 0  →  2|v| − 1 0, −1, 1, −2, 2, …  →  0, 1, 2, 3, 4, …

Both arms are exact, because every coefficient is below 2\textasciicircum{}30 in magnitude, and so every zigzagged value is below 2\textasciicircum{}31 and fits 32 bits.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.2 Rice coding, a parameter per block of 64}{2.2 Rice coding, a parameter per block of 64}}

A natural n under the Rice parameter k is written in two parts. The first is its quotient q = n shifted right by k, in unary: q ones, then a zero. The second is its low k bits. It costs q + 1 + k bits. A small k suits a block of small values and a large k a block of large ones, and k is chosen per block of 64 values:

\begin{itemize}
\item The guess is the bit length of the block's mean (the sum over the count, rounded down).
\item The candidates run from the guess less two to the guess plus one: four candidates, or two or three where the guess is 0 or 1.
\item Each candidate's cost over the whole block is counted exactly, and the cheapest wins. A tie goes to the smaller k.
\item The chosen k is written in 5 bits, and any k from 0 to 31 can be named.
\end{itemize}

Nothing about the data is assumed. The parameter is read from the block's own values, and the cost that picks it is an exact count of bits. This is keys\_\allowbreak{}explained §7 (the noise is read, never modeled) applied to writing: no distribution is fitted, and a block of noise and a block of structure are coded by the same rule, each at its own cost.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.3 The escape at quotient 24}{2.3 The escape at quotient 24}}

A single large value in a block of small ones would cost its whole quotient in unary: at k = 0, a value near 2\textasciicircum{}31 would cost about 2\textasciicircum{}31 bits. A quotient reaching 24 is therefore written as 24 ones, with no zero after them, followed by the value's 32 bits. The reader sees 24 ones and knows 32 plain bits follow. So:

\begin{itemize}
\item no value costs more than 24 + 32 = 56 bits;
\item no block of 64 costs more than 5 + 64 × 56 = 3,589 bits;
\item the cost counted when k is chosen includes the escape exactly, and a block chooses its k knowing which of its values will escape.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.4 The limit, 2\textasciicircum{}30}{2.4 The limit, 2\textasciicircum{}30}}

ENGINE\_\allowbreak{}COEFFICIENT\_\allowbreak{}LIMIT is 2\textasciicircum{}30. It guarantees three things: every sum a lifting step forms stays inside a 64 bit integer's exact range; every coefficient fits a 32 bit integer; and its zigzag fits 32 bits, and the escape writes it whole. The tower flags any coefficient that reaches the limit, and a flagged sample is an error, never wrapped. The width is proved before anything is written, as keys\_\allowbreak{}explained §6 asks, and a sample that would break it is not written at all.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.5 Chunks: every part reachable, the whole decoded in parallel}{2.5 Chunks: every part reachable, the whole decoded in parallel}}

A Rice stream is serial as written: where value i + 1 starts depends on the length of value i. Read naively, decoding the last value means decoding every value before it. The chunks remove that.

\begin{itemize}
\item Blocks are grouped 64 to a chunk: 4,096 coefficients.
\item \textbf{Measure.} Each chunk's bits are counted first, one thread a chunk. The count is exact, and it is the same count that chose each block's k.
\item \textbf{Scan.} A scan sums the counts into each chunk's first bit. This is the prefix sum of the chunk lengths, exact in 64 bits.
\item \textbf{Write.} Each chunk is written from its first bit on its own thread. No chunk waits on another, because the scan has already said where each one starts.
\item \textbf{Read.} Decoding is the same: each chunk from its stored first bit, on its own thread.
\end{itemize}

The stream is limbs of 32 bits, bit b at bit b mod 32 of limb b / 32. The first bit of every chunk is kept beside it, 8 bytes a chunk. So any chunk, and with it any floor's coefficients at their known offsets, is reached without decoding anything before it. That is the fluidic draft's elevator (fluidic\_\allowbreak{}2; noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower §9 and §17): E(f, t) reads floor f at time t directly, and the clock takes you to any floor as fast as you can press the buttons.

The reader returns an error for a stream whose chunk count is not its coefficient count's, or whose chunks do not start inside it in order: that stream is not this data's.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{2.7 What the compression does not claim}{2.7 What the compression does not claim}}

It is not the floor of the data. Grouping each floor's coefficients row by row made the stream of 44b6\_\allowbreak{}0113de3b 1,157,533 bytes smaller, and coding pairs along t apart for each floor made it 1,987,962 bytes smaller, though pairing along t hurt on most other samples (ledger, 21 September). The stream as written is not the smallest one possible. What does bound it from below is the floor of §1.3. A bit plane at maximum entropy carries a full bit for every place it covers, and no exact coder writes it in fewer. That bound is standard (Shannon's). Whether the tower's highs inherit the floor's planes, plane for plane, has not been measured in this stream, and here the bound is theory.

\section{\texorpdfstring{3. The check folded in: the pixel-for-pixel test, compressed in time}{3. The check folded in: the pixel-for-pixel test, compressed in time}}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{3.1 Folded into the widen and the narrow}{3.1 Folded into the widen and the narrow}}

The tower has to widen the sample's sixteen bit voxels into 32 bit coefficients before it lifts anything, and it has to narrow the rebuilt coefficients back to sixteen bits when it is lowered. Each is a pass that touches every voxel once. The CRC-64/XZ rides both:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The widen} takes the CRC of the sample, each voxel its low byte and then its high byte, the way a .stack holds it.
\item \textbf{The narrow} takes the CRC of the rebuilt sample the same way, and flags any value outside sixteen bits, which no sample holds.
\end{itemize}

Neither is a pass of its own. This is the first standing rule, key:transform->product, 1 cycle (keys\_\allowbreak{}explained §5): the transform and the check are one product, taken at the same moment over the same voxels.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{3.2 Segments, joined pairwise by GF(2) advance operators}{3.2 Segments, joined pairwise by GF(2) advance operators}}

One register carried voxel by voxel would make the pass serial. So each thread takes the CRC of one segment of 64 voxels (128 bytes) from a zero register, and the segments are joined afterwards. The join rests on one fact: a CRC from zero is linear over GF(2). For a left part L and a right part R,

crc₀(L ‖ R)  =  A\_\allowbreak{}|R| · crc₀(L)  ⊕  crc₀(R)

where A\_\allowbreak{}|R| is the 64 × 64 bit matrix that carries a register across |R| zero bytes. The key in \texttt{crc\_\allowbreak{}key.\allowbreak{}h} holds A for 2\textasciicircum{}0, 2\textasciicircum{}1, …, 2\textasciicircum{}47 bytes: 48 operators of 64 columns of 8 bytes each, 24,576 bytes.

The join takes pairs from the right. The first segment is the short one, and every other segment at every level spans the same bytes, and one operator serves a whole level: level l carries registers across 2\textasciicircum{}(7 + l) bytes. For a 44b6 sample that is 6,553,600 segments joined in 23 levels, using operators 2\textasciicircum{}7 to 2\textasciicircum{}29. Then \texttt{crc\_\allowbreak{}finish} carries the all-ones start across the sample's 838,860,800 bytes, adds it, and inverts. The result is CRC-64/XZ exactly, as a byte by byte CRC would give it.

The key's reach bounds the message. The last operator the key holds is 2\textasciicircum{}47, reached at join level 40, and the join takes up to 2\textasciicircum{}41 segments of 128 bytes, and \texttt{crc\_\allowbreak{}finish} takes any length below 2\textasciicircum{}48 bytes. That is the “2\textasciicircum{}48 byte steps” of keys\_\allowbreak{}explained §4: past it, the join returns an error at that level in place of reaching for an operator the key does not hold.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{3.3 The accumulator stays one size}{3.3 The accumulator stays one size}}

However many segments are folded, the accumulator is 64 bits: over 64 voxels, over 419,430,400, or over the whole set, where each sample's CRC is carried on in the order the samples were named. That is the folding in hash\_\allowbreak{}boundary\_\allowbreak{}functional\_\allowbreak{}folding.md: an accumulator that stays one size however much is folded, and a severe boundary condition adds no dimensions (noise\_\allowbreak{}sieve\_\allowbreak{}tower §11). The fold carries the \emph{verification} of the work, never the work. Every voxel is still read once, in a pass that had to read it anyway, and the fold makes checking that read cost 64 bits.

Two samples with equal CRCs are equal pixel for pixel, short of a chance of 2\textasciicircum{}−64. The CRC is the pixel-for-pixel test compressed in time.

\section{\texorpdfstring{4. How the three compose}{4. How the three compose}}

Lift, encode, write; read, decode, lower. Each is exact and closed, and each hands the next a plain value: coefficients as a vector of exact integers, then an \texttt{Engine\allowbreak{}Stream}. They are composed only in the entry (keys\_\allowbreak{}explained §9). The codec is therefore one chain, and it composes transitively:

lower ∘ decode ∘ read ∘ write ∘ encode ∘ lift  =  identity on the sample

The CRC folded into the first pass and the last is that equation checked, at no cost of its own. \texttt{maint/\allowbreak{}prove\_\allowbreak{}codec.\allowbreak{}sh} ran it three ways after any change to the codec: every .kcr proved again from the file alone, with the set's CRC printed; one sample ingested again and compared byte for byte with the cached .kcr; and one sample's entropy history projected again and compared byte for byte.
