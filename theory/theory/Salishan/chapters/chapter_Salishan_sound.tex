\chapter{The recordings under the instrument}

\section{What the prosody field holds}

This chapter asks whether the prosody residual of a sound representation identifies the speaker, a
question the orior workbook \cite{src:Quigg-orior-workbook} leaves open, and reports what the
representation holds instead.

\textbf{Bev Phillips}, of Lytton First Nation, spoke all three of these recordings, in
nɬeʔkepmxcín, in the Lytton (ƛ̓q̓əmcín) dialect. \emph{When Old One Created the Earth}
\cite{src:Hall-and-Phillips-2025} is an original spíləx̣m of hers, written and told by her, and she
is an author of that paper and of \emph{You Will Be Sorry} \cite{src:Hall-and-Phillips-2024} with
Brent Hall. She chose the third story, \emph{The Moon and the Birchbark Canoe}
\cite{src:Givens-and-Hall-2023}, and helped translate it, and Katherine Givens and Brent Hall
transcribed and glossed it. The language is hers, and so are two of the three papers.

\subsection{The question}

The workbook states it and leaves it open: pitch against the speaker's own median is a per-speaker
constant subtracted, the center of mass again. What remains in that field is the residual, and
whether that residual identifies the speaker is the question.

The representation keeps prosody apart from the segment code deliberately, because a field made
shift invariant would put a stressed form and an unstressed one at one address. It holds four things
per 10 ms frame: loudness, pitch against the speaker's own median, how that pitch is moving, and
voicing. Each is thresholded at its own median over the recording. Each bit splits the frames in
half by construction, and what a distance between two recordings reads is the joint arrangement of
the four and never their levels.

\subsection{The answer, through the necessary condition}

Only one speaker is held, and there is no discrimination question to ask. The askable question is
the condition underneath it: a residual that identifies a speaker has to hold still within one.
Three recordings of Bev Phillips should then sit near the floor of the estimator.

A necessary condition cannot confirm identification, and its failure settles the matter. The thin
sample is therefore enough.

Every recording is trimmed to 23489 frames, the shortest, because a null taken at one length bounds
nothing about another. The floor is the recording split by alternating frames and measured against
itself, which samples it instead of cutting it in place. A midpoint cut would put the first half of a
narrative against the second and report the distance between two passages, the defect that inflates
every split-half distance of the corpus chapter by 1.35 to 3.64 times.

\textbf{The residual does not hold still within one speaker. It does not identify her.} One
person, one language, one dialect, and her three recordings sit nine to nineteen times the
resolution apart on the field built to hold what is left after her own pitch range is divided out.

\subsection{What that does not settle}

Three narratives in three ICSNL volumes vary together. The story, the session, the microphone and
the encode all move with the recording and nothing here separates them. The measurement shows that
the field does not hold still across them, and not which of those moved it.

The failure is also one directional in the useful way. Had the three sat at the floor, that would
have been consistent with the residual identifying her and consistent with the field holding nothing
at all, and a second speaker would have been needed to tell those apart. They do not sit at the
floor, and no second speaker is needed to read that.

\subsection{Who spoke is read from the paper}

The speaker of each recording is taken from what its paper says and never from its filename. The
filename of the ICSNL 58 recording names Givens and Hall, and the paper says the story was recounted
in Nɬeʔkepmxcín by Bev Phillips, who also provided much of the translation, with Givens and Hall
the linguists who transcribed and glossed it. A paper names its speaker in its prose, and a name put
on somebody's words on no other evidence is a wrong attribution.

The design follows from that reading. Two speakers with one pair each give a comparison with a one in
three ordering and no strength in it. One speaker across three recordings gives a necessary
condition, and a necessary condition that fails settles the question.

\section{The segment field is not comparable between recordings}

\subsection{What the representation does}

The representation rotates the segment measurement before thresholding it, onto the right singular
vectors of the centered measurement, and the measurement it is handed is one recording. Segment bit 3
of one recording and segment bit 3 of another are therefore different axes, and a distance between
two recordings' segment profiles is partly a distance between two rotations.

The rotation exists because neighboring bands rise and fall together, and thresholding them as they
come gives bits that restate each other. The basis it produces belongs to the recording.

\subsection{A comparison that does not isolate the rotation}

Comparing two halves of one recording, which share a rotation, against two whole recordings, which do
not, gives ratios of 8.8 to 1.6 across the widths and looks like a confirmation.

It is not one. The prosody field is never rotated and gives 18.6 by the same measure. Two recordings
differ for every other reason as well, and a check both arms pass is measuring something both arms
have.

\subsection{What isolates it}

One recording, cut in half once, and the two halves compared twice. In the first arm the
representation is computed on the whole recording and the frames are cut afterwards, and one
rotation computed over both halves is used for both. In the second, each half of the samples is represented
alone and carries a rotation computed over itself. The audio is identical and every other step is
identical. The gap is the rotation.

recordings is reading their bases.} The field is not comparable across recordings without a shared
rotation, and no segment distance between recordings is reported here as a reading of speech. A
comparison of two recordings' segment fields has to fit one rotation over both first.

\subsection{Why the prosody field escapes it}

It is not rotated: its four columns are each a named thing, and rotating them would mix pitch back
into the field the segment code was made shift invariant to get away from. Loudness, pitch against
the speaker's own median, the movement of that pitch and voicing mean the same thing in every
recording.

The field that can carry the workbook's question is therefore the comparable one, and the answer it
gives is negative.

\section{The waveform itself, with nothing thrown away}

\subsection{Why the representation cannot answer this}

The representation reduces a recording to 28 bits every 10 ms and discards the rest. It takes an
envelope, smooths the harmonic comb off it, keeps 24 coefficients, rotates them onto that
recording's own singular vectors, and cuts every column at its median. Each of those is a bound, and
a number taken through a bound describes the choice of bound as well as the data.

The decoded waveform has no such choice in it. All three recordings are written out at full integer
width, in order, 215 MB of samples, and every reading below is taken off that. The bytes are checked
against the corpus's signed audit first, because a measurement that moves for no reason is to be
blamed on the bytes before the method.

\subsection{A linear slice reads the silence}

Reading the top \(w\) bits of each sample returns \(H_2(2)\) equal to \(H_2(1)\), and \(H_2(16)\) at
5.04 bits, far under what a speech waveform should carry.

The recordings say why. \emph{When Old One Created the Earth} peaks at 16954 of 32768 and its median
absolute sample is \textbf{6}. Half of a twenty-two minute narrative sits inside thirteen levels of a
sixty-five thousand level converter, because a told story is mostly the quiet between words. The top
bits are sign extension: at two bits they take two values with the commonest holding 0.549 of the
samples.

A linear slice of a waveform spends its levels where the signal is not. The workbook records the
same defect four separate ways, with the correction.

Two seatings are therefore reported. Linear over the range each recording uses, whose \(H_2\) curve
is a fact about the amplitude distribution. And quantile, which puts an equal share of samples in
every symbol, so that \(H_2\) comes out at \(w\) by construction. The second is the point: the
marginal is flattened to maximum entropy and every state is reachable. What is left to read
afterward is arrangement and not loudness. It is the same subtraction the per-run test makes on two
dialects.

\subsection{Where the arrangement sits}

The block shuffle keeps everything shorter than its span and destroys everything longer. Sweeping
the span locates the structure instead of only detecting it. The workbook builds this null on text;
it needs recordings of tens of minutes to apply to a waveform. These are 3.9, 13.9 and 22.5.

Read on the quantile seating at eight bits, live against its own shuffle:

\subsection{There is no unit scale in it, and that absence is the result}

The obvious reading of that table is a knee somewhere near the word, and the text of one of these
recordings is held to check it against: 1736 words over 1350 seconds is 0.778 seconds a word,
arrived at with no reference to the audio.

Swept finely between a phone and six seconds, there is no knee. The excess over the shuffle decays
smoothly and fits a power law in the span:

\[
\text{excess} \;\propto\; \text{span}^{-0.752}, \qquad r^2 = 0.988
\]

over a 64-fold range of spans, with every one of eleven points landing between 0.88 and 1.19 of the
fit. Nothing happens at 0.778 seconds. Nothing happens anywhere.

\textbf{The waveform's arrangement has no characteristic scale.} The absence is itself the
measurement, and it is the case the workbook names: data carrying structure at every scale has no
characteristic scale for a window, a band or a ceiling to sit at. What holds still for such data is
how the quantity changes with scale, and here that is an exponent measured to \(r^2 = 0.988\).

\subsection{What that settles about reading speech back}

\textbf{Detecting that a recording is speech is available and is not the interesting half.} The
departure from the null runs 363 to 684 at sample scale and is still 1.07 at six seconds. Nothing
this large needs a careful instrument. Most of it is the amplitude envelope, which is to say the
silence between words, and a measure reading that is reading that a person paused.

\textbf{Transcription is not available from this reading, and the reason is structural.} Writing down
what was said requires knowing where one unit ends and the next begins. The method finds a boundary
in text by a symbol whose gaps are far tighter than geometric \cite{src:Quigg-delta-null}, and the
whole of that construction rests on there being a scale where the regularity sits. This waveform has
no such scale. A power law with no knee supplies no unit boundary at any span. There is nothing to
segment on and nothing to write down.

The same obstacle stands from the other side: the null for animal vocalization has to keep each call
intact while permuting the order of calls. That null requires fixing the unit, and the unit is the
thing being asked for. The block shuffle is the closest available substitute and it returns no unit.

\textbf{Dialect cannot be asked at all.} The audio held here is one speaker, one language, one
dialect: Bev Phillips, nɬeʔkepmxcín, Lytton. There is no second dialect to discern from the first,
and a summed reference over one dialect is that dialect. The dialect question has an answer in this
work and it is on the text side, where the published labels of Mellesmoen and Kye
\cite{src:Mellesmoen-and-Kye-2026} supply a border the algorithm never sees. Asking it of sound needs
recordings carrying a border somebody else labeled, and the corpus holds none.

\section{The join between the web and the sound}

\subsection{The missing stage}

The chapter \emph{From the page to a distribution over sounds} states the three stages and says
which one is absent: the word web is built on written forms, and nothing takes a character out of the
text space and hands back what it stands for. Two dialects writing one word two ways are still two
nodes.

The section above looks for the unit in the waveform alone and finds a power law with no knee. That
is the middle of the three ways a partition is fixed, an estimate, and an estimate reaches nothing
past what the sample already carries. The third way adds information the sample does not hold, and
it is the only one that does.

\subsection{The supervision is printed on the page}

Hall and Phillips \cite{src:Hall-and-Phillips-2025} write that there is no standardized punctuation
convention for nɬeʔkepmxcín, and instead of periods they separate each sentence of the transcription
with a timestamp into the recording. The hand extraction carries those timestamps in its gloss
column.

That is \textbf{126 sentences of Bev Phillips's own spíləx̣m, each carrying the interval of audio it
was spoken in and the exact letters she said}, and no algorithm produced any of it. It sits in the
gloss column of a table checked against its paper in both directions.

It is checked before it is used: the timestamps run forward, the last is 1336 s against a 1350 s
recording, and the 126 sentences cover all of it. A sentence runs 2 to 40 seconds, median 10.

\subsection{A segment is a grapheme cluster and not a character}

The segments are formed by the clustering rule of the chapter \emph{From the page to a distribution
over sounds}: every mark and modifier joined to the base in front of it. On this paper that gives
\textbf{4839 segments over 50 distinct}. \texttt{ə́} is one of the fifty, at 45 instances, the
segment that carries the Lushootseed border.

\subsection{Placing a segment inside its sentence, and why the crude answer is allowed}

A timestamp bounds a sentence and not a segment. The segments inside one are placed evenly, which is
wrong in detail, since a vowel is longer than a stop and nothing here knows that.

The comparison it is held against allows it. The null permutes which letter is attached to which
interval and leaves the rest unchanged. Every interval keeps its position, its length and its audio,
and the even spacing sits in both arms exactly. An error in the placement costs both arms the same
and cannot manufacture a result. It also bounds the finding: what reads as holding together is
holding together at the resolution even spacing allows.

\subsection{What the pooled sound gives}

Each interval becomes the loudness heard in 64 log spaced bands, normalized so that a loud instance
and a quiet one of one segment are one shape. A segment type's sound is the mean over its instances,
and that is their sum. Thirty-four of the fifty types carry at least 24 instances.

Two things have to be read off that together, and reading either alone gets it wrong.

\textbf{The alignment is real.} Nine of two hundred shuffles reach what the letters a person wrote
down give. The letters and the audio are not independent, and the only thing joining them is a
timestamp printed in a paper.

\textbf{The letters do not separate.} Between over within is 0.214, which is to say a segment's own
instances scatter about five times further than one segment's mean sits from another's. A
representation that recovered a letter from its sound would have that the other way up. This one does
not, at this resolution.

The join exists and carries something, and it is nowhere near reading a letter off the sound. The
stage between written form and sound is measured, and the number on it is small.

\subsection{What it needs next, which the measurement names}

Even placement is the binding limit and it is fixable without giving up the supervision. The letters
would still come from the hand extraction and only the boundaries would come from the audio, and the
null would still permute labels over whatever boundaries were found. The control does not weaken. A
sentence of ten seconds holding thirty-eight segments gives each one 0.26 s where a stop is nearer
0.05. Most intervals straddle a boundary or land in silence. That is where the scatter is.

What must not be done is to let the audio choose the letters. The timestamps and the transcription are
everything that makes this supervision and not an estimate, and an alignment tuned until the letters
separate would be the algorithm scoring itself against a split it proposed, a test that passes
whatever the data.
