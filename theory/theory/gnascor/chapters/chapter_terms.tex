\chapter{Terms}
\label{ch:terms}

The language's keywords are its own and are kept. Each entry below gives the field's term for
the same concept and its source. Terms with no keyword are listed where this research paper uses
them in a narrower sense than the field does.

\section{States}

\noindent\textbf{DUAL.} Both sides answer inside the timeout. Active-active operation of a
redundant pair.

\noindent\textbf{LEAD, RITE.} One side answers, the left or the right. Active-passive operation,
the serving side named.

\noindent\textbf{VOID.} Neither side answers. An outage; in a probe, both probes failed.

\noindent\textbf{BUSY.} Both sides answer, each dearer than every held ask of the baseline.
Saturation, one of the four golden signals of monitoring \cite{src:SRE}.

\noindent\textbf{WAIT.} One side answers and the other answers past the timeout. Latency, the
second golden signal \cite{src:SRE}.

\noindent\textbf{BLOK.} A side refuses or ends its asker. Errors, the third golden signal
\cite{src:SRE}.

\noindent\textbf{GRAY.} A side not asked: no answer, as against VOID, an answer of no. Belnap's
value None, told nothing, in his four-valued logic for a question-answering machine
\cite{src:Belnap}.

\noindent\textbf{SYNC.} A superstate standing for every transition between a load state and a
binary one, entered by FUZZ carrying the state left and left by FIZZ carrying the state reached.
Harel's superstate \cite{src:Harel}.

\section{Transitions}

\noindent\textbf{Mnemonic, label.} The output written on a transition of a Mealy machine, a
function of the state left and the input that moved it \cite{src:Mealy}.

\noindent\textbf{NEXUS, CORE, SURV, ZERO, DRAG, HOLD, DEAD.} Self-loops: a state held from one
cycle to the next.

\noindent\textbf{PASS, BACK.} Failover and failback, the serving side changing from left to right
and back.

\noindent\textbf{DROP.} A stable state lost to VOID.

\noindent\textbf{JAMM.} Saturation turning into refusal. Gridlock.

\noindent\textbf{HALT.} Any state reaching a refusal.

\noindent\textbf{LOSS.} A lagging side timing out into an outage.

\noindent\textbf{SPRK.} From VOID straight to DUAL. Recovery; the trial request of a half-open
circuit breaker succeeding \cite{src:Nygard}.

\noindent\textbf{WAKE, JOIN.} One side returning from VOID; a second side joining one already
serving.

\noindent\textbf{SURG, SNAP.} Saturation clearing into full service; a lagging side catching up.

\noindent\textbf{VENT.} A saturated pair dropping one side. Load shedding.

\noindent\textbf{TILT.} Full service decaying to one side by a fraction of a cycle's lag.

\noindent\textbf{FUSE.} Both sides lost at once with no decay. A tripped circuit breaker
\cite{src:Nygard}.

\noindent\textbf{FUZZ, FIZZ.} Entry into and exit from a superstate, GRAY or SYNC.

\noindent\textbf{Coherence clock.} The stream of states and labels. A health status stream.

\section{The protocol}

\noindent\textbf{Ask.} An address and a predicate, returning a cost unbound and a bit bound. A
health probe with a timeout \cite{src:Kubernetes-probes}.

\noindent\textbf{Unbound pass, baseline.} Costs measured with no timeout and their least, spread
and mean. A benchmark baseline, ranked on its minimum \cite{src:Chen-Revels}.

\noindent\textbf{Derived timeout.} The timeout read off the baseline. An adaptive timeout, of the
form of TCP's retransmission timeout \cite{src:RFC6298}.

\noindent\textbf{Watchdog stop.} An ask stopped at a limit before it reported its cost. A
right-censored observation \cite{src:Kaplan-Meier}.

\noindent\textbf{Spurious timeout.} A correct answer arriving past the timeout.

\noindent\textbf{Gate, rank.} Feasibility decided on exact predicates, then cost ordered among the
feasible. A lexicographic objective; filter then rank.

\noindent\textbf{Known order, slicing.} Asks over covering sets of links in the order of an
S-matrix. A Hadamard weighing design \cite{src:Hotelling, src:Sloane-Harwit}.

\noindent\textbf{Contention.} A set of links costing more than the sum of its parts. A quadratic
interaction term.

\noindent\textbf{Pooled ask.} One bit for a set of checks. Group testing
\cite{src:Dorfman, src:Ungar}.

\noindent\textbf{Reference compiler.} Holding the language's code against a vendor compiler's on
every input. Differential testing \cite{src:McKeeman}.

\noindent\textbf{Turn count.} A counter kept inside a loop body that bounds it. A loop variant
\cite{src:Floyd}.

\noindent\textbf{Floor.} The noise floor of a measurement, written to the language's
\texttt{.knf} view.

\section{Information}

\noindent\textbf{Information is coherence.} The language's principle that friction measures
information content. Kolmogorov complexity \cite{src:Kolmogorov} read through the minimum
description length principle \cite{src:Rissanen}; by H1, closer to logical depth
\cite{src:Bennett}.

\noindent\textbf{Kept bits.} \(H(\text{pair}) - H(\text{pair}\mid\text{label})\), the mutual
information between a transition and its label \cite{src:Shannon}.

\noindent\textbf{Critical path.} The longest chain of dependent steps \cite{src:Kelley-Walker}.
