\chapter{Where it stops}

This chapter states the boundary of the claim. Nothing here is quoted past what it supports.

\section{Right angles only}

Every entry read has a cell with right angles. Oblique cells are skipped, not measured and reported as failures. A shift along a coordinate is a shift along a crystal axis only when the axes are orthogonal, and on an oblique cell the coordinates would have to be sheared first.

That is a real restriction on coverage. It restricts which crystals are read and claims nothing about which crystals could be. Whether the same detector reads a monoclinic or triclinic cell after a shear is untested here.

\section{Two atoms are two atoms}

On a grid of 0.25 angstroms two atoms can land on the same voxel, and the later one overwrites the earlier with nothing recovering the lost site. That is harmless for the lattice period, since a period is a property of the repeating arrangement and not of any particular site, but it means the grid is not the structure.

Exact coordinates collide only where a deposit puts two sites at the same place. Nothing is lost that the deposit does not already carry.

\section{Exact does not mean true}

The exact reading recovers the number a deposit carries and stops there. A cell edge written as 4.76050(5) has an uncertainty of five in its last place, fixed by the people who measured the crystal, and dropping that bracket is all this work does with it. Agreeing with a deposit to the digit is agreement with a deposit.

What the exactness rules out is a second uncertainty underneath the first. The instrument contributes nothing to the error. Whatever error remains is the deposit's and can be argued about on its own terms.

\section{The detector is not the measure}

The work carries two instruments, and reporting one as the other is a standing source of confusion. The crystal result belongs to the shift agreement detector, the same one that returns an image width from a byte sequence and a key length from a Vigenère cipher. It is the instrument with external ground truth.

The null permutation identity~\cite{src:Quigg-orior-workbook}, which carries most of the findings elsewhere in the work, has no external positive control and does not read these cells. A crystal row does not transfer to it. Quoting the crystal agreement as evidence that the departure measure works moves a result between two instruments that share no part of their construction.

\section{What a cell edge is worth here}

Nothing to crystallography. The cells are published to more decimal places than this reaches, by refinement procedures built for the purpose, and a cell recovered from a deposited cell is a round trip and not a new measurement.

What it is worth is the direction of the evidence. An instrument that finds a period nobody told it about, on hundreds of axes whose answers were written down in advance by other people, is shown to find structure that is there. A memoryless control can only show the instrument does not invent structure that is not. Those are different claims and only one of them can be earned with a memoryless control.

\section{The open question}

The detector reads a periodicity. A crystal is the case where periodicity is exact and known. It is the easiest possible instance and it says nothing about the hard one: how far the period has to degrade before the detector stops finding it. Disorder, thermal displacement, a superlattice, an incommensurate modulation and a quasicrystal are all cases where the answer is interesting and none of them is measured here.

That experiment is worth running against published structures where the degree of disorder is itself published, which keeps the answer somebody else's number.
