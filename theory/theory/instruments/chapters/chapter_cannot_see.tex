\chapter{What the instruments cannot see}

This chapter states what is measured about SHA-256 and the block header, at what scope, and which instruments cannot see what they are pointed at, because a battery of null results is worth only the sensitivity of the battery.

One finding outweighs every null result about SHA-256 here: \textbf{the digits of pi pass every test in the battery, and the digits of pi have a generating program of about a kilobyte.}

That is not a remark about pi. The battery checks for uniform bit shares, flat autocorrelation, full algebraic degree, no co-variation between output positions, and incompressibility to LZMA. A sequence with a short generating program sails through all of it. Reporting \emph{no structure found} on the strength of it says less than it appears to.

\section{Five instrument faults}

All five run in the same direction, each producing an apparent finding, and each is detected by a result being too clean and not by care beforehand.

\textbf{A statistic read off the top of a sort.} The deepest timestamp reversals appear to cluster near \(280\) seconds. They are the eight largest values of a sorted list, which are close together by construction. The full population shows no band.

\textbf{Three noise floors derived instead of drawn.} A shuffle of hour \emph{labels} returns identical counts every draw and prints a standard deviation of \(0.00\). A per-pool phase bar set at two Poisson floors, for a statistic that is a maximum minus a minimum over twenty-four bins and spans three and a half to four and a half by construction, sits below chance: every pool clears it, and twenty random trough hours read as geography. A window-scatter floor from chi-square's variance carried through a logarithm gives \(0.0582\) where shuffling gives \(0.1015\).

\textbf{A generator with a period.} Survey arms built as \(\mathit{at} \times (2\,\mathit{arm} + 37) + 11 + 97\,\mathit{arm} \bmod 256\) are linear in the arm index, and both terms wrap together every \(256\) arms: arm \(0\) and arm \(256\) produce byte-identical headers. Nine hundred thirty one files hold two hundred fifty six distinct arms, up to four copies each, and the duplication inflates the summed squared \(z\) from \(256\) to \(942\). That reads as a thirty sigma discovery and reproduces the duplication factor to within one percent. Hashing each header from its arm index, instead of stepping it, removes the period.

\textbf{A test whose answer is geometry.} Reading a state through a mirrored helix appears to test the handedness of SHA-256, which is chiral by design: six rightward rotations and no leftward one. But reflecting a point set negates every longitude, conjugating every coefficient and negating every phase, for any data whatever. The result is fixed before the data is consulted and comes back at \(10^{-16}\), which is float64's floor.

\textbf{A scalar standing in for a structure.} Per-degree power is invariant under all of \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\), and reporting it alone implies that magnitude and phase separate cleanly. They separate only on rotations about the reading axis. Under a general rotation the coefficients mix, magnitude redistributes across orders inside a degree, and the summed power is preserved because that mixing cancels and not because nothing moved. Summing over orders makes the mixing invisible.

\section{What is established, at its real scope}

\textbf{On SHA-256, bounded and not proven flat.} Across \(550\) billion evaluations spanning \(256\) genuinely distinct headers, the output bits are individually unbiased to about four parts in a million and carry no co-variation a permutation null cannot produce. A Walsh-Hadamard sweep over all \(262{,}144\) input masks per output bit, the linear approximations of linear cryptanalysis~\cite{src:Matsui-1994}, finds no linear approximation surviving on nonces it did not see; every in-sample maximum sits below the null's own expected maximum. The algebraic degree in the nonce bits saturates by round eight, and no transform has fewer coefficients than points to ride. Reversing every rotation gives a function statistically identical to the standard one~\cite{src:FIPS-180-4}: avalanche~\cite{src:Webster-and-Tavares-1986} means differ by a third of a standard error, and both reach half the output at round eighteen. The avalanche criterion over every combination of the seven sub-functions is measured in~\cite{src:Vaughn-and-Borowczak-2026}, which finds no attack arising from combining them. Handedness is cosmetic, and the rotation amounts carry the diffusion.

Every one of those is a bound with a domain, and not an absolute. A finite observation of an unbounded input space can bound an effect and can never establish absence.

\textbf{On the block header, a real and large signal.} The sixteen bits BIP320~\cite{src:BIP320} reserves for version rolling, which the Stratum extension of BIP310~\cite{src:BIP310} negotiates, give away \(1.82\) bits of collision entropy, \(588\) times the floor chance produces at that width and depth, and \(45.7\) percent of what the field could carry. Per-bit shares run from \(-5\) to \(-51\) standard errors against a swept counter's prediction, and the whole distribution is bimodal with spikes on exact powers of two: conventions, and not a sweep. The leak also moves, at \(p = 0.010\) against a shuffle null, which reads as software being deployed and retired inside a forty eight day window.

\textbf{On the clocks, a physical effect cleanly separated.} Timestamps in \(2.98\) percent of blocks precede their own parent's, by up to \(395\) seconds. The clocks tick correctly: low-bit shares reach \(3.10\) against a calibrated null of \(3.35\), and residues mod ten give chi-square \(9.9\) on nine degrees of freedom. The mining process is a clean Poisson at \(\mathrm{sd}/\mathrm{mean} = 0.9879\). The effect is therefore offset, and neither drift nor granularity, and the magnitudes are four orders past anything NTP produces.

\textbf{And the separation that ties them together.} The tilt lives in the fields miners \emph{choose} and is absent from the field the function \emph{produces}. Across \(178\) testable digest positions, conditioned per block on its own target, the loudest reads \(+2.78\) standard errors against a \(3.22\) bar. Same eighty bytes, same blocks, same miners.

\section{What has no instrument}

A one-way function is the gap between a description of about a thousand bits and a search costing \(2^{48}\), and nobody has proven one exists. SHA-256's hardness is conjectural. Every measurement above assumes the gap and confirms its local consequences, which is circular, and the pi control exposes the circle.

Kolmogorov complexity~\cite{src:Kolmogorov-1965} is uncomputable, and no tool decides it. A compressor gives an upper bound: compression proves structure, failure to compress proves nothing, because a compressor hunts repetition and a generating program is not repetition. Measured directly, pi compresses to \(1.0010\) against random's \(1.0001\). The positive control fails completely, and any claim of the form \emph{it does not compress, therefore no structure} is a statement about zlib.

What separates pi from noise is neither distribution nor compression. It is reproducibility: an independent method computes the same digits, and agreement between two programs sharing no arithmetic demonstrates a generating program. That is a predictive test, and the predictive test for a hash is the linear approximation search, the one axis above that pi would fail.

\section{Reading the interior instead of the boundary}

Every measurement above reads a digest, the boundary of the computation. That is the right shape for a physical system, where the interior cannot be reached and has to be inferred from what escapes. It is the wrong shape here, because nothing about this interior is hidden. The schedule words, the eight state words at every round, and the six values built inside a round are all readable at every instant. Those six are \(s_1\), \(\mathit{ch}\), \(t_1\) on one chain and \(s_0\), \(\mathit{maj}\), \(t_2\) on the other. Nothing forces a boundary reading here.

Read that way, one flipped input bit shows something the digest cannot.

The nonlinear functions lag the linear ones by a round, and the lag persists: at round seventeen the linear values sit at \(15.5\) to \(16.0\) bits of difference while Choose and Majority sit at \(14.3\) and \(14.8\).

The mechanism is in their definitions. Choose is \((e \wedge f) \oplus (\neg e \wedge g)\). Where \(f\) and \(g\) \emph{agree} at a bit position the result does not depend on \(e\) there at all. Majority cannot be moved by one of three inputs either. The nonlinear functions therefore absorb the first arrival and pass it on only once a second input has been reached. A digest sums all six paths into one number and cannot say which carried what.

\section{The rules, which are a different kind of statement}

Everything above is a frequency, and a frequency needs a null, a bar and an argument. The support does not. For a single-bit input difference, an output position either moves or it never does, and a position that never moves in six hundred excitations is not rare. It is forbidden, and every trajectory requiring it is ruled out outright.

At round seven, \(214\) of \(256\) positions are forbidden to a single input bit. The support grows about nine positions a round after the third, putting the grammar's closing near round thirty, and that is consistent with the avalanche independently reaching \(126\) bits moved by round twenty. The function therefore spends roughly its first thirty rounds before every input can reach every output, and its remaining thirty-four past the point where the support constrains anything.

This is the sharpest result here and the only one needing no statistics to stand up. Its limit is equally sharp: forbidden transitions prune trajectory \emph{searches}, as differential cryptanalysis does. Mining evaluates forward and searches no trajectories. The grammar does not help there.

\section{Two response measurements and a lead, read correctly}

The faults here share a shape with the five above.

\textbf{Susceptibility does not clear.} The loudest cell of the real \(512 \times 256\) table reads \(-4.33\) standard errors against a null table's loudest of \(-4.11\). A maximum over \(131{,}072\) cells is itself a random variable with real spread, and one draw of it is not a bar. Calling that a clearance is a derived threshold set too low.

\textbf{The pair response has no power at all.} Flipping two input bits and comparing against the exclusive-or of the two single differences gives \(128.012\) bits, and the null arm gives \(127.938\). Both are pinned at \(128\), which is half of \(256\) and therefore complete decorrelation. The statistic is saturated in both arms and cannot discriminate. Calling the excess \emph{the nonlinearity} implies it carries information, and it does not.

\textbf{A lead that dissolves at depth.} At \(1{,}200\) samples the per-bit batch-to-batch swing has a loudest of \(1.394\) with the top swingers uniformly cold, which looks like a second channel invisible to a heat map. At \(60{,}000\) samples the null arm's own loudest is \(1.3965\), no bit clears it, and the correlation between swing and heat is \(-0.0671\) inside a band of \(0.1250\). The anticorrelation is the artifact, and no signal remains.

\section{The one measurement that can return a discovery}

Everything above returns a bound or a null. The relation search returns a statement: a closed form either holds or it does not, and anyone can check it in an afternoon without repeating the work.

Its control is the 1996 BBP formula for pi~\cite{src:Bailey-Borwein-and-Plouffe-1997}. Pointed at base sixteen, power one, stride eight, the pipeline returns an exact relation, and the known formula holds on the same values to \(895\) bits of \(900\), the remainder being the series truncation. The relation is not unique: Bailey's compendium~\cite{src:Bailey-2023} lists the zero relations, BBP-type sums that evaluate to zero, and notes that an integer relation search returns one unless they are excluded. The exact relations form a lattice, and the known formula is one member. Demanding a particular member is the wrong control and reports a correct answer as a failure.

One region is closed and should not be searched. Borwein, Galway and Borwein~\cite{src:Borwein} prove that pi has no Machin-type BBP arctangent formula in any base \(b \neq 2\) that is not a proper power, and they know of no constant lacking a Machin-type formula that has a BBP formula of any other form.
