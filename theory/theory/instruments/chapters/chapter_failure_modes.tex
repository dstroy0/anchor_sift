\chapter{Failure modes, and the subtraction that removes each one}

This chapter holds the apparatus to the same subtraction the data is held to. It lists fifteen failure modes, each with an instance, the detector that removes it and the detector's status.

This is not a checklist. It is a sieve, and the operation is subtractive: each detector removes a class of explanation for a number, and what is left after every subtraction is the residual. The residual is the finding. That is the same operation as the sigma-delta null~\cite{src:Quigg-delta-null}, measurement minus what a null would have given, applied to the apparatus and not to the data.

Every instance of all fifteen modes makes a result look better than the truth. None errs toward a weaker finding. That asymmetry is itself the strongest reason to run the sieve instead of trusting a number.

Status is one of \textbf{built}, where a mechanical detector exists and runs over every measurement; \textbf{partial}, where a detector exists but is applied only where a defect is already suspected; and \textbf{open}, where the mode is known and nothing yet catches it.

\section{Absence read as measurement (built)}

Measuring a quantity that structurally cannot depend on the input, and reporting the resulting zero or perfect agreement as a result.

\textbf{Instances.} Rotating the nonce, which enters at \texttt{W[3]} in round three, instead of the chaining value, reports a decay that never happens. Measuring through \texttt{W[17]}, which does not depend on \texttt{W[3]}, reports \(100.000\%\) agreement, which is zero equaling zero. Labeling rounds 1 to 3 \emph{premise holds: no} is a verdict on a nonce that has not entered yet.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Exact reachability, computed and not sampled. Every state bit carries the set of input bits that can reach it, propagated through the real operations: rotation permutes positions, shift drops them, bitwise operations union bit by bit, and addition unions upward through the carry. Addition is the only widening operation, making this an over-approximation. A path may carry no influence, but \textbf{the absence of a path is exact}, the correct side to err on for a precondition.

\textbf{What it settles.} The nonce reaches no state bit until four rounds have run, and saturates both written words the moment it arrives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
rounds run & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 \\
\midrule
bits of \texttt{state[0]} reachable from \texttt{W[3]} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 32 & 32 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In the schedule, \texttt{W[16]} and \texttt{W[17]} carry \textbf{zero} bits of \texttt{W[3]} while \texttt{W[18]} carries all \(32\), correctly, since \texttt{W[18]} is the first word whose \texttt{W[t-15]} term is \texttt{W[3]}. Both of the first two instances are visible directly in those two tables, before the measurement instead of after it.

\textbf{The control, run in the opposite direction.} An analysis claiming reachability everywhere would pass an absence test trivially. Both halves are needed. Over \(24{,}000\) random flips at rounds one to six:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
pairs the analysis called unreachable & \(21{,}257\) \\
of those, a flip that changed the bit & \textbf{0} \\
pairs the analysis called reachable & \(2{,}743\) \\
of those, a flip that landed & \(35.07\%\) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

One violation in the first row falsifies the analysis outright. The second row has to sit away from both extremes, and does.

\textbf{A structural fact that comes free.} Every one of the \(256\) state bits reads all \(768\) input bits from \textbf{round 21} on, the widest cone growing \(256\), \(320\), \(352\), \(384\), \(416\), thirty-two bits per round after the first two, which is one schedule word arriving each round. That number is exact.

\section{Hand-derived null (partial)}

Comparing a measurement against an analytical reference derived by hand, where the derivation is wrong.

\textbf{Instances.} An expected worst of \(3840\) cells gives a \(5.47\) sigma keyhole that falls to \(4.19\) and then to nothing at \(64\times\) the samples. Overshoot modeled as geometric in nibbles produces a \(7.27\times\) excess; the target is not on a nibble boundary and the true figure is \(1.0076\). A max-statistic compared against a per-bin uniform produces \(775{,}000\times\) where the permutation null gives \(184.5\times\), inflated by about \(4{,}200\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} \(\Delta_\Sigma[M] = M(x) - \mathbb{E}_{\pi \sim \Pi_\Sigma}[M(\pi(x))]\). A permutation null cannot be derived wrongly because it preserves the carrier's own counts by construction and not by arithmetic.

\textbf{Status.} Built and applied to one statistic. The keyhole scan's expected worst and the corpus-size ratios are both hand-derived and neither is checked.

\section{Asymmetric treatment of the two arms (open)}

Applying one operation to one member of a pair and a different operation to the other, leaving the difference in treatment inside the measured difference.

\textbf{Instance.} Adding \(K\) to one member and \(\mathrm{rotr}(K)\) to the other produces a \(25.8\%\) survival that appears to overturn a measured zero. The same constant to both gives \(0.000000\%\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} A swap test. Exchange the two arms and re-run. A statistic meant to be symmetric must return the same number; one deliberately asymmetric must say so and say why.

\section{Reading more output than the operation writes (built)}

\textbf{Instance.} Measuring all eight output words when a SHA-256 round writes only \texttt{state[0]} and \texttt{state[4]}. The six untouched words agree trivially and the result reads \(P = 1.00000000\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} A write mask, computed beside the read set, since it is the same question about what the circuit does. Every output word is assumed a copy of every input word, and each of \(2000\) random trials removes the pairings that failed; what is left after every trial is carried instead of written.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
rounds run & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\
\midrule
words written & 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\
words carried & 6 & 4 & 2 & 0 \\
share of an eight-word read that agrees for free & \textbf{75\%} & 50\% & 25\% & 0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{An eight-word read after one round agrees on three quarters of its comparison before the round function is consulted.} Reading only the written words needs no correction factor and no argument. It is the right read.

\section{Sample below the detectable effect (open)}

Running an experiment whose expected count under the hypothesis is far below one, and reporting the absence of an effect as evidence.

\textbf{Instance.} \(k = 8, 12, 16\) at \(400\) states, where \(k = 16\) predicts \(0.006\) hits. \(k = 4, 6, 8\) at \(5000\), with the detectable effect size printed alongside, can see the effect.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Every measurement prints its expected effect size and its detection floor \emph{before} its result. A null then reads as \emph{below the floor} and not as \emph{absent}.

\section{Estimator ceiling set by sample size (partial)}

Reporting a plug-in estimate whose value is fixed by how many samples were drawn and not by the distribution.

\textbf{Instance.} The Rényi~\cite{src:Renyi} order one half plug-in is \(2 \log_2 \sum \sqrt{p}\). Where every observed value is a singleton that sum is exactly \(\sqrt{n}\), and the estimate reads \(\log_2 n\) whatever the true entropy is. At \(2{,}000{,}000\) pairs the ceiling is \(20.93\) bits and the estimate reads \(18.25\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} Print the ceiling next to the estimate. Where they are close the estimate is mostly sample size and is not a measurement.

\textbf{Status.} Done for the Rényi estimates. Every other sparse-sample estimator is unchecked, and Good-Turing coverage~\cite{src:Good-1953} is the obvious place to look.

\section{Degenerate pairs (partial)}

Constructing a pair meant to differ and failing to check that it does.

\textbf{Instance.} Exclusive-or at positions that are not distinct flips bits back and leaves some pairs identical. The result prints \emph{smallest output distance: 0 bits}, which reads as a collision, directly above prose saying nothing came close.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Count and report the pairs whose intended difference is zero. A nonzero count reports a construction defect and carries no result.

\section{Optimizer-dependent result (built)}

A compiler folds, contracts, reassociates and hoists. A folded measurement is indistinguishable from a computed one by reading the output.

\textbf{Instance.} A whole hashing implementation that is a constant function under optimization, and three performance ratios that move with the build.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Build every measurement three ways, \texttt{-O0}, \texttt{-O2}, and \texttt{-O2 -ffp-contract=off}, and compare the outputs with stopwatch lines dropped.

\textbf{Result.} \(19\) of \(22\) judged measurements are byte-identical across all three arms. The three that move are all ratios of measured throughputs: extranonce cost \(11.1\times\) / \(28.3\times\) / \(23.4\times\), expansion speedup \(0.932\times\) / \(0.395\times\) / \(0.256\times\), delivery search-over-verify \(1.08 \times 10^{19}\) / \(5.46 \times 10^{19}\) / \(6.15 \times 10^{19}\).

\textbf{What it does not close.} That a folded constant is folded \emph{correctly}. For the Rényi measurement that is settled separately by a reimplementation of every printed prediction in another language against another libm. Nothing else has that check.

\textbf{An alignment fault under the unoptimized build.} The known-answer test and one measurement produce no \texttt{-O0} output because they segfault there. The trace:

\begin{verbatim}
vmovdqa %ymm0,-0x20(%rbp)   rbp = 0x5fe0b0, target 0x5fe090, which is 16 mod 32
\end{verbatim}

\texttt{vmovdqa} is the \emph{aligned} 256-bit move and requires \(32\). MinGW-w64 GCC at \texttt{-O0} hand-aligns a pointer for one over-aligned \texttt{\_\_m256i} local, the emitted \texttt{add \$0x1f; shr \$0x5; shl \$0x5} being visible in the prologue, and then places another at a fixed offset from a frame pointer it never realigned. \texttt{-mstackrealign} changes the prologue not at all. \textbf{The fault is in the compiler, a known defect of 32-byte stack alignment on 64-bit Windows~\cite{src:GCC-bug-54412}, and no source change avoids it}; the instruction is available and \texttt{-O2} runs it happily.

The resolution is that the \texttt{-O0} arm drops \texttt{-mavx2}. The hashing core gates its vector arm on \texttt{\_\_AVX2\_\_} and the \texttt{\#else} arm defers to the scalar reference. The unoptimized build is therefore the reference arm, the arm a fold audit wants, since it is the arm whose arithmetic the statistics read. The known-answer test then runs \texttt{-O0} clean: \textbf{24 run, 0 failed.}

\textbf{And what that costs.} For the two measurements exercising the vector arm, the \texttt{-O0} column tests different code from the other two columns. Agreement there is evidence about the reference arm and not about the vector arm. The vector arm is covered instead by the known-answer test asserting it equal to the reference on the FIPS 180-4 vectors~\cite{src:FIPS-180-4}, the genesis block, block 125552 and \(1000\) consecutive real headers. That is a strong check, and it is a different check, and reading the audit table as though all three columns meant the same thing for those two rows would be the kind of substitution this sieve exists to catch.

\section{Prose contradicting its own table (open)}

\textbf{Instance.} Prose saying the two \emph{agree often} directly above a column reading \(0.0000\%\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} Every prose claim names the number it rests on. The two can then be compared without re-deriving the claim.

\section{Conflated quantities (open)}

\textbf{Instance.} A characteristic, a product over one chosen intermediate path, treated as a differential, the enumeration over all intermediates. Only the two zeros in such a table are informative.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Naming discipline. Two quantities that differ get two names, and the name records which one is meant.

\section{Partial standing in for the whole (partial)}

\textbf{Instances.} Reduced rounds in five measurements, one output word of eight, a 64-bit prefix of a 256-bit digest, one frame, one constant, chunk two without the header, sampling in place of enumeration. Every one of these is a projection reported as the thing.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Lead with the whole, and label a partial as partial in the same breath. The Rényi measurement enumerates all \(2^{32}\) nonces instead of sampling, and reads the whole 256-bit digest.

\section{Max-statistic against a per-bin null (partial)}

Reporting the largest of many bins against the expectation for one bin.

\textbf{Instance.} The \(775{,}000\times\) above, which the permutation null puts at \(184.5\times\).

\textbf{The subtraction.} The permutation null over the maximum itself~\cite{src:Westfall-and-Young-1993}, or an extreme-value reference. Drawing \(n\) samples into \(r\) bins gives an expected maximum well above \(n/r\), and that expectation is the reference.

\section{Argmax over a tied set (partial)}

Selecting \emph{the most common} value where many values share the top count. The sort's tiebreak makes the selection, and the data does not.

\textbf{Instance.} A walk through delta space steered by the most common outgoing delta. Counting how many share that maximum:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
round & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
\midrule
deltas tied at the peak & 1 & \textbf{12} & \textbf{21} & \textbf{19} & \textbf{17} & \textbf{29} & \textbf{17} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

From round one onward there is no single most common delta. Between twelve and twenty-nine of them share a top count of two occurrences in \(400{,}000\) draws, and the walk follows whichever has the largest numeric value. \textbf{A comparison between peak-steered and tail-steered walks is a comparison between two tiebreaks from round one on.}

\textbf{What remains.} The peak \emph{value} is unaffected, since a tie does not change the height of the maximum, only which delta carries it. The compound figure stands. The strategy comparison does not.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Count the ties at the argmax and print the count beside it.

\section{A result that exists at one seed (open)}

Thirty-two of the fixed seeds in this work are the same constant. A statistic appearing at one seed and not at others is a draw, and not a finding.

\textbf{The subtraction.} Sweep the seed and report the spread across seeds beside the headline. The detection floor is then measured instead of derived.

\textbf{Why it counts here.} The three-arm compiler audit establishes that \(19\) measurements are deterministic: the same seed gives the same answer under three different builds. Determinism is not stability, and the audit does not test whether the answers move when the seed does.

\section{The same formula written twice (built)}

Two copies of one statistic, differing in a case one of them handles and the other does not.

\textbf{Instance, in the sieve's own instrument.} Three copies of the Rényi deficit sum: the general one over bins, an inline one over the count histogram, and a third inside the Arikan block. The empty-bin rule is opposite at order one and at every other order. A bin holding nothing contributes exactly \(-1\) at a positive order and exactly \(0\) at order one, since the count in front of the logarithm goes to zero faster than the logarithm goes to minus infinity. The general copy knows both rules. The histogram copy applies the first rule to order one and puts \textbf{0.2965 in a table where 0.8272 belongs}, on the headline full-domain run.

\textbf{A second instance, one layer out.} A Poisson prediction at order one missing a \(-\log_2(\mathrm{mean})\) term. That term is exactly zero when the mean count is one, the full-domain case. The wrong formula \textbf{agrees perfectly at the only point it is read at} and disagrees everywhere else. A defect that hides at the operating point is the worst kind, and only a run at a smaller domain exposes it.

\textbf{The subtraction.} One function that knows the empty-bin rule for every order, with every path going through it. Not a check on the copies: the removal of the copies.

\textbf{A limit.} The reimplementation in another language against another libm carries the same missing \(-\log_2(\mathrm{mean})\) term. An independent implementation written by the same author reproduces the author's misconceptions faithfully; what it catches is transcription and folding, and not a wrong idea. What catches the wrong idea is running at a domain where the term does not vanish.

\section{What the sieve removes}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
mode & detector & status & what it removes \\
\midrule
1 absence as measurement & exact reachability & \textbf{built} & 3 defects \\
4 over-reading the output & write mask & \textbf{built} & 75\% of a one-round read \\
8 optimizer & three-arm build diff & \textbf{built} & 3 performance claims; 19 cleared \\
14 one seed & seed sweep & \textbf{built} & a survival result confirmed not a draw \\
15 formula written twice & one deficit function & \textbf{built} & 2 wrong Rényi numbers \\
2 hand null & \(\Pi_\Sigma\) permutation & partial & a factor of 4,200 from the headline \\
13 tied argmax & tie count at the peak & partial & the walk's strategy comparison \\
6 estimator ceiling & ceiling beside estimate & partial & the order-1/2 plug-in as a measurement \\
11 partial for whole & lead with the whole & partial & five reduced-round claims \\
7 degenerate pairs & zero-difference counter & partial & a false 0-bit output distance \\
3, 5, 9, 10, 12 & none & \textbf{open} & nothing \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Five modes are closed, five are partial, five have no detector. Of the open modes, mode 3 (the swap test) and mode 5 (printing the detection floor before the result) are both mechanical. Modes 9 and 10 are discipline instead of instrumentation and will not yield to a tool.

Mode 15 deserves the most weight, because it occurs inside the instrument built to catch the others. The sieve does not exempt itself. Both of its instances are exposed by running the same code at a domain it was not designed around, the cheapest detector on this whole list: \textbf{run every measurement at a second scale, and compare the shape instead of the number.} A statistic that is a property of the function scales predictably; one that is a property of the apparatus does not.
