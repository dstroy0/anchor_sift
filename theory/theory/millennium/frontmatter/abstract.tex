\chapter*{Abstract}

This is a reconnaissance and states no result. It records what the Millennium Prize problems ask, in
the words of their official statements; what was published against one of them in September 2026;
which of the rest an arbitrary-precision apparatus could be aimed at; and the reading that is not
done. No problem is attacked here, and no priority is claimed over anything.

The corpus is seven documents. Five are Clay Mathematics Institute problem descriptions, covering P
versus NP, the Hodge conjecture, the Riemann hypothesis, quantum Yang--Mills theory, and the Birch
and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture. Two are Riemann: his 1859 memoir in the Wilkins translation, and four
photographed pages of his handwriting. Each was identified from its own pages. The photographs hold a
working draft and not the published memoir, carry no extractable text, and circulate under a
filename that names the memoir; a citation built from that filename would point at the wrong
document.

Navier--Stokes is absent from the corpus because the Clay index of unsolved problems does not list
it. On 8 September 2026 OpenAI published a proof of finite-time blowup for the forced
three-dimensional incompressible equations, with a Lean~4 formalization. Fefferman's statement asks
for a proof of any one of four statements, and two of the four name a smooth external force. Forcing
is therefore inside the problem as posed.

The formalized statement is checked against the problem statement on six points: viscosity strictly
positive with no vanishing limit, the decay conditions on the data and on the force, a force smooth
on the closed half-line, a conclusion quantified as a negated existential, kinetic energy bounded for
all time, and the periodic pressure condition the errata add. All six match. The statement is not
the authors' own: it is adapted from the Formal Conjectures project's published formalization, and a
separate module discharges it. A proof assistant checks a deduction and never a translation, and
this arrangement answers that objection as far as an arrangement can.

The limits are stated where the claims are. The Lean development is not built and nothing here is
machine-checked. Of the papers, the statements and the bridging argument are read and the proofs are
not. A priority dispute runs in this field and no position is taken on it.

The problem the apparatus can reach is Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer. The refined conjecture is an exact
numerical identity, and checking an instance comes down to whether a computed real number lands on an
integer. The Riemann hypothesis is the trap: the apparatus matches its subject and not its
obstruction, and counting more zeros bears on no proof. The null for the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer
measurement is drawn through the same pipeline as the effect, because a derived null sets the bar too
low.
