\chapter{Claims that do not hold, with what refutes each}

A refuted claim with its cause attached is the cheapest kind of knowledge. This chapter lists the claims about SHA-256 that the measurements refute, each with the measurement that refutes it.

\section{Claims about this work's own measurements}

\textbf{Forward converges, the inverse does not.} It does not hold. Read as a statistic and not as a threshold crossing, the inverse scales as \(\sqrt{N}\) at every depth from nine to fourteen: ratios of \(4.00\), \(4.00\), \(3.99\), \(3.99\), \(4.22\), \(3.83\) for sixteenfold more pairs. Both directions have walls; only the shape of the approach differs.

\textbf{The inverse has no wall, sixteen and climbing.} It does not hold. It dies at fifteen and round sixteen reads \(2.438\), \(1.317\), \(0.881\) as pairs rise, which is noise shrinking. One more round per fourfold increase is the decay rate being measured, not a trend to project.

\textbf{The word statistic goes blind at round ten.} It does not hold. The per-bit refinement sees more where there is more (inverse round fourteen reads \(18.5\) by bit against \(7.5\) by word), but no depth in either direction reads by bit and not by word.

\textbf{Forward round ten is small, not off.} It does not hold. At \(2^{30}\) pairs a continued slope predicts \(9.706\) and it reads \(2.386\), unmoved across \(2^{26}\), \(2^{28}\), \(2^{30}\). The same extrapolation on the inverse as a control lands within four significant figures at all three sizes.

\textbf{Decay is geometric, \(29.7\times\) forward against \(2.1\times\) inverse.} It does not hold. Sweeping from round one shows both accelerate; that comparison puts forward at its steepest against the inverse four rounds earlier on the same shape. A slope of \(1.469\) per round fitted through rounds pinned at the statistic's ceiling is the ceiling.

\textbf{The peak destruction rate tracks word width.} It does not hold. Peaks read \(3.50\), \(2.05\), \(1.67\), \(3.54\), \(5.24\), \(3.03\), \(4.86\) bits against widths of \(2.00\) to \(5.00\), and the column is not measurable at that resolution regardless.

\textbf{The schedule pair is lossy because it carries shifts.} It does not hold. All four functions have rank thirty-two and kernel zero.

\textbf{The sparse words step by four.} It does not hold. The exact trace floors the nonce at \(2, 1, 6, 2, 4, 2, 7, 6, 5, 2, 11\) across \(W_{18}\) to \(W_{28}\); only \(W_{19}\) is a genuine path and the floor-two words are \(18, 21, 23, 27\), spaced three, two, four. The step is which cancellations clear the sampling floor.

\textbf{A minimum weight per word measures sparsity.} It does not hold. A minimum is a tail statistic: at \(2^{27}\) pairs chance alone gives a one-bit word once per word. The rate against \(33/2^{32}\) is the measurement.

\textbf{A floor of one means the path is reachable.} It does not hold. \(W_0\) floors at one on \(W_{33}\) and is never observed in \(2^{25}\) pairs. The floor wants a carry-free chain and that chance falls with its length.

\textbf{Nonce bit thirty-one carries \(180\) sigma.} It does not hold. A sweep over \(2^{31}\) nonces leaves bit thirty-one zero in every sample, against an expectation computed over the full space. The sweep has to cover the whole \(2^{32}\).

\textbf{The \(5.0\%\) word-round saving is worth \(5.0\%\) of runtime.} It does not hold on the CPU. Implemented, exactly correct on two hundred thousand nonces, it measures \(0.998\), \(0.978\), \(1.079\), \(0.957\), with a median about \(1.00\). Word-rounds bound arithmetic, and on the CPU arithmetic does not bound runtime. On the device, where the kernel is ALU and power bound, the same change is worth \(4.34\%\).

\textbf{A single timed pass can resolve a five percent effect.} It does not hold. One pass each reads \(0.935\) to \(1.166\), a spread wider than the effect.

\textbf{Register pressure limits the device kernel.} It does not hold. Thirty-eight registers, zero spill stores, zero spill loads, and occupancy capped by the thread limit and not by registers.

\textbf{Single-bit masks can see linear structure.} It does not hold, by its own control. A \(\mathrm{GF}(2)\)-linear function's output bit is an exclusive-or of many input bits and is balanced against any single one. The known-linear arm reads the noise floor exactly like every other arm. The linear axis remains untested.

\textbf{The width sweep measures annihilated state functions at several widths.} It does not hold. That is the raw-amount object; in the clamped object \(\Sigma_0\) is clean at every width and only width twelve carries a kernel.

\textbf{The octant alphabet saturates.} It does not hold, refuted by the object and not by the statistic. A measurement on the finalized digest, where \(0\) of \(378\) carried words match the words they are copied from, cannot see the register shift at any sample size; on the round's own working state \(378\) of \(378\) match. The finalization adds the chaining input back modulo \(2^{32}\) and destroys the structure being looked for. On the state the delta starts at \(1.27\times\) the redraw floor and crosses to \(0.76\times\): a trend, with no saturation in it.

\textbf{The octant delta sits at a level.} It does not hold, for any level: a ramp, a stationary value and a floor all fail. The quantity has a trend and crosses its own null between the ends of the run. A threshold placed on it is a threshold on the wrong shape.

\textbf{Sixty-four distinct signatures out of sixty-four rounds is coherence.} It does not hold, by a rank. The eight-letter reading is a linear map of rank \(8\) on a \(256\)-dimensional source, leaving seven free numbers at fixed weight and \(248\) directions that move no letter at all. Seven numbers separate sixty-four arbitrary states whether or not the seven carry meaning. Distinctness at that sample size is free.

\textbf{A ridge at residue twenty-six decays at \(1/32\) per round.} It does not hold. Residue twenty-six sits \(-1.36\) sigmas from the class mean, a deficit, and the \(1/32\) decay belongs to the common mode every class shares.

\section{Claims taken from the published literature}

\textbf{SHA-256 fails the strict avalanche criterion at rounds fifty-four to fifty-seven.} It does not hold. Measured at three sample sizes, the absolute deviation halves each time \(k\) quadruples, \(1/\sqrt{k}\) exactly, while the sigma holds at \(4.5\) across a sixteenfold range. The threshold crossed is \(5.327\) sigmas against a null that peaks at \(4.85\) unaided.

\textbf{SHA-256 never meets the strict avalanche criterion at any round.} It does not hold. The persistent \(0.21\%\) is \(4.2\) sigmas at a million trials, \emph{below} what the maximum of \(131{,}072\) null draws produces without any function at all.

\textbf{Choice and majority dampen each other, ratio \(0.508\).} Reproduced at \(0.5082\) and explained. Choice alone gives a SAC-mean of \(0.01562361\), majority alone \(0.01560888\), and the two together \(0.01562336\), identical to choice alone to two parts in ten million. The pair does not dampen; majority contributes no diffusion that choice does not already provide. The measured value is \(A\) while the independence model predicts about \(2A\), and the ratio is one half by construction.

\section{The shape they share}

Five of these are one error: a maximum over \(N\) cells compared against a threshold built for a single test, two published and three in this work. Two more are a trend read from a threshold crossing instead of from the statistic. Two more are a baseline measured under different conditions from the effect. Three more are a level asserted for a quantity that has a trend, the same error inverted.

Two are a different shape and a worse one. \textbf{The saturation claim reads the wrong object}, and the coherence claim \textbf{counts a separation that a rank makes free}. Neither can be caught by a better null, because the null is fine: one measures a state that does not contain the effect, and the other measures a quantity whose dimension is too small to carry it. The discipline therefore needs a second rule beside the first (\textbf{name the object, and count the dimensions of the reading}), and both failures stay silent in the direction that looks like a result.

Refuting them takes no new cryptanalysis. All but two require measuring the null the same way as the effect; those two require asking what is being read.
