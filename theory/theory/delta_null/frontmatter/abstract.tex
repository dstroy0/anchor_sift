\bigskip

\noindent Measure how far an object is from the most disordered arrangement of its own parts.

That is the method. Each subject in this work applies it with a different answer to what a part
is: atoms in a crystal cell, symbols in a corpus, bytes in a file, coordinates on a circuit board.
The reference is built only from the object's own parts. There is no prior estimate, no
training set, and no model or neural network. The reference is the maximum entropy arrangement of
those parts, the shuffled copy that keeps every count, and the measurement is the exact difference
from it. In statistics this is a permutation test \cite{src:Good-2005}.

Two propositions set the limits of the search. First, any subset of a pattern's positions is a
necessary condition for the pattern. A filter that checks a subset never loses a true match. That
holds whatever chose the subset, whatever the alphabet is, and whether or not the positions have an
order, as long as each check compares symbols, or compares ranks counted together with the
pattern's. Filtering followed by verification is standard in string search \cite{src:Navarro}, and
this is the condition under which the filter is lossless. Second, the reverse fails for every proper
subset: passing the checks never proves a match. The full comparison cannot be skipped, and every
implementation has to verify what its filter lets through. Never losing a match costs nothing.
Confirming one costs a full comparison.

The search is implemented in the orior engine, and Section 2.7 of the first chapter states its
guarantees, each with the condition it needs. The
counts are exact for any valid set of checks on byte data, and on ranked data with 256 classes or
fewer under a transitive equality test. Placing the checks greedily drops at least \(1 - 1/e\) of
what the best placement drops when every alignment is scored \cite{src:Nemhauser-Wolsey-and-Fisher}.
The search goes at most four levels deep, and fewer when the data, the candidates available or the
number of checks asked for run out first.

The work uses two basic measurements:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Entropy of the object.} The set \(T\) measured against its own maximum entropy. It
    has no unit. Shannon defines entropy in section 6, page 389, where \(H\) is largest, at
    \(\log n\), when every \(p_i\) is equal \cite{src:Shannon}.
  \item \textbf{Difference from the permutation null}, \(\Delta_{\Sigma}\). How far \(T\) is from the
    maximum entropy arrangement of its own parts. In its bitwise form it lists the exact symmetries
    of \(T\) that the shuffled copies do not reproduce.
\end{enumerate}

The two are not equal as evidence. The shift agreement detector finds a period by how often the data
agrees with a shifted copy of itself, and published measurements check its answers. The difference
from the permutation null compares the data with shuffles of the same symbols, and no outside source
holds the answer to check it against.

The negative result is that collision entropy does not change when the data is shuffled. An object
and its most disordered rearrangement have the same symbol counts, and so the same collision entropy.
No bound built from a histogram can tell structured data from a rearrangement of the same symbols.

What follows is the simplest form of the algorithm. Other arrangements are in the orior engine
workbook \cite{src:Quigg-orior-workbook}. The chapters work through the method as it runs, and the
last chapter defines its terms.
