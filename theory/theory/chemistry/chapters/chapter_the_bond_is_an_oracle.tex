\chapter{The bond length is a test oracle}

\section{A fact, not a consensus}

The method keeps its outside answers apart, as test oracles, and grades against them. A family tree
of languages is a reconstruction argued from cognates. Agreement with it is agreement with a
scholarly consensus, and where the two disagree nothing in the method can say which is wrong. A
published crystal cell edge is the other kind: somebody measured it, refereed it and wrote it down,
and the instrument either recovers it or does not. The bond length is that second kind and more so.
Valence fixes it whatever anyone believes. A carbon-carbon single bond is near $1.54$ angstroms, a
double near $1.34$, a triple near $1.20$, and a substance does not get a vote.

Bond lengths therefore belong apart from the family trees, because a bond length is a fact and a
family tree is a consensus and the two must not be graded as the same kind of answer. The values
the method consults for a protein already come from this table: the backbone bonds are near $1.46$,
$1.52$ and $1.33$ angstroms, nitrogen to alpha carbon, alpha carbon to carbon, and the peptide
carbon to the next nitrogen. None of those three is measured by the instrument that reads the
backbone. They are the answer it is checked against.

\section{Two domains already rest on it}

The method has a representation for a protein and one for a crystal, and both stand on chemistry
without naming it. The protein backbone is a chain of bonds whose lengths cycle with a period of
three, because the three bond types repeat down the chain, and that period is fixed by chemistry
before the chain is measured. It is a positive control with an answer not produced by the sample: a
detector that returns anything other than three there is wrong, and no argument afterward can save
it. The three comes from valence, and valence is chemistry.

The crystal is the same fact tiled. A cell edge is a repeat distance, and what sets the repeat is the
sizes of the atoms and the lengths of the bonds between them, packed into the smallest cell that
reproduces under translation. The instrument recovers $453$ of $453$ published edges inside one
voxel~\cite{src:Quigg-crystallography}, and the thing it is recovering is a bond geometry stacked.
Chemistry is not a third domain beside the protein and the crystal. It is the layer under both, the
building blocks and the one rule that fix the lengths those two readings recover.

\section{The fact makes the trap visible}

A test oracle that fixes a period exactly is also what exposes a detector's own failure. A sequence of
period $P$ agrees with itself as well at $2P$ and $3P$. A detector that takes the tallest lag
reports a harmonic about as often as the period. On the protein backbone chemistry says the answer
is three, and a detector returning six or nine is wrong on the spot. Without the fixed answer the
harmonic passes as a clean result, a confident number with a factor of two that nothing flags.

That is the argument for chemistry as a test oracle stated from the other side. It supplies an
answer, and the answer is exact and external. An instrument checked against it cannot hide an error
inside a tolerance. A bond length carries no spread that a detector's mistake can shelter in. It is
the hardest test available because the sample can argue with it least.
