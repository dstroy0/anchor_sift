\chapter{Valence is a necessary condition}

\section{The rule the sift already states}

The engine's sift rests on one proposition: any subset of a pattern's points is a necessary
condition. No selection rule built from the pattern can lose a true occurrence, and the converse
fails for any proper subset. Every surviving candidate still has to be confirmed. Errors are one
directional. A discrepancy is always an over-count, and it is detectable without knowing the answer.

Valence is a proposition of exactly that shape. A main-group atom in a stable molecule completes its
octet: eight electrons in the outer shell, two for hydrogen and helium, counting shared pairs once
for each atom that shares them. This is a condition on every atom at once, and a real molecule
satisfies all of them. So the octet is a necessary condition on an arrangement, and a candidate
structure that fails it at any atom is not a molecule. No true structure is ever refused by the
octet, because a true structure meets it everywhere. What the rule discards is candidates, and that
is cost, not correctness, the sift's own division carried onto chemistry unchanged.

\section{A truthy reading at each atom}

Read one atom and its bonds and the question is boolean. Sum the electrons it owns outright and the
pairs it shares, compare against the shell it is filling, and the atom either closes or it does not.
Carbon with four single bonds closes at eight and is true; carbon with three is a radical and is
false; carbon with five cannot be drawn at all. The reading needs no tolerance. An electron pair is
present or absent, a bond is drawn or not, and the count is an integer compared against an integer.

The molecule is the conjunction. It is true when every atom closes, and the sift's intersection is
the operation that reads it: each atom's condition is a set of admissible local environments, and the
arrangement survives where it lies in all of them at once. This is the same cascade the engine runs
on a text or a lattice, with the anchor being an atom's satisfied octet instead of a rare symbol at
an offset. Nothing is ported to bring it here. The sift was written over an index set with no
interpretation, and an atom is one more thing an index can hold.

\section{Why the exact compare is irreducible}

The octet selects candidates and does not confirm them, exactly as the proposition warns. A drawn
structure that satisfies every octet can still be the wrong isomer, because the same atoms with the
same valences admit more than one arrangement: ethanol and dimethyl ether share a formula and both
close every atom. The rule narrows the field to the arrangements that could be molecules and leaves
the choice among them to something the rule does not see, which for a real substance is its energy
and for this engine is a departure measured against the disordered packing of the next chapter.

The one-directional error is worth stating in chemistry's terms. A structure the octet refuses is
refused rightly: it cannot be a closed-shell molecule. A structure the octet admits is not thereby a
molecule., a search that uses valence to prune loses nothing real and keeps some things that are
not real, the only failure the sift permits and the reason a verification pass is never
optional.
