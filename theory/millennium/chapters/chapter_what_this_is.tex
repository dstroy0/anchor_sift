\chapter{What this is}

\textbf{Purpose:} State what this book records, what authorized it, and the boundary of what it
claims. \textbf{Scope:} \texttt{theory/\allowbreak{}millennium/\allowbreak{}}, and the corpus at
\texttt{Downloads/\allowbreak{}millenium/\allowbreak{}} as it stood on 2026-09-11.

\section{The book}

One session, on 2026-09-11. Somebody had already gathered the corpus. The instruction was narrow:
check one specific claim, then write down what you found. This record is what came of that.

Four things sit in it. First the corpus, and what each document turns out to be once you open it,
as against what its filename promises. Then the state of the Navier--Stokes problem, which moved
three days before anyone started writing. Then a reading of that move against the official problem
statement and against the Lean formalization that shipped with it. Last, a recommendation about
where the project's apparatus might be aimed, which belongs to somebody else and is recorded here
without being adopted.

No problem gets attacked. Nobody measured anything, ran anything, or opened a line of attack. Lead
a book with a method and you have already claimed the method reaches something; no claim of that
kind appears here or is supported here.

\section{What is not claimed}

Nobody claims priority in these pages and nobody wants it. \texttt{theory/\allowbreak{}%
crystallography} says as much about its own construction. Saying it twice, here, is deliberate.

The reading is the part nobody did. Take any of the five open problems in the corpus: somebody has to
go through the field behind it and set down what that field already knows. Nobody here did. Until
somebody does, these pages assert no new method and credit no old one.

This book has a second reason to be blunt about that. A priority dispute is live in the field the
Navier--Stokes chapter covers. Nobody here looked into it. No position gets taken, and no line in
that chapter should be mistaken for evidence on either side. The chapter reports what two documents
say. Who got there first is a question it never asks.

\section{The rail this was written under}

Five rules governed the writing, and each one limits what the later chapters may claim.

\textbf{Cite nothing unread.} Opening a document is what earns it a citation here. When a fact came
by report instead, the sentence carrying that fact admits it.

\textbf{A peer's reading buys you no second witness.} Three sessions fed into this book. Whenever
one handed over a fact, somebody went to the file or the document to confirm it, instead of writing
it down on trust. Two of those confirmations came back different, and the ledger chapter holds both.

\textbf{Gaps go in the sentence making the claim.} Not a footnote, not an appendix. The
Navier--Stokes chapter runs a whole section on what went unverified, and marks the individual soft
spots where they fall.

\textbf{Withdrawn claims stay on the page, with whatever killed them.} The ledger chapter does
nothing else. Eight claims came out over the course of this work, six of them belonging to this
session.

\textbf{Draw the bar. Never derive it.} Reason a threshold out of a distribution and you inherit
every variance source you forgot, all of them pushing the same way. That rule turns up here as a
correction and not as a precaution: the null offered for the recommended measurement had been
derived, its author pulled it, and the toolkit chapter carries the drawn replacement.

\section{Whose decisions these were}

Scope never belonged to this book. Which problem, whether the effort exists at all, whether any of
this got written: those were Douglas Quigg's to settle, and the instruction behind this book came
from him. The toolkit chapter's recommendation is the project architect's, set down for Quigg to
rule on and not acted on.

One structural call went deliberately untaken. The shared preamble already declares theorem, lemma,
proposition and definition environments, numbered within chapter, and not one existing book uses
them; they put propositions in prose and number them by hand. Switching to the declared environments
would reach all seven books through that one shared file, so this book follows the practice it found
instead of changing a convention it has no standing to change.
