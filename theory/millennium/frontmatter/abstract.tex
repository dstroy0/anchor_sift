\chapter*{Abstract}

This book is reconnaissance and not a result. It records what the Millennium Prize problems are as
their official statements pose them, what happened to one of them in September 2026, which of the
remaining ones this project's apparatus could be pointed at, and what nobody here has done. No
problem is attacked in these pages and no priority is claimed in them.

The corpus is seven documents: the Clay Mathematics Institute problem descriptions for P versus NP,
the Hodge conjecture, the Riemann hypothesis, quantum Yang--Mills theory and the Birch and
Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, together with Riemann's 1859 memoir in the Wilkins translation and a
four-page photographic scan of Riemann's handwritten working manuscript. Each was opened. The scan
is not the published memoir, carries no extractable text, and is named as though it were the paper;
that is the one finding the corpus chapter exists to record, because a citation seeded from its
filename would enter the registry wrong.

Navier--Stokes is absent from the corpus because it is absent from the Clay index of unsolved
problems. On 8 September 2026 OpenAI published a proof of finite-time blowup for the forced
three-dimensional incompressible equations and a Lean~4 formalization of it. Fefferman's official
problem description asks for a proof of one of four statements, and two of them, (C) and (D), name a
smooth external force inside the statement. Forcing is therefore in the problem as posed, and this
work said the opposite twice before reading the document. Both retractions are recorded in the
last chapter with what killed them.

The formalization was checked against the problem statement on six points: strictly positive
viscosity with no vanishing-viscosity limit, the decay conditions on initial data and force
unrelaxed, smoothness of the force on the whole closed half-line, the conclusion in the negated
existential form, kinetic energy bounded uniformly in time rather than at the initial time alone,
and the periodic pressure condition added by the errata appended to the problem statement. It passes
on all six. The statement discharged is not the authors' own: it was adapted from an independent
Lean formalization of the problem published beforehand by the Formal Conjectures project, and the
adapted file retains deliberate placeholder gaps while a separate module discharges the identical
statement and prints its axiom dependencies. That architecture answers the objection this project
raises against machine-checked proofs, which is that a proof assistant checks the deduction and
never the translation.

What was not done is as important and is stated where it belongs rather than in a footnote. The Lean
development was not built and nothing here was machine-checked; the reading is of source text. The
166-page paper was read at its statements and its bridging argument, not throughout. The
2,669-file proof tree was not entered. The priority dispute running in the field was not
investigated, and no position on it is taken or implied.

On which problem this apparatus reaches, the recommendation recorded here is Birch and
Swinnerton-Dyer, and it is the project architect's and not this book's. The reasoning is that the
refined conjecture is an exact numerical identity whose verification is a question about whether a
computed real lands on an integer, which is the shape of question a precision engine answers.
Riemann is recorded as the trap in the same chapter: the apparatus matches the subject and not the
obstruction, computation of zeros on the critical line is long past anything reached here, and no
finite count of zeros bears on the proof. A null offered for the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer
measurement was withdrawn by its author for being derived rather than drawn, and the drawn
replacement is recorded in its place.

Nothing in this book is a claim to a new method and nothing in it is an attribution. The reading of
the literature behind any of these problems has not been done here, and until it is, that remains
the missing work and not a gap in someone else's.
