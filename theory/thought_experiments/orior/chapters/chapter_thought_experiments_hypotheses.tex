\chapter{Hypotheses from the notebook}

A notebook of suppositions was written beside this work, in the order the thinking happened. It is kept
whole, with the posits that have no test, as wants in the workbook~\cite{src:Quigg-orior-workbook}.
Four of its entries, and one proposal written beside it, are concrete enough to state with the test
that would decide them.

\section{Ordering anchors by symbol rarity}

The proposal orders the anchor set by the rarity of the symbol each anchor tests, rarest first, so
that the first comparison carries the most rejection power~\cite{src:Quigg-delta-null}. Comparing a
pattern's characters in increasing order of frequency is established in string search, in Sunday's
optimal mismatch algorithm~\cite{src:Sunday-1990}. Here it is a change
to a function that exists, it costs one sort when the search is planned, and it predicts the direction
of a number the bench already prints. On the skewed corpus the first probe refutes about 67 percent of
alignments, the figure a collision probability of 0.33 gives.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} Ordering by rarity raises the first probe's refutation rate above that figure.
\textbf{Test.} The same corpus and patterns, with and without the sort. \textbf{Status.} Not run.

\section{Tail error under a periodic disturbance}

Introducing a periodic disturbance on one arm lowers the joint error on an undisturbed co-arm by
several orders of magnitude. Random distributions with no affine structure work best as the
disturbance.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} The error reduction, plotted against the percentage of the tail included, follows
a smooth curve, and a straight line would mark a branch that could reach a tail error of \(10^{-26}\).
\textbf{Test.} That plot, over disturbances of each kind. \textbf{Status.} The plot is not made.

\section{Doping as a known distribution}

A known dopant distribution plays the part a salt plays in cryptography: the hard work of measuring it
is done once, and afterwards it is known.

\textbf{Hypothesis.} From measured dopant distributions, new allowed dopant arrangements can be
predicted. \textbf{Test.} Hold measured arrangements out of the distribution and predict them.
\textbf{Status.} Not run. The crystallography research paper reads a published cell edge back from
exact site coordinates~\cite{src:Quigg-crystallography}, and no dopant data is read.

\section{A protein backbone as vectors and magnitudes}

Ramachandran's allowed regions constrain a protein backbone's dihedral
angles~\cite{src:Ramachandran-1963}. Representing a backbone as a sequence of vectors and magnitudes
keeps the order of assembly.

\textbf{Status.} The representation is built. Walking the backbone in assembly order, nitrogen to
alpha carbon to carbon, it reads the bond lengths N--C\(_\alpha\), C\(_\alpha\)--C and C--N on five
structures from 137 to 24{,}024 bonds. Every length lies within 0.025~\AA{} of the standard values,
1.458, 1.525 and 1.329~\AA~\cite{src:Engh-Huber-1991,src:Quigg-orior-workbook}. The lengths are set by
valence, which makes this a positive control. \textbf{Hypothesis, not tested.} The allowed constructs predict
chirality and new allowed proteins.

\section{Use on cultural material}

A culture that was never recorded lives only in its people, passed from one generation to the next.
Where they are gone, what was true of it, which statements belong to it, can no longer be determined,
though the information need not be gone. A reconstruction presented as true where truth cannot be
determined misrepresents the culture it claims to recover.

This work therefore examines only what is present, and asks whether a reconstruction recreated it. The
tool is not to be used to regenerate cultural material without a human representative of that
culture.
