\chapter{Delta null as a boundary}

\section{The mapping}

The ground set \(\Sigma\) has no topology, metric or order. It supplies only distinctness: two symbols
are equal or they are not. The measurement \(\delta_0\) is the indicator of equality, and it turns a
comparison at a displacement \(d \in G\) into one bit, match or no match. The anchor set
\(A \subset D\), a subset of a pattern's points, acts as a boundary between the candidate alignments
and the rejected ones.

Checking \(\mathrm{Anc}_A(s)\) decides whether an alignment \(s \in G\) lies inside the feasible region
or crosses the boundary into the rejected exterior. The contrapositive
\(\neg\mathrm{Anc}_A(s) \Rightarrow \neg\mathrm{Occ}(s)\) states that hitting the boundary once excludes
\(s\) permanently. The choice of \(A\) is free with respect to correctness, and the boundary governs
only cost: the density and entropy of \(\Sigma\) over \(A\) set how many candidates the filter rejects
per operation. Because the boundary is defined through displacements in \(G\) and equality in
\(\Sigma\) alone, the search should be invariant under translation, permutation and change of
coordinate representation.

\section{Three claims, and where each stands}

\textbf{Invariance under translation, permutation and change of coordinate representation. Measured,
and it holds.} The lattice bench sweeps exactly this: 3{,}421 rows over alphabets of 2 to 256 symbols,
patterns of 1 to 32 points, dimensions 1 through 8, a rotated point set, a scatter no rectangle covers,
a complex alphabet with irrational parts compared over its storage, and an order check against a
permuted base list. Of 465{,}546 true occurrences, none is refused. The core takes a base list, a
displacement list and a callback answering whether two positions carry the same symbol. It has no
dimension parameter and no symbol type, and a core that cannot see either cannot depend on either.

\textbf{Hitting the boundary once excludes the point permanently. Proved, and checked on every row.}
This is the first proposition of the permutation null~\cite{src:Quigg-delta-null}: any subset of a
pattern's points is a necessary condition for an occurrence. A failure at one anchor refutes the
alignment and no later evidence restores it. Every bench that runs an arm against the exact compare
checks it.

\textbf{The volume is recoverable from its boundary. Fails.} The second proposition answers it: the
converse fails for every proper subset of the points~\cite{src:Quigg-delta-null}. The exact compare is
irreducible, and every arm has to verify what its anchors admitted. A boundary that excludes is not a
boundary that determines.

\section{The demon refuses and does not predict}

The strong form of the idea, a Laplace's demon that recovers the interior from the boundary, fails.
The weak form survives, and it is the algorithm: an anchor set is a boundary, crossing it is final, and
nothing about crossing it says what lies on the other side. A filter that refutes cheaply and
determines nothing is the shape the whole construction has. It is the reason soundness costs nothing
while completeness costs a full compare.

\section{What is not testable here}

The holographic principle in physics concerns a bound on the information a region can
hold~\cite{src:t-Hooft-1993,src:Susskind-1995}, in line with the entropy bound~\cite{src:Bekenstein-1981}.
Nothing in this repository measures that, and the resemblance to an anchor boundary is an analogy. The
analogy earns its place only in the three claims it produced, two of which hold and one of which
fails.
