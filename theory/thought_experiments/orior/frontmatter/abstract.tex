\chapter*{Abstract}

Every measurement in this body of work reads an object against a reference built from the object's
own parts: the most disordered arrangement its counts allow. This research paper sets out that method
and applies it to ideas instead of corpora. A reference of this kind is constructed and laid over a
real system, and it has to be allowed instead of derived, as the oldest working constructions in
mathematics were. Two practices follow. An instrument is run on inputs built to make it fail, since one
that cannot be made to say no reports nothing when it says yes. And a possibility that reads as
incoherent is allowed and pushed until it stops cohering, since what reads as sense to a person and
what a structure permits are measured here to come apart.

The delta null idea is the worked case. Of its three claims, invariance under translation,
permutation and change of coordinate representation holds over 3{,}421 bench rows and 465{,}546 true
occurrences, and permanent exclusion on hitting the boundary holds by construction. Recovering a volume
from its boundary fails, because the converse of exclusion fails for every proper subset of a
pattern's points: the boundary refuses and does not predict. A closed boundary holds a number of
separable readings set by its measure over the finest resolution, independent of shape and topology,
which mode counts on spheres and integer-point counts on flat tori confirm against Weyl's law to within
0.0122 percent; what the interior holds is a separate question that nothing here tests. Four entries of
a notebook of suppositions, and one proposal beside them, are stated as hypotheses with the test that
would decide each. The notebook itself, and the posits with no test, are kept whole as wants in the
workbook.
