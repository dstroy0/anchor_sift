\chapter*{Abstract}

This research paper states the ideas behind an exact-arithmetic engine in forms a proof or a
measurement can decide, and sets each against its literature. The noise of an image sensor has four
sources whose variances add, and averaging frames separates the fixed pattern from the rest. Search
for a hash below a threshold costs the reciprocal of its success probability however it is
parallelized, and folding compresses the proof of the search, not the search. A deterministic
interpretation of quantum mechanics leaves the exponential cost of simulating a circuit where it is.
For a renewal process, the laws of how long a bit holds its value fix its entropy rate, and a bench
is given that would measure the claim. A compiler that compiles itself to identical output shows a
fixed point, and diverse double-compiling is what shows its source accounts for it. Learning a
machine's rules from probes is exact only up to a stated bound on its states. Identity written into an
instance at spawn and a signed hash chain prove commitment, not truth. Each chapter states what the
engine builds of it and what it does not.
