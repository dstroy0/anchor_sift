# The engine table

**Purpose:** The machine, part by part: what each part computes, the exact algebra it holds to, what it does today, what it wants, every hypothesis tried there with its result, and the next move.
**Layout (Doug, 23 September):** `engine/base/` never sees a body, `engine/nbody/` is defined over n ≥ 1 bodies, `engine/prg_sch/` composes the runs (answer_key, cfg, nbody_program, run_cfg, run_log), `engine/render/` draws a step's state, and `engine/sims/` holds the sims (M19). The benches are the root `bench/`, the tests the root `test/`, and every build writes only into the root `build/`: each build into its own `build/<date>_<time>_<name>/`, removed when a later build starts more than a day on, with the finished program copied up into `build/` and `build/logs/` and `build/tsv/` never removed. The six steps (ingest, seal, lift, measure, partition, relate) and every file's step are in [engine/README.md](../../src/engine/README.md).
**Scope:** `engine/`: exact arithmetic, the residual operator, the component tree, the moments, the correlation operator, the marginal, matching, the entropy history, the files (their size against the floor is tracked in [compression_table.md](compression_table.md)), the record machine, errors, the seal (integrity; its theory is [obsignatio_seal.md](obsignatio_seal.md)), the scheduler, the sift, render, the memory operations, the period reading, the sims, the root universal, and the ask and the state. The programs that run on it are separate concerns with their own tables: cell tracking in [cell_tracking_table.md](cell_tracking_table.md), and RSNA knee MRI (its own session). Numbers are in [ledger.md](ledger.md). Statuses follow [README.md](README.md).

## The rule the machine is held to

**The engine is optimized for no scale** (Doug, 23 September). A size, a spacing, an order, a window, a width, a cell or a voxel count is never written into the machine. Each comes in with the request or is read from the data. The machine is exact at every scale its words can hold, and where a word is too narrow it says so (a request error, or `needed_bits`) and never rounds. Tuning the machine to one program's scale is the error this table exists to catch: it has been put in and ripped out repeatedly, and the audit below lists what is still in.

The machine and each program are optimized against each other only through the interface at the end of this table. Merged, neither can be optimized on its own.

The quotient is multiplied back, and a remainder is refused as `ANCHOR_EXACT_NOT_EXACT`. `anchor_exact_gcd` is Lehmer's (Knuth's algorithm L). ## The machine's algebra

Every line is exact. No line assumes a scale: orders, windows, widths and spacings are symbols the request fills.

### A0. Exact numbers (M1): `decimal_double`

A decimal d = (−1)^neg · digits · 10^ex is 2^{ex} · 5^{ex} · digits. Its binary value's leading W bits are found by multiplying or dividing by the widest power of 5 that fits a limb, chunk by chunk, with the fives stripped from a fraction first: the result is the exact integer ⌊|d| · 2^{−e2}⌋ with e2 chosen so the integer has W bits, and `terminates` says whether the rest is zero. When the value needs more than W bits, `needed_bits` says how many and `fits` = 0. Nothing is rounded.

### A1. The residual (M2): `residual_program`, `keymath`, `cycle`, `unit_sweep`

Per axis a, with [1 1] the unit step:

  L_n = [1 1]^n ∗ I,  B_s ∗ B_b = B_{s+b},  R = 2^{|b|} L_s − L_{s+b}

- **The two terms are two rungs of one ladder.** B_b applied to B_s I is B_{s+b} I; R is the difference of rungs s and s + b of one binomial ladder of the same image. Every residual for every (s, b) is a difference of two rungs; one sweep that keeps its rungs gives all of them. Orders per request, and a sweep over candidate periods, cost one pass (theory).
- **The kernel sums to zero.** B_s sums to 2^{|s|}; 2^{|b|} B_s and B_{s+b} both sum to 2^{|s|+|b|}. A constant added to I leaves R unchanged: the medium's mean cancels exactly.
- **The heat identity.** In centred steps H = [1 2 1] = [1 1]², with m = b/2 per axis:

  4^m δ − H^m = Σ_{k=0}^{m−1} 4^{m−1−k} H^k (4δ − H),  and 4δ − H = [−1 2 −1] = −Δ²

  so, per axis, R = Σ_{k=0}^{m−1} 4^{m−1−k} · (−Δ² S_k), with S_k = H^k S and S = H^{s/2} I. H/4 is one explicit step of the discrete heat equation; R is 4^m times what m heat steps remove from the smoothed field: the residual is exactly a weighted sum of negative discrete Laplacians down the ladder. In three axes the same telescoping runs axis by axis: 2^{G}δ − B_{b_z}⊗B_{b_y}⊗B_{b_x} = (2^{b_z}δ − B_{b_z})⊗2^{b_y+b_x}δ + B_{b_z}⊗(2^{b_y}δ − B_{b_y})⊗2^{b_x}δ + B_{b_z}⊗B_{b_y}⊗(2^{b_x}δ − B_{b_x}) (theory: exact algebra, not built as a pass).
- **Parity.** 2δ − [1 1] = [1 −1], a first difference, centred half a voxel off. An odd background order puts the subtracted term half a voxel from the kept one, and an odd smooth order moves both by the same half voxel. That is ruling (a): refuse odd b, hold odd s with its offset.

The positive set is P(x) = [R(x) > 0].

### A2. The component tree (M3): `max_tree`

- c(x) is the dense rank of R(x) among the admitted voxels. Each tree node n has a level λ(n); ν(x) is x's own node, the node at level c(x) that holds x. The component at level r holding x is the ancestor a of ν(x) with λ(a) ≥ r > λ(parent(a)). Its voxels are exactly those x′ with ν(x′) in a's subtree.
- Number the nodes in DFS order; a's subtree is one range [in(a), out(a)).
- **Probes by LCA.** Two voxels share a component at level r exactly when r ≤ λ(LCA(ν(x), ν(x′))). Sort k probes by in(ν). The partition at every level at once is given by the k − 1 levels λ(LCA(p_i, p_{i+1})) of neighbors in that order: at level r, neighbors i, i + 1 are together exactly when r ≤ that level, and a probe whose own level is below r is absent. The slide's bisection over levels becomes k − 1 LCA queries (theory).
- **The overlap at every level from one count.** For a lag v, the own-node pair table J(v)[n, n′] = |{ x : ν_t(x) = n, P_{t+1}(x + v), ν_{t+1}(x + v) = n′ }|. Then for components A at level r of t and B at level r′ of t + 1:

  `C_{r,r′}[A, B](v)` = Σ_{n ∈ sub(A)} Σ_{n′ ∈ sub(B)} J(v)[n, n′]

  a rectangle sum over the two DFS ranges; one summed-area table of J answers every pair of levels. The census (one partner, unpaired, mutual, forked) is the degrees of the support of C at the chosen levels. Today's overlap recounts per sampled cut (theory).

### A3. The moments (M4)

With own-node moments μ(n) = Σ_{ν(x)=n} (1, x, x xᵀ), every node's moments are Σ_{n′ ∈ sub(n)} μ(n′): one prefix over the DFS order gives every component at every level. The centred second moment is K = m M − S Sᵀ, carried with its mass, never divided.

### A4. The correlation operator (M5)

For frames t, t′, a shift v, a label a of t and a label b of t′ (or ∅, a positive voxel under no label):

  `C_{t,t′}[a, b](v)` = Σ_x [L_t(x) = a] · [P_{t′}(x + v)] · [L_{t′}(x + v) = b]

With a weight φ(x) ∈ {1, x, x xᵀ} at v = 0, t′ = t and b = a, the same sum gives the moments. Its reductions:
- the argmax over v of the sum over all labels (`shift_agreement`);
- a local argmax per label from a start lag (`climb_machine`);
- a transpose: `C_{t′,t}[b, a](−v)` = `C_{t,t′}[a, b](v)`;
- box sums over nested widths around a lag, from one summed-area table over v (the core: each width is the box on top of the one below);
- the autocorrelation of a box, a tent, over one frame's own C (the contact form).

The labels may be any level's components: with A2, C at every pair of levels is a rectangle sum of J. So the level is one more axis of the same box, and all of the tree's frame-to-frame reading is C over (v, r, r′), counted once.

### A5. The marginal (M6): `marginal`

For sources S and targets T with integer weights ω(s, t) and a null weight ω(s, ∅) per source, an arrangement α maps each source to a target or ∅, no two sources to one target:

  Z = Σ_α Π_s ω(s, α(s)),  Z_{s→t} the same over α(s) = t,  P(s → t) = Z_{s→t} / Z

Z is the permanent of the |S| × (|T| + |S|) matrix [ω(s, t) | diag ω(s, ∅)]. **It factors over the connected components of the support graph** (s ~ t where ω(s, t) ≠ 0): the only coupling between sources is a shared target; the sum over all arrangements is the product of the sums over components, and Z_{s→t} / Z over s's component is the global ratio exactly. A local set smaller than the component is exact only when it is the component. The cost lives in the component's size; a permanent is hard in general; the component's size is what to measure and report (theory).

### A6. Matching (M7): `heaviest_matching`

The one-to-one set of largest total weight on exact integer costs; no weight is scaled or compared as a fraction.

### A7. The entropy history (M8)

For voxel x, bit j and frames 0 … F − 1, with f_j(x) the number of transitions where bit j flips: f_j(x) ≡ bit j of (I_0(x) ⊕ I_{F−1}(x)) (mod 2). A voxel where this fails means the history was not taken whole; it is the pass's own proof. The windows partition the transitions; their width is a symbol (see the audit).

### A8. The files (M9)

Integer lifting (predict, then update) takes each step's integer quotient by a power of two, and the inverse step subtracts the identical quotient, computed from the same integers; each step is a bijection on integers and nothing is lost: the tower rebuilds every voxel. The proof is not the algebra but the read-back: every sample is rebuilt from the file on the device and compared voxel for voxel, and again from a second read of the source pixel for pixel.

### A9. The golden ladder (M11)

β(n) = the number of Fibonacci rungs at or below n after the first, over the rungs that fit 64 bits.

### A10. The seal (M13): `obsignatio`

Each level ℓ keys its nodes: K_ℓ = BLAKE3-derive-key-context("obsignatio aeterna 2026-09-23 " ‖ ℓ), H_ℓ(m) = BLAKE3-keyed(K_ℓ, m). For a sample of extent (T, Z, Y, X):

  ρ(t, z, y) = H_row(I[t, z, y, ·]),  π(t, z) = H_plane(ρ(t, z, ·)),  υ(t) = H_volume(π(t, ·)),  Λ = H_lanes(υ(·))
  κ(c) = H_chunk(LE64(ℓ_c) ‖ bits_c),  Σ = H_stream(offsets ‖ B ‖ κ(·))
  σ = H_side(H_side stored(deflated) ‖ H_side inflated(inflated)),  μ = H_members(tables ‖ names)
  Ω = H_sample(head ‖ Λ ‖ Σ ‖ σ ‖ μ),  Θ = H_set(Ω_1 ‖ … ‖ Ω_n)

- **Locality.** A change at (t, z, y, x) changes exactly ρ(t, z, y), π(t, z), υ(t), Λ, Ω and Θ. A change in chunk c changes exactly κ(c), Σ, Ω and Θ. Any other node agrees except with probability 2^−256.
- **Size is strength.** A k-bit check misses a random corruption with probability 2^−k, whatever the function. The DAG's gain over CRC-64 is structure: nonlinearity, locality and composition, not a smaller word.
- **The tree is BLAKE3's.** Pairing each level and carrying the odd node up is BLAKE3's left-heavy tree exactly (its left subtree is the largest power of two of chunks below the whole); the level-parallel device fold equals the host's stack fold.
- **Where it stops.** The tower lifts across all four axes; a decoded row can be checked only after a whole decode. The stored bytes check alone.
- **The shared shape.** The seal folds the lattice x, y, z, t, as the tower's floors lift it and the moments' prefix sums it (A3). The residual, the tree, C and the seal are each one lattice read once with a different combining operator. The seal's operator alone is not associative; its tree shape is fixed, and that fixed shape makes a mismatch locatable (observation).

### A11. The scheduler (M14): `tessera`

The device holds C bytes; U(τ) is in use. Each admitted job j has a declaration d_j, a reservation r_j (from d_j, only growing) and a measured use u_j(τ) by pid:

  O(τ) = U(τ) − Σ_j u_j(τ),  H(τ) = C − O(τ) − Σ_j max(r_j, u_j(τ))

- **Admission:** k is admitted when d_k ≤ H(τ); admission alone keeps H ≥ 0. Doug, 24 September: "We reserve what they ask for and then if it cost less we remember that for next time". The code in the working tree reserves max(d_k, p_σ) instead (`tessera_ledger_wants`), which Doug did not approve (M14).
- **Growth:** u_j(τ) > r_j sets r_j = u_j(τ) and warns. Only growth and outside processes drive H negative, and both are measured; the books equal C − H at every sweep.
- **Memory of a request:** p_σ = max over a run of u_j(τ), kept under the request's signum σ. It is a measurement at the sweep's resolution, not a byte-exact constant: identical requests have read peaks 4,194,304 bytes apart (M14). The engine is deterministic in its request and its result, not in the bytes a sweep samples. A new declaration d > p_σ is over budget: asked, held, overridable.
- **Liveness:** a ticket is (pid, start time); a reused pid is a different process. A closed socket or a gone process releases r_j at once.
- **Backfill with the head kept:** the history keeps (p_σ, t_σ). The head's shadow S is the least time with H + Σ_{e_j ≤ S} max(r_j, u_j) ≥ d_h, and the spare is X = that sum − d_h. A job k behind the head goes now if d_k ≤ H and (now + t_σk ≤ S or d_k ≤ X). A signum with no history is never backfilled.
- **Time:** every deadline sits in one min-heap keyed by time to change; the root is the next event, handled in order.

### A12. The period reading (M18): `period_read`

On an axis of extent n with the other axes holding M = N/n voxels, take L = ⌊n/2⌋ lags and the start positions whose coordinate on the axis is below n − L. Every lag ℓ ≤ L then compares the same Q = (n − L)·M pairs:

  a(ℓ) = #{x : I(x) = I(x + ℓ·e)},  r(ℓ) = a(ℓ)/Q

- **The local peak** (Doug, 23 September). A candidate p must stand above both its neighbor lags, and so must 2p:

  a(p) > max(a(p − 1), a(p + 1))  and  a(2p) > max(a(2p − 1), a(2p + 1))

  Its height is the smaller rise, h(p) = min(a(p) − max(a(p − 1), a(p + 1)), a(2p) − max(a(2p − 1), a(2p + 1))), an integer count over the axis's Q pairs, and its margin is the rational h(p)/Q. The candidates run over 2 ≤ p with 2p + 1 ≤ L; P ≤ ⌊(L − 1)/2⌋: 8 on an axis of 34 slices (L = 17), 5 on 24, 4 on 20.
- **The floor: a drawn null per axis** (Doug, 23 September). The request names d draws. For axis a, draw k permutes each line along a on its own by π_k; the shuffled lattice I∘π_k holds exactly the same values on every line, its histogram is the original's, and every other axis keeps its structure; only a's own order is destroyed. Each draw reads a's strongest height, h_k = max_p h_{I∘π_k}(p); the draws that reach a peak make a's band, sorted exactly. A read of rank r makes r·d shuffles, one per axis per draw.
- **The choice: the fundamental** (Doug, 23 September). P is the smallest candidate whose height clears the band's top:

  P = min { p : h(p)/Q > max_k h_k/Q }

  with each comparison a 128-bit cross-multiply. A period P also peaks at 2P and 3P with heights matching P's; the strongest peak picked a multiple; the smallest peak above the top picks P. An empty band, where no draw reached a peak, or a given top of 0 lets any peak through; the smallest peak is P. When none clears, P = 0 and the reported candidate is the strongest peak (the largest height, the smallest p on a tie). A lag that makes no peak at p and 2p is never a candidate. Nothing is rounded, and no constant is written in.
- **The fundamental leaves the rate alone.** Some peak clears the top exactly when the strongest peak does; an axis reads a period under the fundamental exactly when it did under the strongest peak; the rule changes which period is read, not whether one is (theory).
- **The set-wide band** (Doug, 23 September). The knee's first ruling was 73,158 draws a series, so that fewer than one false period is expected over the set's 24,386 series × 3 axes = 73,158 axes. **Measured:** 152 ms a draw on 34 × 960 × 960 (RTX 3070), about 3.1 hours a series and about 75,000 GPU-hours for the set. So one band serves the whole set. The 152 ms was the global shuffle's, one shuffle and one count a draw. The per-axis null makes one of each per axis, and it is **measured** (23 September, RTX 3070, on 34 × 960 × 960 = 31,334,400 voxels, the same kind of lattice: a smooth ramp x + y + 2z plus uniform noise 0 to 63). `period_draw` takes 455.9 ms a call, the mean of 8 after a warm-up; a call is one count of the original, then a shuffle and a count on each of the 3 axes. `period_read` takes 1,934.2 ms at 4 draws and 9,136.5 ms at 20; a marginal draw costs 450.1 ms and the fixed cost is 133.7 ms (each as the run printed it from the unrounded times). That is 2.96 times the global shuffle's 152 ms, as three shuffle-and-count passes against one would give. From the measured draw, not a measured set run: about 9.1 hours a series (3.1 before) and about 223,000 GPU-hours for the 24,386 series (75,000 before). So one band serves the whole set the more. Its limits: one run on one lattice; host wall-clock with the syncs; and `period_draw` allocates its buffers on every call; part of the 455.9 ms may be allocation (not separated). The timing program was a scratch program, not a repo test. `period_draw` shuffles each axis once along its own lines, keyed by the content, the draw number and the axis, and returns each axis's strongest height (0 over Q where no peak is reached). The program calls it across its series, keeps the top per axis, and passes those tops to `period_read` as `null_top` with no draws of its own; a given top with draws is a request error. P is the smallest peak above the given top, and a top of 0 lets any peak through. The tops compare exactly across shapes, because the cross-multiply carries each Q. Whether one band is fair across shapes is Doug's call.
- **The false-period rate.** With no structure along axis a, each line's order along a is one more arrangement of its values, whatever the other axes carry, and if the draws behave as uniform shuffles the d + 1 heights are exchangeable (theory). The lattice's own strongest height then stands strictly above all d others with probability at most 1/(d + 1), exactly that when no two tie. The caller sets the rate with d: 1/9 at 8 draws, 1% at 99. **Measured,** 5 of 192 random axes at 8 draws (2.6%, M18). With a period 5 planted along x at 1,024 e, the unplanted z and y read a period on 5 of 64 at 8 draws (against 64/9) and 2 of 64 at 19 draws (against 64/20) (`period_power`, M19): the per-axis null holds the bound when another axis carries a strong period.
- **Measured: the fundamental is read** (`period_power`, M19). A period 5 is planted along x of 32 × 64 × 64 under the camera law, at amplitudes 0 to 1,024 e, 32 volumes each. At 8 draws x reads 5, a multiple (10 or 15), or another lag: 10, 6, 0 at 16 e; 26, 3, 0 at 32 e; 32, 0, 0 at 64 and 128 e; 31, 1, 0 at 256 e; 32, 0, 0 at 1,024 e. At 19 draws it reads 5 on 32 of 32 from 64 e up.
- **The strongest peak (superseded, Doug, 23 September): it read a multiple.** The candidate was the largest height. Once the plant was found, the reading landed on a multiple, 10 or 15, 103 times in 156 at 8 draws from 32 e up, and at 32 e read 5 on 8 of 32 and a multiple on 20 (**measured**, `period_power`). The multiples' peaks are as high as P's; the noise picked among them.
- **The global shuffle (superseded, Doug, 23 September): it read a period where another axis carried one.** One Feistel shuffle of the whole index per draw served every axis. With the plant at 1,024 e along x, the unplanted z and y read a period on 23 of 64 at 8 draws (36%, against 1/9) and on 10 of 64 at 19 draws (15.6%, against 1/20) (**measured**, `period_power`). The explanation, not measured (the agreement levels were not printed): the global shuffle destroyed x's structure too; the null's agreement fell below the live lattice's at the lags of z and y, and the exchangeability the false-period rate rests on failed there.
- **The margin rule (superseded, Doug, 23 September).** For a candidate p with 2p ≤ L, take the lags that are not multiples of p: B of them, with A = Σ a(ℓ) over them (the total less the multiples). The margin was m(p) = (a(p) + a(2p))/(2Q) − A/(B·Q) = (B·(a(p) + a(2p)) − 2A) / (2·B·Q), the largest positive margin the candidate. A smooth lattice agrees more at every short lag than at the long ones; the rule read smoothness as a period: a smooth ramp with noise read P = 2 or 3 on every axis (the 34 × 960 × 960 timing run, **measured**). Under the drawn null it passed 21 of 192 random axes at 8 draws (10.9%).
- **Why chance could not do this.** The old floor was m ≥ Σc²/N². Σc² is unchanged when the values are rearranged; a floor built from it cannot tell structure from a rearrangement of the same values, and uniform random data passed 63 of 192 axes (33%). The drawn null keeps the histogram and Σc² exactly and changes only the arrangement, the thing a period is. Σc² is still reported, as `collisions`.
- **The shuffle.** A keyed Fisher–Yates on each line along the axis, one thread a line: for step s from n − 1 down to 1, the element at s swaps with the one at h mod (s + 1), h a 32-bit hash of the line index and s under four keys (`PERIOD_ROUNDS`). Each key is the content signum's words mixed with c · 4 + round, for the counter c = draw · rank + axis. The seed is the lattice's content; the same content draws the same band. Superseded: a Feistel network on the whole index, 4 rounds, with cycle-walking to stay below N (the global shuffle).

### A13. The record machine (M10): `keymath`, `key_schedule`, `cycle`

A record program is a list of steps s_1, …, s_n over registers of limbs, each step reading earlier registers and writing its own, run for every record in one sweep.

- **The steps are n** (Doug, 23 September: "increase the scheduler steps to n"; 24 September: stack the floors, "make it so").
  - The step count has no bound. `ENGINE_RECORD_STEPS_MAX` is gone: it sized a per-thread array holding one sign per step, and each sign now sits beside its register in the file.
  - The binding resource is the file: `ENGINE_RECORD_LIMBS_MOST` = 256 limbs of live registers.
  - A stack of floors runs as one program in one launch. Each floor is a round that reads the floor below and leaves its state, often wrapped, for the next.
  - The layout frees each register in a single pass, bucketed by its last reader. It frees the same registers at the same steps as the per-step scan it replaced.
  - The algebra of the stack, what it compresses and what it leaves, with the stacked-against-chained measurement (13 to 22 times, records equal): [vertical_time_compression.md](vertical_time_compression.md).
  - **Proved:** 700 floors of a round over four 32-bit words (xor, and, sum, xor with a constant, a 32-bit wrap), 4,204 steps, run in an 8-limb file. On 1,024 lanes the device equals the host word for word, and both equal the same rounds on the CPU's own 64-bit two's complement at every tapped floor (`test/engine/record_bitwise_test`).
- **Xor, and, and the two's complement wrap** (Doug, 24 September).
  - `ENGINE_RECORD_XOR` and `_AND` work bitwise on each operand's two's complement, sign-extended without end, one bit wider than the wider operand. The imprint narrows that where it can show a register is never negative. An and with such a register is no wider than it, and an xor of two such is no wider than the wider. The result's sign is read from the operands' signs.
  - `ENGINE_RECORD_WRAP` takes left modulo 2^w, read back signed, with w ≥ 4 in `right`.
  - **Proved:**
    - Every output bit is rebuilt from the raw fields one bit at a time, and each sign still holds 64 bits past its written width. This holds on 4,096 lanes in the 64-limb file and 512 in the 256-limb file.
    - Hand-worked answers match.
    - The narrowed widths are exactly the rule's: 32, 20, 20, 32, 41, 41, 32 and 32 over unsigned 32- and 20-bit fields and a signed 40-bit field. All 4,096 lanes equal the oracle past every narrowed width.
    - A wrap below 4 bits is refused at imprint (`test/engine/record_bitwise_test`, 24 checks, 0 failed).
- **Register reuse** (opt-in, 23 September). Register r is live from the step that writes it to its last reader. With reuse on, a liveness free-list returns r to the file once its last reader has run; a chain needs only the registers live at once, not one per step. **Proved** on a 21-step additive chain: the same output from 3 limbs as from 21. Off by default; a program that does not ask keeps one register per step: each register placed at the running total of the limbs before it, the same bump as before; a program with no tables keeps the same layout as before, by construction (not measured against the old binary).
- **The lookup table** (Doug, 23 September: "implement the lut"). A table step reads the low b bits of one register, b ≤ 32, and returns T[x mod 2^b], an `out_bits`-wide value, from 2^b rows of ⌈out_bits/32⌉ limbs. It is a general one-variable function on the lane's alphabet: 2^16 entries for a u16 lane, built once by pushing every value through. **Proved:** filled by running the ordinary operations over the whole 16-bit alphabet, the table equals them on 4,096 lanes, on the device and the host (M10).
- **Composition keeps the order.** Reading g through f gives the table (g∘f)[x] = g[f[x]], one table for the chain: the floor is 2-D, but the operations need not be, for a chain of them "gets collapsed in order onto it" (Doug, 23 September). Composition regroups, (h∘g)∘f = h∘(g∘f), but does not reorder: g∘f ≠ f∘g. **Proved** with f(x) = 65535 − x and g(y) = \|y − 30000\|. Regrouping: g read through f equals the ops for \|35535 − x\|, and one table composed on the host equals the two. No reordering: f∘g differs on some lane.
- **The two towers** (Doug, 24 September: a second tower over the first one's boundary, inverted, and the two collapse together).
  - Floor division is a record floor: ⌊v / 2^k⌋ = EXACT_QUOTIENT(v − AND(v, 2^k − 1), 2^k), rounding toward −∞ for every integer v, as `tower_floor_shift` does.
  - With it, the crystal's 5/3 lifting T (A14) and its inverse are sums, differences, constants and that division: stacks of record floors. An operation program F over the crystal and T⁻¹ compose into one stack F ∘ T⁻¹ by the regrouping law.
  - **Proved:** one 5/3 level over 8 signed 16-bit samples and its inverse, 129 steps in one program and a 12-limb file. On 4,096 lanes the device equals the host word for word, the forward floor equals tower.cu's formulas, and the inverse returns every sample exactly (`test/engine/record_bitwise_test`).
  - Open: the neighbor gather across levels, what F ∘ T⁻¹ saves, and the whole crystal as one stack ([vertical_time_compression.md](vertical_time_compression.md)).
- **The full Kolmogorov–Arnold form.** With linear steps only, the machine held the theorem's skeleton with linear edges; the table gives it general one-variable edges, which is item 1 of [kolmogorov_arnold.md](kolmogorov_arnold.md) and the LUT of [noise_sieve_tower.md](noise_sieve_tower.md) §2. Which functions to tabulate, without fitting, is still open (item 3 there).

### A14. The root universal (M20): `tower`

The tower and the table are separate objects; laid together they are one (Doug, 23 September). Let W_k be the approximation on floor k, L_k level k's lifting, and E_k the edges laid on floor k.

- **The edge is a bijection.** E(w) = (w with its low b bits x replaced by π(x)), π a permutation of [0, 2^b), the high bits passed through. So E is a bijection on the coefficient word and E⁻¹ uses π⁻¹ (proved by construction; the round trips below are the check).
- **The order.** Lift is EN ∘ L(N−1) ∘ … ∘ E1 ∘ L0 ∘ E0, and lower is its inverse, E0⁻¹ ∘ L0⁻¹ ∘ … ∘ EN⁻¹. The edges on one floor are a stack, undone last laid first. **Proved:** every edge program in `tower_edge_test` and `root_universal` rebuilds the exact lanes, 171 random edges on 48 volumes through the whole crystal path.
- **The fold.** Two edges on one floor are one edge: π_b ∘ π_a, laid as one table, lays the same crystal. It keeps the order, since π_a ∘ π_b lays another, and a lifting level between two edges blocks the fold. **Proved** on 8 volumes each.
- **What it is** (theory, Doug's framing). The tower is the Kolmogorov–Arnold form's additive skeleton, the table its one-variable edge, and with edges between the floors the collapse is an outer sum with nonlinear edges.
- **Its two bounds** (theory; kept). It is universal over the finite coefficient alphabet: every function of the indexed bits is one table, exactly, not approximately. It is not the continuous-function theorem of Kolmogorov and Arnold. And inside the reversible stream every edge is a permutation; the second bound is one of width, not of kind (below).
- **The dimensional expansion** (Doug, 23 September: "reaching a boundary here means we need a dimensional expansion to realize the transform"). A function f that is not a bijection (\|x\|, a comparison) rides the stream through the Bennett embedding, (x, y) → (x, y ⊕ f(x)): a permutation of the pair for any f, since y comes back as the carrier ⊕ f(x) (proved by construction). The price is width: a permutation takes b_x bits, any f takes b_x + b_y, within the 20-bit limit. No engine change; the tables are built on the host. **Proved** by `tower_edge_test`, 25 checks, 0 failed. \|x\| of an 8-bit two's complement field and x ≥ 100, each a 16-bit Bennett edge (8 bits of x, 8 of carrier) on the collapsed floor of 1 × 8 × 8 × 8, alternate over 64 lattices: they round-trip on 64 of 64; f(x) reads out of the carrier (x kept, the carrier moved by exactly f(x), the high bits passed) on 64 of 64; the edge touches only its one coefficient on 64 of 64. The two edges on floors 1 and 2, where lifting mixes their outputs, round-trip. \|x\| laid bare on 16 bits, not embedded, is refused, since x and −x collide. The quantum forms of the same expansion are standard theorems (theory here): Naimark (an ask is a projective measurement in a larger space), Stinespring (a transform is a unitary in a larger space, then a discard) and purification (a mixed state is part of a pure state in dimension d²); the ask is A15.
- **The price** (measured, M20). An unfitted edge is exact but not free. On floor 0, where every coefficient is, a random 12-bit permutation raises the coded bits from 6.802 to 12.608 a voxel; on the collapsed floor, one coefficient, the cost does not show at three decimals at any width. The reading, not measured: a random permutation of the low bits sends small magnitudes to large ones, and the magnitude coder pays for each; the cost scales with the coefficients the edge touches. Whether a fitted or data-named edge lowers the bits, not only raises them, is not measured.

### A15. The ask and the state (M21): `ask_state`

The terms are M21's (Doug, 23 September). The idea is standard: QBism (Fuchs and Schack) and the SIC-POVMs.

- **The ask returns the state.** For a qubit and E_i = (I + v_i·σ)/4, the answers are p_i = (1 + r·v_i)/4. With Σ v_i = 0 and Σ v_i v_iᵀ = c·I, Σ p_i v_i = (c/4) r; r = (4/c) Σ p_i v_i and the state is read back from the answers alone (theory; **proved** on 25 states for each ask, c = 1 and c = 4/3). A complete ask needs at least d² outcomes, here 4 (theory, standard).
- **The phase falls out of the ask.** The equator states differ only in phase: each answers ½, ½ to the 0/1 ask, which sees magnitudes only. The complete ask tells all 91 pairs of the 14 apart (**proved**). So the phase is not lost; one basis's ask cannot see it, and a complete ask holds it.
- **The crossing rule.** The chance of + on axis a is (1 + r_a)/2 = Σ p_i (½ + (2/c) v_{i,a}), a fixed linear map from the complete ask's answers (theory; **proved**, 75 of 75 for each ask). Its weights onto +x are 3/2, 3/2, −1/2, −1/2 for the rational ask and ½ ± ½√3 for the SIC. A negative weight is what classical total probability never has, and the difference is interference.
- **The valid set.** A distribution is a state exactly when \|r\|² ≤ 1. (1, 0, 0, 0) gives \|r\|² = 12 for the rational ask and 9 for the SIC; it is refused, and it crosses onto +x at 3/2 and ½ + ½√3, no probabilities (**proved**). On the 455 distributions with denominator 12 the valid set is 55 (rational) and 87 (SIC), and 104 and 72 more pass the three axis crossings without being states (**measured**): the valid set is stricter than the axis crossings.
- **Exact numbers.** Every value is exact: rationals on the exact integer, and the SIC's √3 carried by its relation, (a + b√3)(c + d√3) = (ac + 3bd) + (ad + bc)√3, with the sign of a + b√3 read from a² against 3b², never equal unless both are 0, since √3 is irrational (proved by construction). The SIC's answers cross into the rational ask's exactly, 25 of 25 (**proved**).
- **The dimensional expansion** (theory, standard theorems). Naimark: an ask is a projective measurement in a larger space. Stinespring: a transform is a unitary in a larger space, then a discard. Purification: a mixed state is part of a pure state in dimension d². The engine's form is the Bennett embedding of A14, proved.
- **Doug's statements** (his; the first built in `ka_psi`, the rest theory). "The exp is symbolic after the second nth exp": ψ's terms γ^−β(r), with β(r) = (nʳ − 1)/(n − 1), are carried as a digit and an exact integer exponent, a sparse form whose cost is polynomial in the scale, not the expanded digits. `ka_psi` carries ψ this way to β(60) = 1,152,921,504,606,846,975 (built; 152 checks, 0 failed). ψ is the inner function of Sprecher's version of the superposition theorem, as J. Braun and M. Griebel state it ("On a constructive proof of Kolmogorov's superposition theorem", Constructive Approximation 30(3) (2009) 653–675, doi:10.1007/s00365-009-9054-2, the record checked at Crossref; the preprint read, pages 1 to 5). Their Theorem 2.1 takes integers n ≥ 2, m ≥ 2n, γ ≥ m + 2 and a = [γ(γ − 1)]⁻¹, and writes f(x) = Σ_{q=0}^{m} Φ_q ∘ ξ(x_q), with ξ(x_q) = Σ_{p=1}^{n} α_p ψ(x_p + qa), α_1 = 1 and α_p = Σ_{r≥1} γ^−(p−1)β(r). ψ is defined on the terminating base-γ rationals D_k = { Σ_{r≤k} i_r γ^−r } (their 2.2), and in Köppen's form (their 2.7) level k adds its digit at the weight γ^−β(k). So γ^−β(r) is the γ-adic weight of level r. What ψ is, strictly increasing and continuous in Köppen's form and not so in Sprecher's, is proved exactly by the sim `ka_psi` (M19), to depth 60 with the exponent never expanded, in "Kolmogorov's inner function, exactly" of [kolmogorov_arnold.md](kolmogorov_arnold.md). Its "Separation" part finds the paper's Lemma 3.4 false at k = 1 and restores Lemmas 3.7 and 3.8 at every k with a corrected bound and a ramp narrowed by γ². The outer functions' contraction is not built. "If we can represent a two-sqrt product as two ints and their words of math operations then that is countable": the numbers named by finitely many integers and a finite word of operations (the algebraic, the computable, π as its program) are countable, and the exact storage holds each of them. And "Pi compresses to 1kb": π as a spigot program.
