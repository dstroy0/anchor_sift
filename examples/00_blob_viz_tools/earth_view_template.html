Hashrate by Longitude

A daily cycle in UTC is a statement about geography. Every miner follows its own local clock, so hashrate spread evenly around the planet cancels to nothing in UTC — a daily signal exists only where the distribution is lopsided. Twenty-four hours close a circle and so do 360 degrees, so this dial is the planet seen down its own axis, with block count drawn as radius.

The dip's local hour is a control, not a constant, because turning an hour into a longitude needs a mechanism nobody has proved. Electricity peaks in the local afternoon, so curtailment should sit near local 16:00 — but a maintenance window would not, and a cheap-power surge would sit opposite. Move the control and the inferred longitude rotates; that rotation is the assumption, made visible instead of buried in the arithmetic. Reference longitudes are drawn as labels only and are never fitted. Count and mean interval are the same statement read two ways: within a difficulty epoch, arrival rate is proportional to running hashrate, so the two curves must oppose each other or the reading is wrong.
