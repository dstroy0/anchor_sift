Combed Spiral

SHA-256's 256 output bit positions, placed on the golden spiral — index k at height 1−2(k+0.5)/256, longitude kγ — so there is no seam and no pole pile, and a cluster on the surface is a cluster in the data. Size is pooled deviation, colour is how many arms agree on the sign. Those answer different questions: depth says how large, arms say whether it is anything at all.

Arms cost nothing to keep — the counters are 256 numbers whatever the depth — but the two things they buy scale differently. Pooled depth improves as √k; agreement improves as 2ᵏ, because each further arm halves the chance a run of agreeing signs is coincidence. At this arm count a position agreeing unanimously would happen by chance — times over 256 positions, so the exponential axis has already saturated: past here, more arms buy depth only. No position is both unanimous and past four standard errors, which is what a flat construction looks like when you can finally see it clearly enough to say so.
