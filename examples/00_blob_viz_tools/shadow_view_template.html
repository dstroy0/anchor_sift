Shadows of SHA-256

The object is three-dimensional and has 8.4 million cells, so nobody looks at it directly. Every reading is a shadow — the field collapsed along one axis. The residue fold is one shadow; the chi-square is the same object flattened all the way to a single number. These are the four shadows this instrument can cast, each measured at 218 message pairs per cell with an independent seed at every round.

How to read it. Colour is the signed deviation in standard errors: blue below chance, orange above, and the panel color is exactly zero, so a shadow that shows nothing is showing nothing. In the residue view the ridge sits at class 26 and loses one thirty-second of its amplitude per round from round 12, holding to four decimal places for five rounds before the knee. It is gone by round 23, and past that the whole field is white noise at every lag in both axes. Nothing in these shadows walks: a Doppler scan built to detect drift reads |drift| ≤ 0.09 at all fifteen spatial frequencies, against a positive control it recovers at 131σ.
