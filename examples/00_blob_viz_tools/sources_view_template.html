SHA-256 Against Noise

Every null in this research is a number. This is the same question asked by looking: put the measured field beside a matrix that is pseudorandom by construction — same kernel shape, same tally, same denominator, compression replaced by random words — and see whether they can be told apart. Anything the two share is the harness. Anything only one has belongs to that source.

Both panels share one color scale, taken from whichever side reaches further, so the comparison is fair and a quiet field looks quiet rather than being stretched to fill the ramp. The statistic under each is the root-mean-square deviation across the whole field in standard errors: 1.00 is exactly what a binomial matrix gives, so a source reading near one is indistinguishable from noise by that measure however it looks. The residue projection is signed, blue below chance and orange above; the bit projections are power and cannot be negative, which is a limitation of that readout rather than of the field.
