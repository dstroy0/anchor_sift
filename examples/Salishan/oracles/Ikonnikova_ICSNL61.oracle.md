# Ikonnikova_ICSNL61.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of Specific Features of Salish Toponyms by Olga Ikonnikova, Taganrog Institute of
Management and Economics, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

The paper's thirteen examples are place names drawn from dictionaries. §1 names the source for each
language: Halkomelem from Galloway 1993, Coeur d’Alene from Reichard 1934, Lushootseed from Bates et
al. 1994, Northern Straits (Lummi) from Gibbs 1863, Shuswap from Kuipers 1974 and Thompson from
Thompson & Thompson 1996. It names no source for Colville-Okanagan, the language of (1) and (10).
It also names Lillooet, from van Eijk 1997, 2013, and gives no Lillooet example. The paper names
no speaker, and this table supplies none.

The who for each place name and each word or affix of an etymology is its language. The who for the
English of an example is the work §1 names for that language, as the paper cites it. For the
Colville-Okanagan English it is Olga Ikonnikova, because the paper names no source. The prose, the
labels and the notes on the page carry Olga Ikonnikova. The paragraphs quote Superanskaya 1973,
Matveev 1987 and Berezovich 1998 and 1991 in English, and the gloss of each row names the quotation.

THE LETTERS

The text layer holds two glottal stops. (1a) has ɂ, U+0242 LATIN SMALL LETTER GLOTTAL STOP, and the
page draws it at x-height. Every other example has ʔ, U+0294, which the page draws taller. It also
holds two schwas: ǝ, U+01DD, in (4a), (5a) and (6a), and ə, U+0259, in (11a). The page draws the two
schwas the same, and the table follows the text layer for both. The raised w of the
Colville-Okanagan, Thompson and Lushootseed forms is ʷ, U+02B7, in the text layer and on the page.
The Halkomelem forms are written in a practical spelling with kw and xw on the line, a colon for
length and straight apostrophes, U+0027, in Xwth'kw'em, Ts'simtelo:t and S'oyewot. Symbol note rows
give each of these.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the reader normalizes the text layer to
NFC before it compares. The text layer holds the proceedings line of page 1 ahead of the title. It
also sets spaces inside words and before punctuation that the page does not print: or onyms,
objec ts, L illooet, Finno -Ugric, = elik and 1863 ). The table writes each form as the page prints
it.

Every form was looked up in the text layer, and every word of the text layer carrying a mark
outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 223 forms, leaving out the 12 notation and symbol note
rows, 27 are not found verbatim. With all white space removed from both sides, 26 of them are found.
The one left is the second bullet point of §3, which runs from page 4 onto page 5, and the text
layer sets the page number 168 inside it. Of 44 words in the text layer that still carry a mark
outside ASCII once quotes and brackets are stripped from their ends, 2 distinct words are not in the
form column: elik‘creative, which the page sets with the quote against the word, and the bullet •,
U+2022, which the table leaves off three bullet points. The English glosses of the etymologies sit
in the gloss column, with no quote marks.

The page has one error with a damage row, Whashington in (3a), transcribed as printed. Notation rows
record the rest. §1 cites Galloway 1993, Reichard 1934 and van Eijk 1997, 2013, and the reference
list has Galloway 2009, Reichard 1933-1938 and a van Eijk entry with no year. Bates et al. 1994 has
no entry. The prose writes Matveyev and the citation Matveev. The Halkomelem examples write Frazer
River and (5a) and (12b) Fraser. The Cheam sisters are spelled two ways each. Unclosed quotes and
parentheses are noted on the rows that carry them.
