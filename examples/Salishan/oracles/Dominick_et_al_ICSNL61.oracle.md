# Dominick_et_al_ICSNL61.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of payɛčxʷʊt yɛχat θ qʷaytən: Always Remember Your Language by q̓ami Phyllis
Dominick, Henry Davis, Laura Griffin, Marianne Huijsmans, Gloria Mellesmoen, D. K. E. Reisinger,
Bailey Trotter and Kaining Xu, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

The story is q̓ami Phyllis Dominick's. Kaining Xu recorded it on May 27, 2018 in St. Paul's
Hospital, Vancouver, and the paper prints it four times. §4.1 is Phyllis's own handwritten English
version, typed to keep her spelling. §4.2 is the story as she told it in ʔayʔaǰuθəm, with English
passages of her own set in italics. §4.3 is the authors' English translation, with her English
passages kept. §4.4 sets each ʔayʔaǰuθəm sentence as a numbered example, (1) to (35), with a
segmentation, a gloss and a translation, and her English passages as unnumbered lines.

The who for each form is its language, ʔayʔaǰuθəm. The who for §4.1 and for each of Phyllis's
English passages in §4.2 to §4.4 is q̓ami Phyllis Dominick. The segmentations, glosses and
translations are the authors', and so are the prose, the footnotes and the reference list; those
rows carry the seven other authors. The title and name rows carry all eight.

THE LETTERS

The orthography writes the open e as ɛ, U+025B, the iota ɩ, U+0269, the upsilon ʊ, U+028A, and the
uvular fricative as Greek chi, χ, U+03C7. The segmentation tiers write the uvular as x with a
combining caron, x̌, and a null morpheme as Ø, U+00D8, twenty-two times. The glottalizing comma is
U+0313 COMBINING COMMA ABOVE, on q̓, p̓, k̓ʷ, ƛ̓, č̓, t̓ᶿ, and the n̓ and m̓ of
tan̓ and nam̓. The page raises the w of a labialized
consonant and the theta of tᶿ, and the text layer holds them as the modifier letters ʷ, U+02B7, and
ᶿ, U+1DBF. The table keeps each code point as the text layer has it.

On page 3 the comma of ƛ̓ in ƛ̓oχʷɛt and χaƛ̓namapɛč does not show above the letter, and marks of the
same shape sit under letters of the line above. The text layer holds U+0313 on each ƛ, and the
table follows it. A symbol note row gives the places.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the reader normalizes the text layer
to NFC before it compares. The text layer holds the proceedings line of page 1 ahead of the title.
Where a segmentation of §4.4 runs to a second line, the text layer holds the segmentation and
gloss lines interleaved, and the table joins each tier.

Every form was looked up in the text layer, both sides in NFC, and every word of the text layer
carrying a mark outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 229 forms, 12 are not found verbatim.
Nine are the joined segmentations of (6), (12), (13), (17), (21), (22), (23), (26) and (34). The
second paragraph of §3 carries the digit of footnote 1 in the text layer. The paragraph of §4 runs
from page 2 onto page 3, and the text layer sets footnote 1 and the page number inside it. Phyllis's
English passage at the end of §4.2 runs from page 3 onto page 4 the same way. Of 428 marked words in
the text layer, 3 distinct words are not in the form column: natəs.4,5, pipayɛ.”6 and laplɛt.”7,8,
each carrying footnote digits.

The page itself has three errors, and each has a damage row transcribed as printed. Footnote 1 and
the reference list spell Bialystok as Bialystock, and footnote 5 has second-person person plural.
§2 cites Lo & Reisinger 2017, and the reference list has 2019; a notation row records it. The
spelling of §4.1 is Phyllis's own and has no damage rows.

The four versions of the story differ from each other in wording, punctuation and italics, and
the segmentation tiers differ from the orthography in places such as χʷot against x̌ʷit and šɛtegus
against šəʔthigus. Each difference has a notation row, and each side is recorded as printed.
