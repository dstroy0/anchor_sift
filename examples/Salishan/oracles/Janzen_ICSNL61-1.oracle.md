# Janzen_ICSNL61-1.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of The ’Wuikala Stress System by Jonathan Janzen, Independent Researcher, in the
ICSNL 61 proceedings.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

’Wuikala is a North Wakashan language, and this paper is in the corpus because it was given at
ICSNL 61. The paper names no speaker. Its footnote on the title thanks the ‘Wuìkinux̌v Language
Department, the elders and fluent speakers who worked with it, and David Stevenson. The author
checked the stress of each example against the audio on the ’Wuikala First Voices page. (1) is cited
to Howe 2000b and (2) to Stevenson 2025. The words listed without audio in §3.2 are cited to Howe
2000b.

The who for each orthography line, WMA line and cited form is its language, ’Wuikala, or Haisla for
the suffixes of (8) and Kwak̓wala for the title of Janzen 2015. The who for the English of an example
is the work cited on its line, Howe 2000b for (1) line 1 and Stevenson 2025 for (2), and Jonathan
Janzen for every other line. The citations at the right of (8) lines 1 and 4 are for the Haisla
suffixes, as §3.4 introduces them, and those lines carry Jonathan Janzen. The block quotation of §3 carries Lincoln and Rath. The prose, the
tables and the notes on the page carry Jonathan Janzen.

Every example is set in three columns: the local orthography, which marks stress with a grave,
then the Wakashan Meta Alphabet, which marks it with an acute and sets the stressed syllable in bold,
then the English. The kind of the first is transcription and of the second phonemic.

THE LETTERS

The text layer writes glottalization as U+0313 COMBINING COMMA ABOVE on every letter but one: the y
of y̕ak̓ in §3.2 carries U+0315 COMBINING COMMA ABOVE RIGHT, and the page draws that comma over the
a. The uvular fricative is written two ways. ꭓ, U+AB53 LATIN SMALL LETTER CHI, is in the WMA of (2)
and (3) and in §3.2. χ, U+03C7 GREEK SMALL LETTER CHI, is in Table 1, (6), (8) and §3.3. The page
draws the two alike, and the table follows the text layer. The WMA of (5) line 2 has ć, U+0107,
where the orthography has c̓. (8) line 5 marks stress in the orthography with an acute, where every
other orthography line has a grave. The glottal stop of the orthography is ’, U+2019, and ‘,
U+2018, at the start of (2).

In Table 1 the commas of k̓ and k̓ʷ are drawn under the ɡ and ɡʷ of the row above, and between the
rows of (1), (3), (4), (7) and (8) short dashes, dots and commas sit on no letter. Read at 600 dpi.
The text layer holds U+0313 on the letters the table gives and nothing for the stray marks, and the
table follows the text layer. Symbol note rows give each of these.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the reader normalizes the text layer to
NFC before it compares. The text layer holds the proceedings line of page 1 ahead of the title. It
sets a space after most combining commas, as in p̓ ùn̓ akvəla and Kwak̓ wala, and spaces inside some
words, as in Kwakiut l, conduct ed and V oices. The form column joins each word as the page prints
it.

Every form was looked up in the text layer, and every word of the text layer carrying a mark
outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 241 forms, leaving out the 12 notation and symbol note
rows, 88 are not found verbatim. With all white space removed from both sides, 85 of them are found.
The 3 left are the first paragraph of §2, whose First Voices carries the digit of footnote 1 in the
text layer, and two paragraphs that run across a page, at the ends of pages 1 and 6, where the text
layer sets the footnotes and the page number inside them. Of 304 words in the text layer that still
carry a mark outside ASCII once quotes and brackets are stripped from their ends, 1 distinct word is
not in the form column: the bullet •, U+2022, which the table leaves off the rule of §3.

The page has two errors, and each has a damage row transcribed as printed: in the inclusion, for is
the inclusion, in §2, and where to be added, for were, in §3.3. Notation rows record the rest. The
footer of page 1 names the Northwest Linguistics Conference 61 with one editor, where the other
ICSNL 61 papers name ICSNL 61 with nine. §1 cites Kortlandt 1978, and §2 and the reference list
have 1975. Footnote 3 describes ten columns for Table 1, which has nine. The WMA writes e in three
places where the orthography and the prose have ə or u.

Only transcription, phonemic, cited form, cited affix and root rows whose who is a language are the
language. The WMA lines are the author's analysis in a phonemic alphabet. The hyphens, dots and
brackets in them are the analysis and not characters of the language.
