# Barreiro_ICSNL61.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of To the discussion on tense in Hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓.
Elena Barreiro, Simon Fraser University, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

Every numbered example, (1) to (29), is cited by page to Suttles 2004, Musqueam Reference Grammar.
They are the words of the Musqueam people Suttles worked with. This paper names none of them, and
this table does not supply names the page does not print. The two unnumbered examples on page 3 are
Hul’q’umi’num’, cited to Gerdts and Werle 2014 and Gerdts and Schneider 2023, and footnote 5 is the
only place the paper says they are not Hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓. Their speakers are not named here either. The
footnote on the title also thanks Donna B. Gerdts for data given through personal communication, and
no example is marked as coming from her.

THE LATERAL IS ł

The paper writes the lateral fricative as ł, U+0142, 91 times and never as ɬ. That is Suttles's
Musqueam orthography, and the table keeps it. A reader comparing this table with the corpus's other
Halkomelem tables will see two letters for one sound, and that difference is in the papers.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

This PDF was written by macOS Quartz and draws some raised commas away from their letters, some of
them on the line above. Which letter carries a comma is read from the text layer. Whether a comma
exists is read off the page. The page disagrees with the text layer in three places, and the
page wins in each: a raised w in (12) and (17) that the text layer holds as a plain w set
superscript, and the underlined k and x of Sk̲wx̲wú7mesh on page 8 that the text layer holds plain.

Every form was looked up verbatim in the text layer, and every word of the text layer carrying a mark
outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Both lists of misses are named row by row in the table.

Only transcription, segmentation, cited form, cited affix, root and place in Hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ or
Hul’q’umi’num’ are the language. A segmentation holds hyphens and brackets that are the paper's
analysis and not characters of the language. A form in slashes is an abstraction.
