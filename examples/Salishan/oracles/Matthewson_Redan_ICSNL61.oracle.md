Hand extraction of Cw7aoz káti7 láti7 ku naxwít, There was definitely no snake there.
A story in St'át'imcets by K̓weswapáw̓ / Linda Redan, with Lisa Matthewson. ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

Linda was born at K̓maqs (Six Mile) near Lillooet and raised at Cácl̓ep (Fountain). Every relative
spoke St'át'imcets at home and she learned English at day school. She has taught the language
since 1977. She told this story over Zoom on 31 October 2025; it runs three minutes twenty-eight
seconds and Lisa transcribed, translated and glossed it, checking both with Linda.

WHAT IS HERS AND WHAT IS NOT

The bracketed notes are Lisa's, describing Linda's laughter and gestures. In section 2 they are
written in St'át'imcets and in section 3 in English. A reader keyed on the language alone puts
nq̓san̓k and tsúlhcal into the corpus as words Linda said. She did not say them. She laughed.

Example 35 in section 5 is not Linda's at all. It is Sam Mitchell's, published in Van Eijk and
Williams 1981 and quoted here to make a point about pronouns.

Linda moves into English inside her own telling, at ‘I'm like, “Cuy.”’ and again in the coda, and
the paper glosses that as part of the story. Those lines are hers and are kept whole.

THE FORMS ARE AS THE PAPER PRINTS THEM, NOT AS THE EXTRACTION BROKE THEM

tokens and líl̓tem as two more. The name is one word on the page. What is written below is the
page.
