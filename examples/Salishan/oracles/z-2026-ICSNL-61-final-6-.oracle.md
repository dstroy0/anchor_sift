# z-2026-ICSNL-61-final-6-.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of Slit versus Groove Fricative in ʔayʔaǰuθəm Comox With a Note on Long Vowels at
the Phonetic Level by John Hamilton Davis, Bellingham, Washington, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

The ʔayʔaǰuθəm words are from Davis's fieldwork of 1969 to 1980 and later. The paper names who gave
several of them. Noel George Harry gave the words of (9) in February 1970 and the emphatic /bəǰəθ/
and /ǰədis/ of §6, Bill Galligos gave /ʔuwp/ and the “two and a half” syllables of the word for
‘dog’, and Jimi Wilson gave /sa’ma/ in 1970. The gloss of /bəǰəθ/, /ǰədis/, /ʔuwp/ and /sa’ma/
names the speaker, the note row before (9) names Noel George Harry, and the “two and a half” row
names the word for ‘dog’.
Where the paper quotes a person's own words, as Bill Galligos's “two and a half” and Marilyn
Harry's “dragged their vowels”, the who is that person. Where Davis reports what someone said or
did in his own sentence, as Jimi Wilson's observation in §2, the who is Davis.

The who for each form is its language: ʔayʔaǰuθəm, Sechelt, Comox for the forms quoted from Sapir
(1915), Pentlatch for Boas's letters, and English. The Sechelt forms come from Beaumont's Sechelt
Dictionary, as §10 says. The three quoted passages carry their authors, B. Honikman, Edward Sapir
and Wikipedia. Every other row is John Hamilton Davis.

THE PHONETIC LETTERS

Slashes enclose phonemic forms and square brackets phonetic forms, and the table keeps both in the
form. The ts digraph is ʦ, U+02A6. An ejective is marked by a grave accent, U+0300, in the abstract
and in §1 to §9 outside (8), and by a hook above, U+0309, in (8) and §10. The open e is Greek
epsilon, U+03B5, and the uvular fricative is Greek chi, U+03C7. The page prints one turned v; the
text layer has U+028C where it carries an acute and U+1D27 where it does not, and the table keeps
each code point. The page raises the w of a labialized consonant, the theta of tᶿ, the s of dˢ,
and the second vowel of a vowel with stød, and the table writes each with its modifier letter: ʷ,
ᶿ, ˢ, ᵃ and ᵋ.

(4), (5) and (6) derive a long vowel through two intermediate forms, each starred on the page to
mark it unattested. The table puts the star in the gloss column and leaves it out of the form.
The reduplicative prefix is printed C ₁V₁- with a space after the small C, and the table keeps the
space.

Sapir's five forms in §9 are in his own letters. They raise ᵃ, ᵘ, ⁱ and ʸ, and the first long i of
the word for ‘panther’ is a dotless i, U+0131, under its macron and acute, as in the text layer.
Boas's letters in §10 have one notation row each, with the sounds Davis reads them as. The page
underlines the q of q̲u and the k of k̲, and the table writes the underline as U+0332.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the reader normalizes the text layer
to NFC before it compares. The text layer holds every raised letter on the line: kʷ as kw, tᶿ as
tθ, dˢ as ds, aᵃ as aa, εᵋ as εε, and Sapir's oᵘ, iⁱ and gʸ as ou, ii and gy. It writes the double
arrow of (4) to (6) as a capital thorn, Þ, twelve times, and the first letter of the paragraph
after (3) as a Greek capital tau, U+03A4. It splits Vowels in the title as V owels and Vol. in the
Colantoni reference as V ol. It drops the underlines of q̲ and k̲. The table names each of these in
a notation or symbol note row.

Every form was looked up in the text layer, both sides in NFC, and every word of the text layer
carrying a mark outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 208 forms, 42 are not found verbatim.
Forty hold a raised letter that the text layer holds on the line. The other two are the title and
the Colantoni reference, at V owels and V ol. Of 218 marked words in the text layer, 47 distinct
words are not in the form column. Thirty-eight hold a raised letter on the line, six carry the
star of (4) to (6), and the other three are the thorn, the tau of Τhe and the §9 of a
cross-reference in §1.

The page itself has five errors, and each has a damage row transcribed as printed. §1 writes the
language name as ayʔaǰuθəm, without its first glottal stop. §3 has afficate for affricate, and
footnote 1 has slghtly. §9 has Franz Boas write for wrote. The sentence after (2) has no closing
period.
