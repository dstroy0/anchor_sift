# cusinek_et_al_ICSNL61.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of ʔe kʷénc us e tmíxʷ (when the land takes you) by c̓úʔsinek Marty Aspinall.
c̓úʔsinek, Coldwater Reserve, and Reed Steiner, Ella Hannon and Brent Hall, University of British
Columbia, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

The story is c̓úʔsinek's. §1.3 says she recorded, told and translated it, and that every English
translation is in her own words. Steiner and Hannon transcribed it, and Steiner, Hannon and Hall
wrote the segmentation and gloss tiers. The who column follows §1.3: nɬeʔkepmxcín for the
language, c̓úʔsinek for each translation and each footnote that quotes her, and the three authors
for their prose and for the notes on the page. Footnote 17 gives a second English for (27) and does
not say who gave it. c̓úʔsinek asks to be cited by her traditional name, and the table uses it
everywhere the page does. The audio the paper names is not part of this table.

THE LATERAL IS ɬ

The paper writes the lateral fricative as ɬ, U+026C, 352 times, and as ł, U+0142, once, in
kʷałtèzetkʷ in footnote 1. Every other place the paper writes her name it has ɬ. The table keeps
each letter where it falls.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The story is printed three times: running in §2, line by line over its English in §3, and as
seventy-one examples in §4. The three printings differ in a few words, and each difference has a
row of its own.

The text layer writes an accented vowel sometimes as one precomposed code point and sometimes as a
base letter and a combining mark. The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the
reader normalizes the text layer to NFC before it compares. The combining marks that NFC leaves
alone are kept as the text layer holds them: the comma above is U+0313 at 459 sites and U+0315
at 14, in q̕ax̣ném, k̓ʷén̓n̕s, k̓ʷén̓~n̕ and ɬə̣́n̕e wherever they are printed, and the schwa of mə̣̣́m carries U+0323 twice where the page shows one dot. The page and
the text layer disagree in a few more places, and the table names each: three line-end hyphens
held as U+FFFE, a line break after every closing quote of a translation, footnote digits set
against the word before them, segmentation lines broken after ʷ, and the lines of a long
segmentation interleaved with their gloss lines.

Every form was looked up in the text layer, both sides in NFC, and every word of the text layer
carrying a mark outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 443 forms, 109 are not found
verbatim, and each of them falls at a break in the text layer that the table names: one of the
breaks above, or the line break inside t’elh in footnote 15. Of 1625 marked words in the text layer, seven are not in the form column: four carry a
footnote digit, and three are words of the gloss tier, which the gloss column holds.

Only transcription, segmentation, cited form, cited affix and place in nɬeʔkepmxcín or
St’at’imcets are the language. A segmentation holds hyphens, brackets and the null sign ∅, which
are the paper's analysis and not characters of the language.
