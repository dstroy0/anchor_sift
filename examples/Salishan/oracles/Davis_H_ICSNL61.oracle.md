# Davis_H_ICSNL61.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of Evaluative Infinitives in St’át’imcets by Henry Davis, The University of British
Columbia, ICSNL 61.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

WHOSE WORDS THESE ARE

The acknowledgment footnote names Carl Alexander (Qwa7yán’ak) as the principal source for the data,
and footnote 7 says every unattributed example comes from Davis's fieldwork with him. Footnote 7
also says which parts are his: where he supplied a sentence or a translation the paper marks it,
and every other sentence Davis constructed and Carl Alexander judged. The who column follows
footnote 7. St’át’imcets is the who for each form of the language, Carl Alexander (Qwa7yán’ak) for
each translation marked volunteered and each comment of his the paper quotes as CA's, and Henry
Davis for the translations of the sentences he constructed, for his English examples and for his
prose. Footnote 7 prints the name as Qa7yán’ak, and the table records that as damage.

Examples marked (Davis et al. in prep.) come from the dictionary in preparation, and their
translations carry that source as who. (18) and (19) are reported from Matthewson (2005) and carry
Lisa Matthewson. The ʔayʔaǰuθəm examples (94)-(97) come from the three speakers footnote 27 names,
Elsie Paul, Molly Harry and Betty Wilson, and each translation carries its speaker. The passages
Davis quotes from Landau (2013) and Moltmann (2006) carry their authors.

TWO LINES, TWO ORTHOGRAPHIES

Footnote 7 sets out the four-line format of the St’át’imcets examples. The top line is in the van
Eijk orthography used throughout St’át’imc territory, and it is the transcription row. The second
is a morpheme breakdown in the North American Phonetic Alphabet, and it is the segmentation row.
The third line, the gloss tier, is a gloss row. The fourth line is the translation.
Footnote 27 gives the ʔayʔaǰuθəm examples the same four lines, with the community orthography on
the top line. The van Eijk line writes the glottal stop as 7, and a 7 inside a transcription is a
letter. Footnote digits are left out only where the page sets them as footnote markers.

The page underlines the v and l of Qvl in (7) and (48) and of Qvláoka7lhkan in (91), marking the
retracted letters. The text layer has no underline and the table cannot carry one. A notation row
names the three sites. In (24b) the van Eijk line has the NAPA schwa where van Eijk writes e,
and the table keeps it as printed, with a damage row. Four marks have symbol note rows: a dot below
standing alone before x̌aƛ̓ in the abstract, an acute accent set over the c̓ of (7) and over the m of
(16), and two dots below the l of qə̣ḷ̣ in (91).

The judgment marks *, #, ?? and */√ stand before the van Eijk line on the page. The table puts
them in the gloss column and leaves them out of the form.

THE PAGE AND THE TEXT LAYER

The forms are in NFC, as every table in the corpus is, and the reader normalizes the text layer
to NFC before it compares. The page and the text layer disagree in these places, and the table
names each in a notation row. The text layer writes kʷ as kw after the opening bracket of the
infinitive in (18)-(42), (47)-(48), (74) and (77), and <qʷm> as <qwm> in (20a). It writes the
universal quantifier of (54) and (55) as a straight double quote. It holds a space inside the number (55), inside x̌aƛ̓-s-Ø-twítas in (78b), and after
the hyphen of St’át’imcets-English in the references. It renders the long arrows over the scales
of (43) and (79) as runs of hyphens ending in →. Where a segmentation runs to a second line, the
text layer interleaves its lines with the gloss lines, and the table joins each tier. The page's
bold and underlining are not in the text layer. The gloss of (28b) prints 2pl in lowercase among
small capitals, 2PL.ERG in small capitals after it, and the table keeps both as printed. The
residue check puts each of these back from its page render (residue.py CORRECTIONS).

Every form was looked up in the text layer, both sides in NFC, and every word of the text layer
carrying a mark outside ASCII was looked up in the table. Of 661 forms, 82 are not found verbatim.
Seventy-seven are segmentations, and each falls at the kw or <qwm> of the text layer, at an
interleaved line, or at the space in (78b). Four are the ∀x lines of (54) and (55), and one is the
dictionary entry of Davis et al., at the space after St’át’imcets-. Of 992 marked words in the
text layer, nine distinct words are not in the form column. Four carry a footnote digit and one the asterisk of
the title; kwu=waʔ and qʷám<qwm>-ət-s-Ø carry the kw and <qwm> above; and two are the runs of
hyphens that stand for the arrows.

Only transcription, segmentation, cited form, cited affix and root in St’át’imcets, ʔayʔaǰuθəm or
nɬeʔkepmxcín are the language. A segmentation holds hyphens, equals signs, plus signs, tildes,
brackets, braces, the infix brackets < and >, and the null sign Ø, U+00D8, which are the paper's
analysis and not characters of the language.
