# 1983_Hilbert.oracle.tsv

Hand extraction of Poking Fun in Lushootseed.
Vi taqʷšəblu Hilbert, University of Washington. ICSNL 1983.

Read off the paper by a person, not produced by any script. This file is the control: the reader
in corpus_script_extraction is checked against it, and where they disagree the reader is wrong
until someone reads the paper again and says otherwise.

THE FORMS ARE AS PRINTED AND ARE NOT REPAIRED.

This is a 1983 typescript and its scan is damaged. The glottal stop arrives as ?, the schwa as ~
and J and G, labialization as V and v, and the author's own name is set as VI [!.aq liS'"} blu]
lIil bert. A candidate repair table was built and tested the way the Lyon table was: of 103
damaged tokens, 0 are attested in six modern Lushootseed papers before the mapping and 0 after,
because her story vocabulary and their grammar vocabulary do not meet. An untested table applied
to these words would put forms into a corpus that nobody said, so none was applied. The forms
below are the paper's own characters.

WHO IS SPEAKING, WHICH THE OLD FILENAME GOT WRONG

None of the twenty-one examples is Vi Hilbert speaking. Eighteen are her aunt Susie Sampson Peter
of the Upper Skagit and two are Martha LaMont, a Tulalip-Skagit, recorded by Leon Metcalf between
1950 and 1958 and by Thom Hess in 1963, with Hilbert transcribing and translating afterward. The
essay around them is hers. The corpus record used to carry her name in the speaker slot.
