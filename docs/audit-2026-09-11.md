## The injection floor on the nonce bench

**Repaired on 2026-09-11 and reproduced on 2026-09-26.** The nonce bench, `bench_walk.cpp`, had a
negative control, an unrelated nonce, and no positive control. Nothing showed how large a gradient
its readout could detect, and *no gradient was found* could not be told apart from *no gradient
exists*. `theory/theory/cryptography/sha256/REPRODUCE.md:98` states the rule this broke: each null
is measured through the same code as the measurement it bounds.

The repair, `measure_injection_floor()`, adds a known gradient in the readout's own units,
`score(n) = fitness(n) + alpha * set_bits(n)`, and fades it until the correlation test loses it. The
bench left anchor_sift in `bdaed61` on 2026-09-24. The last revision holding it is `bdaed61^`, where
the function sits at `src/engine/c/sha256/bench/bench_walk.cpp:439` and the injected score at
`:479-480`. That revision was built on 2026-09-26 with its own `sha256_core.c`, using MinGW-w64
g++ 13.2.0 at `-O2 -std=c++17` on Windows 11, and run in 36.6 s. Its section 5 prints the audit's
table digit for digit:

| alpha | flip correlation | flip z | control z | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.125 | 0.054942 | 24.57 | 0.09 | seen |
| 0.0625 | 0.013947 | 6.24 | 0.15 | seen, the floor |
| 0.03125 | 0.003023 | 1.35 | 0.18 | lost |
| 0 | -0.000777 | -0.35 | 0.21 | lost, the bench as it runs |

The control stays inside one standard error at every rung. The bench therefore excludes a gradient
of 0.0625 leading-zero bits per single-bit step or stronger. Below that, and in any part of the
digest the leading-zero count does not read, it measured nothing. Mining's target test reads the
same leading-zero count, and a gradient this readout cannot see does not lower the cost of a block.

The audit counted eight references to injection or calibration in `bench_sac.cu`. The copy at
`bdaed61^` has three lines matching either word.

## Dependence decay against lag

The audit tested whether generated text decays toward independence faster than human text, using
mutual information between tokens at distance `k` in `maint/prose/dependence_decay.py`. The result
is recorded in that file's docstring, at `maint/prose/dependence_decay.py:89-119`:

| corpus | slope | sd | chunks of 200,000 tokens | top-256 mass share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| human | -0.4595 | 0.1286 | 36 | 0.5279 |
| assistant | -0.4834 | 0.1761 | 6 | 0.6122 |

The gap is -0.0239 against a standard error of 0.0750, and 17 of 36 human chunks decay at least as
fast as the assistant mean. The gap fell from 0.104 at 39,516 assistant words to 0.024 at 200,000.
The assistant spread is the wider of the two, against the hypothesis that generated text is closer
to memoryless. Slope against mass share across all 42 chunks gives r = +0.269. The decay shape does
not separate the two corpora at this size.

**Not checked on 2026-09-26.** The tool reads `build/papers` (`maint/prose/dependence_decay.py:199`),
absent from this checkout, and the figures stand as the docstring records them. The audit gave the
assistant corpus as 1,345,433 words, and the docstring records 1,313,144 words added to it
(`maint/prose/dependence_decay.py:91`). The two counts have not been reconciled.

## The human tail and corpus assembly

The first run showed an excess over the shuffle at every lag out to 1024 in the human corpus, read at
the time as long-range structure in human writing. The docstring retracts it at
`maint/prose/dependence_decay.py:121-144`. Scrambling the tokens inside every window of 100 words
destroys all arrangement shorter than 100 words and leaves composition above that scale alone. The
excess stayed almost unchanged, 0.040025 against 0.035006 at lag 256 and 0.030351 against 0.023921
at lag 1024. The loader concatenates about 120 papers, and word rates shift at each join. That drift
raises mutual information at every lag.

The docstring names the nuisance at `:151-160`. How many documents a corpus was assembled from sets
its large-lag excess, and cutting two corpora to the same token count does not control it. The
two-band null stands, because it was computed per chunk and never rested on the tail (`:146-149`).
The docstring also records that the whole assistant corpus was written under this tree's ban list
(`:115-119`), which leaves a null on it ambiguous about unrestricted prose.

above and on these unchecked ones. One route stays untested: stop-listing the words that get scored
while measuring gaps in the original word positions, which keeps the sequence intact and excludes
subject vocabulary. Nothing in the tree records a run of it.
