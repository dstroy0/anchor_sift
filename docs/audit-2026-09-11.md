## 2. Fixed during the audit

### 2.1 The nonce-landscape claim had no positive control

`bench_walk.cpp` carried the claim that the nonce landscape is flat: distance-to-target
autocorrelation inside the null band at every lag 1 to 1024 over 200,000 consecutive nonces, and
hill-climbing losing to random draw by 3.81 standard errors at equal budget. The claim was stated as
retiring every ordering strategy at once.

The bench has a negative control, the unrelated-nonce arm at mode 2, and the head-to-head at equal
budget is built correctly. It had no positive control. `bench_sac.cu` carries eight references to
injection and calibration; `bench_walk.cpp` carried none. By the rule stated in
`theory/cryptography/sha256/REPRODUCE.md`, that every null in this work is measured through the same
code as the measurement it bounds and none is computed from theory, the claim was unbounded: *no
gradient was found* was standing in for *no gradient exists* with nothing separating them.

Both arms read the digest through `fitness()`, the leading-zero count, so the gap sat under the whole
result and not under one section of it.

**The auditor's reasoning about why was wrong and the conclusion was right.** The first argument
offered was that quantisation to about two bits per evaluation bounds the sensitivity. It does not: a
correlation averaged over 200,000 samples resolves to about 0.002 however coarse each draw is, since
the standard error falls as one over root N regardless. The correct objection is projection and not
resolution. The leading-zero count is a lossy function of the digest, so structure not reaching that
count is invisible at any sample size. That is the second measurement rule this tree had already
written down, applied to an instrument that predates it.

**Fixed and measured, not argued.** `measure_injection_floor()` adds a known gradient in the readout's
own units, `score(n) = fitness(n) + alpha * set_bits(n)`, and fades it:

| alpha | flip correlation | flip z | control z | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1250 | 0.054942 | 24.57 | 0.09 | seen |
| 0.0625 | 0.013947 | 6.24 | 0.15 | seen, the floor |
| 0.03125 | 0.003023 | 1.35 | 0.18 | lost |
| 0 | -0.000777 | -0.35 | 0.21 | lost, the real bench |

The control is flat at every rung, which makes the bar drawn instead of derived. The earned claim is
now bounded: no gradient of 0.0625 leading-zero bits per single-bit step or stronger, silent below
that, and silent about anything expressed elsewhere in the digest. The engineering conclusion is
untouched, because mining's objective is that same count. A landscape invisible to this readout
therefore cannot lower the cost of a block.

**The reason this could be built while the work is held, and why holding it is not a reason to

manifest later is what converts *these bytes existed* into *these bytes, and here they are*.

publication to anchor is the expensive mistake, because every unanchored day is a day a competing

## 3.7 The register difference is compositional, and it is not long-range dependence

Two instruments disagreed about this tree's prose and the disagreement is now resolved into a
narrowing instead of left open.

`claudese_distance.py` places 46 of 48 files on the assistant side of a human band drawn from 366,833
words. The in-house ban-list gate reports 0.0 banned tokens per 100k on the same files. Both readings
are correct and they are about different things: the ban list counts eight named phrases, and the
detector measures composition against two poles. Eight phrases cannot carry a register, so the gate
has the smaller rank and passing it has been standing in for reading human.

The detector states at the foot of its output that every FILE-LEVEL distance is taken on a bag of
words. This audit read that as arrangement being unmeasured and said so, to five sessions, and that
was an overreach corrected by a second reading of the tool. `web_profile(words, run=2)` is a bigram
web, documented in its own docstring as "the word web, not the bag of words", with the permutation
null already wired to it. Arrangement was already measured at pole level and the run already
reported that both poles sit further from each other than from their own shuffles, so neither is
resolved, and "Nothing was read."

The per-file claim was correct and the generalisation from it was not. Arrangement was not a missing
half; it was a measured null nobody had carried forward.

The hypothesis tested below was worth testing anyway, because it would have said what the difference
IS: generated text is sampled from a context window and should therefore be closer to memoryless,
while human writing carries a thread across paragraphs. If so, the decay of dependence against lag
separates the two.

**It does not.** `tools/prose/dependence_decay.py`, mutual information between tokens at distance
`k`, both poles cut into disjoint chunks of 200,000 words, a slope and a spread measured for each:

| pole | slope | sd | chunks | top-256 mass share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| human | -0.4595 | 0.1286 | 36 | 0.5279 |
| assistant | -0.4834 | 0.1761 | 6 | 0.6122 |

The gap is -0.0239 against a standard error of 0.0750, which is 0.32 standard deviations, and 17 of
36 human chunks decay at least as fast as the assistant mean.

Three things make this a stronger null than a bare p-value.

**The gap shrank as the corpus grew.** It read 0.104 at 39,516 words and 0.024 at 200,000. A real
effect tightens around a stable value as the sample rises; noise walks toward zero. Thirty-four times
the corpus moved the estimate four times closer to nothing.

**The spread is the wrong way round.** The hypothesis says generated text is closer to memoryless, so
its decay should be tighter and steeper. It is neither: at 0.1761 against 0.1286 it is wider. That is
evidence against the proposed mechanism and not a failure to detect it. It is also a question the
earlier shape could not ask at all, because the assistant side was one number with no error bar
compared against a human band.

**The nuisance was measured instead of assumed.** Slope against top-256 mass share, all 42 chunks
pooled, gives `r = +0.269`. Weak, so the difference in share between the poles is not what moves the
slope and matching on it would change nothing. Checking that separately from the level mattered,
because at the level stage the same nuisance turned out to be real: the two poles' shuffle floors sat
29 per cent apart, and an excess over them compared two estimators instead of two corpora.

**A trap avoided, recorded because it would have produced the largest apparent result of the run.**
The human corpus is fixed at 4,655,941 words, so the count of disjoint chunks available falls as
`1/L` while the band narrows as about `1/sqrt(L)`. At the full 1,345,433 words there are three
disjoint human chunks and a band nobody can measure, and the separation would have looked very large.
The chunk length was chosen against that arithmetic and not against the corpus size.

**A second session ran the same design independently and reached a sharper negative.** Its result is
not that the assistant pole lacks a tail the human pole has. It is that **neither pole has one**.
Over a function-word alphabet, at lag 64 and beyond, every window from 1,000 to 32,000 words sits
inside its own shuffle floor: assistant transcript -0.0009 to 0.0013, an Austen novel -0.0004 to
0.0034, 25 gated human papers 0.0011 to 0.0023. Both human poles are as memoryless as the transcript
past about lag 4.

That refutes the premise this audit supplied, on that alphabet. "Human prose carries dependence
surviving to large k" is false over function words.

**The tail is real, and it is corpus assembly and not language.** A first reading of the two runs
said they located the effect: fold content words into one bin and the tail disappears, keep them and
it is there. That was correct as an observation and wrong about the cause. Re-run on the top-256
alphabet at 100,000 tokens with twelve shuffle draws per arm and per-arm nulls, corrected values with
each arm's own floor beside it:

| arm | lag 256 | lag 1024 |
|---|---|---|
| human papers, 120 documents | 0.0102 [0.0027] | 0.0055 [0.0028] |
| Austen alone, one work | 0.0015 [0.0020] | 0.0011 [0.0025] |

The papers carry the tail. The continuous novel does not, sitting inside its own floor at both lags,
though it does carry real structure at lags 16 and 64 that a coarser alphabet had folded away.

**It survives scrambling, which settles what it is.** Scrambling the papers inside every 100-word
window destroys all arrangement below 100 words and keeps only composition drift above it:

| papers | lag 64 | lag 256 | lag 1024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| as they sit | 0.0130 | 0.0072 | 0.0053 |
| scrambled within 100 | 0.0112 | 0.0093 | 0.0071 |

The tail is as large or larger after scrambling. Nothing surviving that is dependence at a distance.

**And the arm whose answer was fixed before it ran.** Austen, the King James Bible and Shakespeare
cut into pieces and interleaved: three continuous human works, three hands, no generated text
anywhere in it.

| piece length | lag 256 | lag 1024 |
|---|---|---|
| 250 | 0.0054 | 0.0081 |
| 1,000 | 0.0080 | 0.0091 |
| 4,000 | 0.0147 | 0.0069 |
| 16,000 | 0.0159 | 0.0135 |

Austen alone has no tail. Austen mixed with two other human works has a larger one than the papers
do, and it grows with the piece length. The assembly made it, and no other feature of that arm could have.

**The premise is therefore inverted, not merely unsupported.** The continuous human work, the one
that actually holds a subject across paragraphs and returns to it, is the arm with no tail past lag
64. The tail belongs to corpora built by concatenation. Large-lag excess tracks how a corpus was
assembled and is silent about who wrote it.

**What this does to the two-band run.** Cutting both poles to exactly 200,000 tokens controls chunk
size. It does not control how many source documents went into each side, and document count is what
the large-lag end measures. A 4.66M-word human pole of many papers and a 1.35M-word assistant pole of
many transcripts will each carry a tail set by its own heterogeneity, and a smooth decay over its own
shuffle at every lag is the drift signature instead of evidence against it. The settling test was one line, it was run by the session whose claim it was, on its own corpus in
its own code, and the tail survived:

| lag | as it sits | scrambled within 100 |
|---|---|---|
| 256 | 0.040025 | 0.035006 |
| 384 | 0.036914 | 0.031646 |
| 512 | 0.035449 | 0.030720 |
| 768 | 0.033085 | 0.027101 |
| 1024 | 0.030351 | 0.023921 |

Destroying all arrangement below 100 words barely moves the large-lag excess. The corpus loader
concatenates about 120 documents with a join, so composition drifts across the joins, and drift
raises mutual information at every lag because knowing where you are in the corpus tells you which
document you are in. The confound was built into the loader and its output was then reported as a
property of human writing.

**What falls and what does not.** The human tail falls, and with it the support it was lending to
content-word recurrence. The two-band null stands: the decay shape does not distinguish the poles at
200,000 tokens and the assistant spread is 0.1761 against the human 0.1286, both computed per chunk
and neither resting on the tail.

**One caveat that session raised against its own surviving result**, recorded because nobody else
would have found it. Assembly is uncontrolled in the two-band run: 4.66M words of many papers against
1.35M words of many transcripts, both sides carrying an assembly signature, and the per-chunk slopes
include it. The null survives and is conservative, since assembly noise in both arms makes a real
difference harder to see. But the slopes themselves are partly assembly and should not be quoted as a
property of either register.

It also found two traps worth carrying, **neither of which bites the two-band result**, checked and
not assumed. *The knee is the sample size, not the author*: the same texts read knees of 6 and 32 to
48 at 38,707-word blocks and 4, 5, 6 at 12,047, so two differently-sized corpora separate on it for
free. The two-band run cut both poles to exactly 200,000 tokens, so the two sides differ in chunk
count, 36 against 6, and not in chunk size; that moves the standard error and not the bias. *One
shared null is not enough*: an early arrangement measured bias once on a transcript shuffle and
subtracted it from every arm, producing a plateau out to lag 1024 in an arm block-shuffled at span 16
that cannot carry dependence past 16 words. A plateau in an arm built to have none is the bias of the
wrong text. The two-band run draws its shuffle from each chunk's own symbols, per chunk and per lag,
so per-arm nulls are what is there.

A third trap joins them, exposed by the interleaving arm. **Matching token counts
between poles does not control assembly**, and assembly is what the large-lag end reads. Two corpora
cut to identical chunk sizes still differ in how many separate documents each chunk was built from,
and that difference alone produces a smooth decaying excess over the shuffle at every lag.

**What it settles.** The register difference is real and it lives in which words get chosen. No
arrangement reading separates the poles: not the bigram web, which returned unresolved, and not the
decay of top-256 mutual information at 200,000 tokens, where the assistant spread is the wider of the
two.

The large-lag excess that looked like the human signature is a property of how a corpus was
assembled. Three continuous human works interleaved produce more of it than 120 papers do, and one
continuous human work produces none. Anything reading that excess as authorship is reading document
boundaries.

**The repair was proposed, measured, and fails in the same place.** Folding content words into one
bin does destroy what "a person holds a subject and returns to it" refers to, so gap clustering on a
content alphabet looked like the version of the design with something to find.
`anchor_sift/examples/language/4_measure/word_burstiness.py` was swept against it. It has something
to find and the something is genre.

At a matched 32,000-word window on content words: papers 0.573, transcript 0.773, novel 0.821. **The
two human poles sit further from each other than the novel sits from the assistant.** Function words
at the same window give papers 0.758, transcript 0.897, novel 0.861, the same story. Linguistics
papers gather hard because they repeat language names and forms in dense sections, which is a
property of the subject and not of the writer.

It also carries the window drift that killed the mutual-information form. Word set held fixed so only
the window varies, function words, windows 4k to 32k:

| pole | 4k | 8k | 16k | 32k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| transcript | 1.022 | 0.968 | 0.952 | 0.935 |
| novel | 0.977 | 0.931 | 0.910 | 0.907 |
| papers | 0.908 | 0.865 | 0.844 | 0.825 |

Monotone down every column, about 0.09 per eightfold window. Smaller than the mutual-information
drift and systematic in the same direction, and a ratio against the word's own null does not escape
it.

**Where this audit's framing was wrong.** An earlier version called the design mis-specified instead of
than refuted: the right statistic on the wrong alphabet. That holds for the missing tail. It does not
hold for the window, and the window is the half written down here. A shuffle removes finite-sample
bias and destroys composition drift along with it, so the corrected value is local dependence plus
drift, and drift is a function of window length. That is alphabet-independent and it gets *worse* on
content words, because content-word rates are exactly what drift when a text changes subject. Moving
to the alphabet the claim needs strengthens the confound that killed the statistic. Mis-specified on
the alphabet; genuinely refuted on the window.

**The asymmetry that explains all five results, and it is the finding.** Five arrangement readings
have now been run against this question: equality autocorrelation in `shift_agreement.py`, the bigram
web in `claudese_distance.py`, mutual information against lag, gap clustering, and the large-lag tail.
Every one reads nothing, orders by genre, or reads corpus assembly. The compositional instrument
separates the poles at 46 of 48 files.

Assembly belongs on that list instead of beside it. Which documents a corpus was built from is a
subject-level property, so the tail is the same failure as the genre ordering wearing a different
statistic.

The reason is not resolution. `claudese_distance` can stop-list subject and medium vocabulary, and a
bag of words survives having tokens deleted from it. **An arrangement statistic does not, because
deleting a token changes every gap and every lag downstream of it.** Stop-listing is therefore
available to a compositional reading and unavailable to a sequence reading, so the first
controls for subject and none of the four sequence readings do.

Stated one notch weaker than an earlier version of this section had it. That version said a sequence
measure cannot be made subject-blind without destroying the sequence it measures, and the untested
route below refutes exactly that: scoring only non-subject words while taking gaps in original
position space leaves the sequence intact and filters only the scored set. The barrier is that the
obvious move, delete and re-index, destroys the object. So subject-blindness has to be applied to the
**scoring** and not to the sequence, and no instrument run so far does that. The strong form would
have foreclosed the one route still open.

**One route remains untested.** Apply the stop list to which words get *scored* while taking gaps in
original position space. That keeps the sequence intact and still excludes subject vocabulary. The
papers' clustering is driven by language names that already sit in `claudese_distance`'s subject
list, so it could close most of the 0.573 to 0.773 gap or show the gap is topic-independent. Open,
and marked untested instead of attempted.

It is a fifth instrument after four nulls and the honest prior on it is low. What earns it a place in
the queue is that its negative is informative: if stop-listing the scored set closes the gap, the
papers' clustering was language names all along, and that retires the whole family instead of one
member of it.

**A prior result this audit cited, wrongly, against that file's own warning.** An earlier version of this
section offered `anchor_sift/src/engine/python/measure/shift_agreement.py` as evidence that the null
already existed, on the grounds that its header records English prose reading nothing above its own

Its header, lines 24 to 33, verified on disk:

> It is not the permutation null measure and the two are not interchangeable as evidence. Three
> separate claims in this work merged them... Each was corrected after the fact, and the pattern
> recurred three times without being noticed once.

> It tests one hypothesis, that the sequence agrees with itself at a fixed offset, so periodicity is
> the only thing it can report. English prose and C source read nothing above their own shuffles,
> and English peaks below its shuffle. **Both have structure. Neither has agreement with itself at a
> fixed offset.**

Periodicity is equality at a fixed offset. Mutual information is dependence at a distance. Prose
returning to a subject changes the word, so it carries dependence at lag `k` with no elevated
equality at lag `k`, ordinary for prose and not the exception. The file's own last sentence
says the structure is there and that this instrument cannot see it.

This audit called it "the equality form", saw the distinction, and used it as the same measurement
anyway. Recorded here instead of deleted, because it would have been the fourth time this tree merged
those two measures, and the first three were each corrected after the fact without the pattern being
noticed.

**What is left of the prior-work point.** `claudese_distance.py` alone carries it: the bigram web
and its null existed and had already returned unresolved before anything was built. Searching for a
prior result before building an instrument remains the cheapest step available, and nobody took it.

Both cited files live in `anchor_sift` and not in this tree.

**What it does not settle.** Long-range dependence in natural language is real and the decay law is a
sound discriminator on the right alphabet; this test as specified has no power against it. The
whole 1,345,433-word assistant corpus was written inside trees where the prose gate runs, so it is
suppressed prose and a null on it is ambiguous by construction. The wider-spread result is the
exception: whatever a ban list does to word choice, it is not plausibly what makes a decay shape more
variable than human writing, so that reading does not lean on the suppression.

## 5. The partition

Douglas set the rule on 2026-09-11: all continuing crypto work is not for the general public. The
default is therefore held, and public is the exception that has to be argued for.

`theory/PARTITION.tsv` classifies 47 artifacts. `tools/book/check_partition.py` enforces it by
walking each book's include graph and refusing any book that reaches material stricter than the book
itself. It fails closed in three directions. An unlisted artifact is held, keeping a new document
private until classified. PENDING is held, so an undecided judgment call cannot reach a public build
by nobody getting to the decision. And a missing manifest refuses the run instead of passing it.

**The hazard it was built for is live.** `theory/cryptography/sha256/main.tex` includes all eleven
chapters, six of them published and five of them not. Nothing in `build_theory.sh` refuses that
combination, and a build therefore produces one PDF carrying both halves and looking finished.
Declaring that book public in the manifest makes the checker name all twelve held artifacts and exit
nonzero, which tests that the gate works and not merely that it runs.

Four documents are Douglas's own judgment calls and five more were nominated by this audit on the
same grounds: the method is general and every worked example is SHA-256, so publishing them as
written leaks the subject even where the method is the point. None was classified either way. The
four have been transcribed into one book, `theory/held/instruments/`, so that a ruling moves one row
in the manifest instead of requiring surgery on a book that mixes the two.
