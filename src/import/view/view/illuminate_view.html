Illuminating With The Chain

256 beams read a 256 bit object by occlusion. The light source chooses where they point, a source with no entropy cannot resolve the object. Four sources, graded on whether the object comes back from its shadows alone.

Rank is how many of the 256 directions the beam set spans. Anything short of full rank leaves part of the object in the dark whatever the shadows say. Lit: chain, csprng, golden.

The degenerate source is the control that makes the rest mean anything: every beam pointed the same way reaches rank 2 and loses 139 bits. Parallel beams all read one line through the object, so their shadows cannot separate sources that differ off that line. If it had recovered the object anyway, direction would never have mattered and the entropy would be decoration.

Collision entropy deficit in bits, which is what a departure from flat is actually worth. The floor is what chance alone manufactures at this sample count, so it is derived rather than chosen. Raw ids miss by up to 4.541e+00 bits; the tail misses by 6.117e-03, which is the floor.

Why the raw stream fails

9 zero bytes
213 ids, 53%
10 zero bytes
187 ids, 47%

Leading zero bytes per block id. Every id begins with nine or ten of them, and that is not a flaw in the chain: it is the chain working. A block id is small because somebody spent 5.47e+23 hashes making it small, so the high third of every id is the receipt and carries no entropy at all. Read raw, the beams it aims reach rank 175 and lose 23 bits. Read from the tail, they reach full rank and lose none.

So the most expensive random numbers in existence are structurally biased, and the bias is precisely the thing that made them expensive.
