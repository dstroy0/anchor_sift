\chapter{Sources, and how much of each was read}

\textbf{Purpose:} Name every source this book rests on, with the registry key it resolves to and
how much of it was actually read, so that no claim here can be traced to a document nobody opened.
\textbf{Scope:} \texttt{SOURCES.\allowbreak{}tsv} in the closed citations repository, twelve rows
entered for this book; \texttt{sources/\allowbreak{}mathematics/\allowbreak{}} beneath it, ten
files held.

Twelve rows. Ten files. Two rows have no file behind them, and one file has no text in it.

Each row carries the key as the public tree spells it. A surname in a sentence buys a reader
nothing. An author, a title, an identifier they can follow: that buys them the source. Fields absent
from a document stay empty in the registry, with a note naming which. Blank misleads nobody. A
guess misleads everybody.

Read-state belongs in the entry. Five of these were opened to page one and shut. Two were read in
part, and the part is named. One cannot be read. Strip that out and a bibliography degrades into a
list of things that exist.

\section{Read in full}

\textbf{Fefferman}, Charles L. Fefferman, \emph{Existence and Smoothness of the Navier--Stokes
Equation}, Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize Problem statement.

Six pages, errata included, cover to cover. Nothing else in the corpus got that treatment. The
whole Navier--Stokes chapter turns on this one document. That earned it the reading. No year appears
anywhere on it, and the paper examined in that chapter cites it without one too, so the year field
stays empty and the row says why. Digest for digest, its bytes match what claymath.org served on
2026-09-11.

\section{Read in part, with the part named}

\textbf{OpenAI 2026 Navier-Stokes}, \emph{Finite time blowup for Navier--Stokes}. 166 pages.
Abstract, introduction, Theorem 1.1, the bridging argument in section 10.4, Corollary 10.6. Nobody
counted how many pages that came to. Sections 3 through 9 and all three appendices carry the
mathematics and went unread.

\textbf{OpenAI 2026 Euler}, \emph{Finite time blowup for the Euler equation}. 57 pages. Abstract,
introduction, Theorem 1.1. Nothing beyond that. The amplification mechanism sits in the unread
remainder, and the limit test in the previous chapter wants precisely that mechanism.

\textbf{Formal Conjectures}, the Lean formalization of the Navier--Stokes problem statement
published by the Formal Conjectures project. Read as source text, in the adapted copy the OpenAI
repository carries, at a commit that file names. Four files out of 2{,}669. None of them built.

Everything this book finds about the Navier--Stokes claim rests on that one file. No copy sits under
\texttt{sources/}: a repository has no single file to hold, so the identifier is a path and the
commit lives in the adapting file's header.

\section{Read at the first page only}

Five official Clay problem descriptions, for the five problems the index still lists as unsolved.
Each was opened, identified off its own first page instead of its filename, and shut. All five match
the bytes claymath.org served on 2026-09-11. Two sit under a different upload month than the rest,
checked by fetching them instead of assumed from the pattern.

\textbf{Cook}, Stephen Cook, \emph{The P versus NP Problem}, 12 pages.

\textbf{Deligne}, Pierre Deligne, \emph{The Hodge Conjecture}, 5 pages.

\textbf{Bombieri}, E. Bombieri, \emph{Problems of the Millennium: the Riemann Hypothesis}, 11
pages. Older tooling than the other four.

\textbf{Jaffe and Witten}, Arthur Jaffe and Edward Witten, \emph{Quantum Yang--Mills Theory}, 14
pages.

\textbf{Wiles}, Andrew Wiles, \emph{The Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture}, 5 pages.

One page of Wiles. Wiles is also the problem the previous chapter recommends pointing the apparatus
at, so the shortfall bites hardest here. That recommendation stands on an exact numerical identity,
and the identity came from the project architect. It did not come off this document. Whoever acts on
the recommendation opens this first.

\textbf{Wilkins 1998}, Bernhard Riemann translated by David R. Wilkins, \emph{On the Number of
Prime Numbers less than a Given Quantity}, 10 pages. The key's year is the translation's, printed
there as a preliminary version of December 1998. November 1859 appears on that same page for the
memoir underneath, which gets its own row. English, legible, and the practical way into the 1859
content.

\section{No text to read}

\textbf{Riemann 1859}, Bernhard Riemann, handwritten working manuscript, \emph{Nachlass} Cod. Ms.
Riemann 3, \emph{Rechenseiten}.

Four pages. Zero extractable characters, counted page by page. All four were rendered and read as
pictures, the only way they can be read. The year on the row is the manuscript's; the scan happened
in 2015.

Its filename says published memoir. What it holds is a draft. That gap is why the entry reads the
way it does, and the corpus chapter spells out what seeding a row off the filename would have done
to a future reader.

\section{Not a document}

\textbf{Clay Mathematics Institute}. Two facts in this book describe what that website said on
2026-09-11, and a fact needs somewhere to point.

The Millennium Problems index listed five unsolved, one solved, and Navier--Stokes in neither
bucket. The Navier--Stokes page itself carried the label \emph{Active}. Those two do not agree.
Both go on the record here as statements about how a website sorted its own contents on one day at
one address. Neither is used anywhere in this book as a statement about a problem's status.

\section{Named in these sources and not read here}

Reference lists carry names, and this book repeats a handful at second hand. Leray on global weak
solutions. Caffarelli, Kohn and Nirenberg on partial regularity. The Beale--Kato--Majda continuation
criterion. Buckmaster and Vicol on non-uniqueness, then Albritton, Bru\'e and Colombo on the same.
Elgindi on axisymmetric blowup. Dokchitser's algorithm for $L$-function values.

Not one was read. Not one is registered. None is cited here as established.

Each gets the status of a thing a read source mentions, which is all it has earned. Writing rows for
them would assert a reading nobody here has done. Somebody should do that reading. When they do, the
rows are theirs to fill.
